Did you know that the Mormon Church was making money off of whorehouses in the early 1900s?
So in the early 1900s, the Mormon Church was struggling financially.
It was for this reason that the church allowed these houses of ill-repute to continue.
The Mormon Church owned buildings on what is now Regent Street that they were knowingly leasing out as brothels.
According to historian D. Michael Quinn, this remained an uncomfortable secret until 1908,
when the Salt Lake Tribune proclaimed that the Brigham Young Trust Company filled these houses on Commercial Street, aka Modern-day Regent Street.
This article noted that the church-owned Clayton Investment Company continued to lease these houses of prostitution,
and that the LDS Church's annual rent was $2,400 from just one of these brothel leases.
Although the LDS Church was technically and legally only the leaser of the buildings,
some general authorities regarded the church's leadership as morally complicit in brothel management.
Internal records even show leadership eventually ordering these properties to be cleaned up or closed,
emphasizing that morality, not money, should be that priority.
But the shift didn't happen immediately. In fact, the Mormon Church did not divest its houses of prostitution until 1941.
So the Mormon Church continued to profit from these brothels under Wilford Woodruff,
Lorenzo Snow, Joseph F. Smith, and most of the presidency of Heber J. Grant.
Interestingly, the Mormon Church climbed out of debt on April 5th of 1907.
So why did they keep profiting off of brothels for 37 years after they no longer needed the money?
Why did they allow brothels at all? Could it be that the church really cares about money?
