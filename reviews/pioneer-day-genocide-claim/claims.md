### Claim 3
- **Anchor context**: whole clip (0:00-0:55) read in full.
- **Research**: No order signed by Young against Native Americans was found. The "extermination order" is Missouri's 1838 Executive Order 44 (Boggs), against the Mormons. In 1850 Young directed Daniel Wells to have the Nauvoo Legion subdue the Timpanogos Utes at Fort Utah; Wells's orders say to exterminate "if necessary" those who would not make peace (Coates 1978; Wells's original order not opened). The Church's own Saints vol. 2 (p. 206) says Young ordered the campaign and the militia "surrounded and killed the remaining Ute men" (red-team brief; page not independently re-read here). Turner (2012): Young "was not an 'exterminator'" and also "presided over the military and demographic conquest and diminution of Utah's Indians". Bear River (1863) was a U.S. Army massacre under Connor with Mormon guides. No scholar's genocide position was read directly (Blackhawk, Farmer, Bagley, Ostler, Christy, Madsen unverified).
- **Grading**: disputed (the "signed order" is misleading; exterminate-language orders, killings and dispossession are conceded).
- **Doctrine tier**: n/a (history). Sensitive topic (Indigenous history).
- **Adversarial Q1**: Arguing over "genocide" and "signed" concedes what matters: Young said "I say go & kill them" (31 Jan 1850), men who had surrendered were killed, the Timpanogos Nation says the order was never rescinded, and the Church has not apologized. The script therefore leads with the 1850 orders and does not defend the label's rejection.
- **Adversarial Q2**: The reel omits that Young also advised "feed them, don't fight them" (1850s) and that the Church History Department acknowledges conflict; the script does not lean on either as a rebuttal.
- **Turnabout**: California (Burnett's 1851 "war of extermination" message; Madley's 150,000 to 30,000) would make "divinely chosen theology" a non-explanation, but it risks whataboutism on a sensitive topic. NOT used.

### Claim 4
- **Anchor context**: whole clip read in full.
- **Research**: The 90% figure is sourced for the Southern Paiute only (NPS; Tom & Holt 2000, "under 25 years"). No source opened gives 90% for all Native peoples in Utah; a statewide 86% over about 50 years appears only as a lead (Reséndez p. 273, not opened). Population at settlement: 12,000-35,000 (Walker 1989). No source gives a percentage by cause. Hunger and loss of land and water made disease deadlier (Compton 2013); documented battle deaths run in the hundreds (Black Hawk War: about 70 whites and twice as many Native Americans per Reeve/Turner); Bear River about 235-300 dead. Disease share is an inference, not a measurement.
- **Grading**: disputed (causes: fact; 90% for the whole population and the "mostly" weighting: unmeasured).
- **Doctrine tier**: n/a. Sensitive topic.
- **Adversarial Q1**: Fixing the numbers to about 86% over 50 years, mostly starvation and disease, does not answer the point that every cause ran through settlement. The script says so ("starvation caused by settlement") and does not argue the harm is smaller.
- **Adversarial Q2**: The reel omits the baseline problem and that violence is documented in the hundreds; the script states both.
- **Turnabout**: none used on this claim.
