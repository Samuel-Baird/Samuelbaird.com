### Claim 1
**Unverified**: LDS Charities no-proselytizing policy wording (newsroom page blocked at fetch), current Africa membership figures, any policy on 'mission trips' by non-missionaries.
- **Anchor context**: whole source read in full (`transcripts/source.srt`); no surrounding text changes the meaning.
- **Research**: Two separate tracks. (a) Full-time proselyting missionaries (18-24 months) do try to convert and do teach the Restoration/Joseph Smith (Preach My Gospel 2023, Lesson 1). (b) LDS Charities humanitarian work gives aid 'regardless of race, beliefs or nationality'; service missionaries are told not to proselyte while serving. The Church has no program called 'mission trips'; the asker blurs the three. History: Nigerians/Ghanaians sought the Church from 1946 on, and the Church held back until Official Declaration 2 (1978-06-08).
- **Grading**: fact for the two-track structure and stated policy; whether volunteers always follow it is unmeasured (speculation). Doctrine-scholar: complete. Historian: partial.
- **Doctrine tier**: Missionary duty = tier 1 (D&C 4, 88:81) + tier 3 (Preach My Gospel 2023). Aid not linked to proselyting = tier 2 (newsroom 2012) + tier 4 (Oaks 2018/2022, Nelson 2019). 1978 revelation = tier 1.
- **Adversarial Q1** — The vocabulary answer is right, but the honest headline is still that the Church is recruiting in Africa (16 new Africa missions announced Oct 2025); the aid arm shares a name/logo and sometimes people with the missions; and the pre-1978 priesthood restriction goes unaddressed.
- **Adversarial Q2** — Nothing material omitted; the asker is a curious non-member. The clip is a question, so open with the direct answer ('yes for proselyting missions') rather than a standalone concession. **No jab recommended** (red-team found none).

### Claim 2
**Unverified**: D&C 133 / Articles of Faith 10 wording (A of F 10 'Zion will be built upon the American continent' is likely the source of the enclave idea), full 1999 letter, Kenya stake count (page blank; one snippet says four), independent non-Church account of Church land in Kenya.
- **Anchor context**: whole source read in full (`transcripts/source.srt`); no surrounding text changes the meaning.
- **Research**: Old policy was physical gathering (Perpetual Emigrating Fund; Mexico colonies 1885, Alberta). Reversed: McConkie 1972/77, Lee 1973 ('every nation is the gathering place for its own people'), Kimball 1978, First Presidency letter 1999, Nelson 2006 ('the gathering takes place in each nation'), Cook 2023. Kenya today: 24,547 members, 4 stakes, 102 congregations, one temple (Nairobi, dedicated 2025-05-18); one Church land purchase found (Karen area-office plot, Nairobi). No Church-run enclave/colony found in Kenya (absence, not proof).
- **Grading**: fact for the policy reversal and current Kenya organization; inference for 'no enclaves exist'.
- **Doctrine tier**: Staying in one's homeland = tier 4 (leader teaching; 1999 First Presidency letter tier 2 but seen second-hand). Gathering by emigration = tier 5 (older practice, use past tense). Gathering of Israel = tier 1 (D&C 133, unverified this run).
- **Adversarial Q1** — Gathering has moved from a place to an institution: stakes under one Utah-headquartered hierarchy, a temple serving seven countries, and the Mexico colonies within living memory; the 1999 letter had to 'reiterate' stay-home counsel.
- **Adversarial Q2** — Do not deny the 19th-century colonies (over half a million acres in Mexico by 1910). 'Converts stay' is policy counsel, not measured behavior; no emigration data. 'Just chapels' is wrong (Kenya has a temple and two missions). **No jab recommended.**
