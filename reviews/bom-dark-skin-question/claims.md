### Claim 1
**SENSITIVE (racial history)**: sec 11: facts and harm first, no analogies, no amused/happy poses, affected-person pass before Gate A. Escalation trigger 4 applies. Unverified: exhaustive text search for 'Black/Africa', Moses 7 / Abraham 1, scripture and essay wording (summarizing fetch), essay date (page shows 2016).
- **Anchor context**: the clip is a setup question (0:11-0:17); the answer is not in it. Responses must stand on what the text says.
- **Research**: The Book of Mormon does not explain how Black people got dark skin. Every skin-color passage (2 Ne 5:21-24, Jacob 3, Alma 3, 3 Ne 2:14-16, Mormon 5:15) is about Lamanites; the one 'black and white' verse is 2 Ne 26:33 ('all are alike unto God'). But the text does tie darker skin to cursing, and the script must say so plainly. Leaders' Black-specific rationale (Brigham Young 1852 onward) rested on Cain, Ham and later premortal 'valiance', not on those passages (Young 1865 treats the Lamanite curse and Cain's curse as two separate curses); Kimball 1960 used 'white and delightsome' for Native Americans; 2 Ne 30:6 read 'white' in 1830/1837, 'pure' from 1840, restored 'white' in the 1981 edition (date sources differ: 1839 approval vs 1840 edition, reconcile before on-screen). 2013 Gospel Topics essay disavows the theory that 'black skin is a sign of divine disfavor or curse'.
- **Grading**: fact for the text; 'never applied to Black people in the text' = inference (no exhaustive full-text search run); literal vs metaphorical reading = disputed (Hunter/Turner literal; Sorenson, Hull, Uriona metaphorical; Steenblik body-paint proposal; Gardner: 'in fact, racist, although not at all in the usual sense').
- **Doctrine tier**: Tier 1 (text); tier 2 (2013 essay, OD2); tier 4-6 (past leader racial readings, superseded).
- **Adversarial Q1** — 'The book never mentions Black people' doesn't answer the charge: the text still has God causing dark skin as a curse (2 Ne 5:21, Alma 3:6), the Church's own essay admits the Cain-curse justification, and the link came through Moses 7 / Abraham 1 plus 19th-century assumption. Disavowal is not an explanation.
- **Adversarial Q2** — Leaders did teach the Cain/Ham/premortal version for ~130 years; the video should concede that plainly. Church has never officially relied on the Book of Mormon for the restriction (Steenblik/Givens cite the Book of Abraham). **Zero jabs.**

### Claim 2
**SENSITIVE (racial history)** trigger 4 and 6 (design question: concession first, then what the text says, no analogies). Unverified: 1949/1969 statements from a primary copy (only the FAIR mirror, a lead with no upstream trace: cannot be cited), start date of the temple restriction, the Jan 23 1852 speech text; current handbook has no race section (don't claim 'the handbook says'). Nelson 2018 NAACP statement is about civility, not the curse doctrine. Young 1852 transcript is rough notes with harmful passages: quote only short verified fragments.
- **Anchor context**: 0:00-0:17 implied claim; no surrounding text changes the meaning.
- **Research**: Two halves. (a) Book of Mormon: skin passages are about Lamanites, not Black people (see claim 1). (b) The Church did teach curse and premortal-disfavor explanations about Black people, which can only be conceded: Young 1852 ('the mark ... of every African'; Pulpit exact), Young 1855 ('seed of Ham, which is the seed of Cain'), Richards 1947 (restriction 'presumably' for lack of valiance), First Presidency 1949 (Cain curse plus premortal conduct) and 1969 (dropped the Cain curse, reason 'known to God'), McConkie 1958 and still 'seed of Cain' in conference Apr 1980. Under Joseph Smith Black men held the priesthood (Elijah Abel, 1836); Young announced the restriction Jan/Feb 1852; Abel refused temple endowment 1879. Ended by Official Declaration 2, 1978-06-08 (McConkie 1978-08-18: 'Forget everything that I have said'). 2012 Newsroom: 'speculation and opinion, not doctrine'; 2013 essay; Nelson Oct 2020.
- **Grading**: fact for the dated events; why the ban began is unknown (Church's own 2012 statement). Half (a) rebuttable; half (b) is true and only concedable (escalation trigger 6).
- **Doctrine tier**: Tier 1 (text); tier 2 (OD2, 2012 statement, 2013 essay); tier 4 (Young, McConkie, Richards); tier 5 (Cain/premortal explanations, now disavowed).
- **Adversarial Q1** — If prophets taught curse-and-colour doctrine for a century and the book still says God darkened a people's skin, why trust the prophet as the mechanism at all? Disavowing leaders' statements about Black people doesn't answer the canonized text.
- **Adversarial Q2** — Kimball (1960) and Hinckley (1997, 'pure and delightsome') used the phrase after the ban's logic was in place; McConkie kept 'seed of Cain' to 1980. An exmo can fairly say the text supplied the vocabulary even though it never names Black people. **Zero jabs.**
