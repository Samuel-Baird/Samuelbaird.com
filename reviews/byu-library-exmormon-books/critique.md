# Critique — opponent lens — byu-library-exmormon-books

Audience: informed ex-Mormon; reviewed the script, beats, claims, sources, both red-team briefs, brand tone, and apologetics playbook.

## Findings

- [MAJOR] n01 / n99 — Routine supervision answers the security mechanism but leaves the stronger privacy objection unanswered.
  Why: The red-team steelman concerns having to register and request a sensitive book rather than read it privately at home. Conceding inconvenience and citing an ordinary security practice does not address that institutional visibility; an informed opponent can accept the guideline and retain the concern. Do not assert that records reach families, bishops, or disciplinary authorities: no such sharing is established here.
  Suggested rewrite: "Reading-room supervision is routine security. Registration and on-site-only reading can still make sensitive reading feel less private; that concern does not by itself establish why these books were placed there."

- [MAJOR] script-level / n07 — The positive case is a documented preservation rationale, not evidence that this rationale explains these titles better than selective circulation limits.
  Why: The script rightly says the suppressive motive is unproved, but the collecting policy only explains acquisition and subject scope. A library can collect critical works comprehensively while choosing not to provide circulating copies. The missing favorable-versus-critical circulation comparison prevents the playbook's Q2 from passing at the stronger explanatory level; do not manufacture a confident exoneration to satisfy it.
  Suggested rewrite: "BYU documents a preservation rationale and collects critical works. That makes preservation a real alternative explanation; we still need the circulation comparison to distinguish it from selective access limits."

- [MINOR] n02 / n07 — Present-tense references to the collecting policy obscure the source's uncertain approval/current status.
  Why: sources.yaml explicitly records an unfilled approval date and notes the document may not reflect current practice. Its contents can be described accurately without treating the published document as a verified current operative policy.
  Suggested rewrite: "BYU's published Americana collecting document covers Mormonism through the present and explicitly includes anti-Mormon works."

- [MINOR] n03–n06 — The Utah comparison needs its limitation before the apparent access verdict.
  Why: The creator compares the U's holding of her own book, not the two institutions' Special Collections reading rooms. n06 currently prevents a clear strawman and correctly separates shelving from room access, but it arrives after roughly thirty seconds of comparison. Earlier placement would prevent the impression that the creator herself made the appointment comparison, or that two-hour appointment blocks prove a daily maximum.
  Suggested rewrite: "She mentions the U because it holds her book. This next comparison is narrower: the two reading rooms' published visit rules, not where the U shelves these memoirs."

## Script-level (two-question test)

Q1 (any beat failing the strawman test — cross-reference above): No BLOCKER. Both source clips remain intact; the script concedes the real checkout barrier and disputes the expressly stated motive inference. The n06 distinction is necessary and should move earlier to preserve that fairness throughout the Utah comparison. The script does not claim a two-hour daily cap or verified memoir shelving at Utah.

Q2 (positive case made, or only negative case?): Partial positive case, but not a full pass under the strict playbook test. The script establishes that supervision has a standard security rationale and that BYU publishes a preservation/collecting rationale. It does not establish that those rationales explain the non-circulation of these particular memoirs better than the critic's alternative. Keep the conclusion bounded to the actual evidence unless equivalent recent favorable-title circulation records can be obtained.

The concessions in n06 and n99 materially improve fairness. On the reviewed evidence, no claim that BYU censors these titles, and no claim that BYU has been proved viewpoint-neutral, is warranted.
