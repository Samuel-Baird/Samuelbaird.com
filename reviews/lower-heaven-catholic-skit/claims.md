### Independent revision scripture findings

## Finding — claim 1 revision
The proposed blanket Catholic judgment on Mormons is inaccurate as a statement of the Catechism. CCC 846 says salvation comes through Christ and his Church and that a person who knows the Catholic Church is necessary yet refuses to enter or remain cannot be saved. CCC 847 explicitly qualifies that statement: people who lack knowledge through no fault, sincerely seek God, and follow conscience under grace may attain eternal salvation. CCC 848 preserves evangelization while acknowledging God's ways of leading the ignorant. A fair comparison can say both faiths affirm a necessary relationship to Christ and authorized Church, with distinctions concerning knowledge and responsibility. It cannot assign hell to every Mormon.

## Proposed grade — claim 1
fact — verified Catholic teaching is conditional, not a universal verdict based on LDS identification. Any asserted judgment on a particular Mormon is speculation.

## Finding — claim 2 revision
The supplied repository note correctly limits its proposed biblical parallel. Lamentations 4:7–9 contrasts former healthy appearance with dark, withered appearance in a famine context; 5:10 explicitly identifies famine as the cause of blackened skin. This shows biblical color language can describe bodily condition rather than ancestry. It supports considering a contextual/figurative reading of Book of Mormon color passages; it does not establish which reading those passages require. Famine could involve actual bodily effects as well as poetic comparison. Therefore the sweeping statement that skin language in biblical Hebrew is always figurative or never ethnic exceeds this evidence. Retain the canonical BoM difficulty and the independently verified historical literal reading; acknowledge a possible biblical idiom without claiming literal pigmentation is impossible.

## Proposed grade — claim 2
fact for what Lamentations says; inference for transferring that imagery to Book of Mormon passages. Disputed for deciding the Book of Mormon's intended color meaning.
