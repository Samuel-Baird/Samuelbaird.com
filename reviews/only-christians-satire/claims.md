## Per-claim notes (adversarial review required for every "Chosen: yes" claim)

### Claim 1

- **Anchor context**: 0:00–0:11 read in full; no hedges. The premise is 'a denomination is Christian if and only if Jesus is in its name.' Read as satire of the 'Mormons aren't Christian' argument; the serious response must not attribute the literal view to the creator.
- **Research**: The word "Christian" itself (Acts 11:26) contains Christ, not Jesus, and was first applied to the disciples at Antioch; no church title is involved. LDS side: 3 Nephi 27:8 states the name principle (a church called in a man's name is a church of a man) AND its condition (only "if it so be that they are built upon my gospel"). D&C 1:30 "only true ... church" is addressed "collectively and not individually"; Largey (BYU reference) says it does not mean other Christians are not genuine followers of Christ.
- **Grading**: fact (Acts 11:26, 3 Ne 27:8, D&C 1:30 wording); inference (that the name is ownership, not a certificate of discipleship).
- **Doctrine tier** (if applicable): Tier 1 scripture (3 Ne 27:8, D&C 1:30); Largey is a reference work, NOT an official statement -- phrased "a standard LDS reference".
- **Adversarial Q1**: Does 3 Nephi 27:8 not simply confirm the reel? Answer on screen: yes, it states the name principle (n06 says so plainly), and then the condition and the ownership-vs-discipleship distinction do the work.
- **Adversarial Q2**: The reel is satire; the video does not claim the creator believed it and sets it up as "stated as a doctrine".
- **Turnabout**: The test applied to its own word: Christian fails the name test (n99).
- **Briefs**: No separate briefs: researched inline by the main session (capy-* subagent roles unavailable); sources in sources.yaml.
- **Red-team persona**: informed Protestant/Catholic reader who accepts the premise that names matter.
### Claim 2

- **Anchor context**: 0:11–0:20 read in full; the 'protesting, not worshiping' and 'worship Martin Luther' lines both rest on the etymology of the label.
- **Research**: "Catholic" first appears in Christian literature in Ignatius (FOTC footnote, Smyrn. 8), defined by the presence of Jesus Christ. "Protest" < Latin protestari "declare publicly, testify" (etymonline), noun c.1400 "avowal, solemn declaration", "disapproval" only by 1751; 1529 Speyer protest per Schaff was against measures contrary to the Word of God.
- **Grading**: fact. The Ignatius sentence is bishop-centered (card shows it in full). Whether Ignatius meant "universal" vs "orthodox" -- FOTC footnote says only "universal" here.
