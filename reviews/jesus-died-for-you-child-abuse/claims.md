### Claim 1
**SENSITIVE (abuse/mental-health adjacent)**: research-protocol sec 11 applies (no analogies, clinical tone, affected-person pass before Gate A). Counter-texts to address in one sentence: D&C 68:25-27, Moses 6:55 ('conceived in sin'), Mosiah 3:16. Bible quotes came through a summarizing fetch: re-check.
- **Anchor context**: whole 97s source read in full; 0:12-0:24 defines emotional abuse as fear/shame/guilt/manipulation, 1:15-1:27 concedes love, accountability and repair can be taught.
- **Research**: Philosopher: the claim bundles a developmental point with an abuse charge and slides from 'can often be' to a verdict on teaching the atonement at all; her own definition describes delivery, not doctrine. Her mechanism is real (some children internalize 'I am bad'). Consistency test: 'a child could misread it' would also condemn Santa's naughty list. Chen & VanderWeele 2018 (opened) associates adolescent religious attendance with better well-being (observational; counts against 'often', doesn't refute her). Scripture: LDS scripture rejects inherited guilt (Moroni 8:8 'little children are whole', A of F 2, Moses 6:54, Mosiah 15:25, D&C 137:10); Matt 19:14 / Mark 10:14-16.
- **Grading**: disputed: what the texts say = fact; 'love-first frame' = inference; psychological claim = disputed. Clinical definition of emotional abuse NOT verified (all fetches failed); no prevalence data.
- **Doctrine tier**: Tier 1 (scripture) for innocence of little children.
- **Adversarial Q1** — Moroni 8 shows children under eight are innocent, but Moroni 8:10 and D&C 68:25-27 make them accountable and baptized 'for the remission of their sins' at eight, the concrete-thinking age she describes. The response answers a claim about infants, not how a child takes in 'Jesus died for your sins' plus repentance and worthiness, and offers no evidence on how LDS children experience it.
- **Adversarial Q2** — Her own alternative ('accountability and repair') itself requires a child to grasp 'my act hurt someone', the guilt cognition she calls abusive; she collapses guilt ('I did a bad thing') into shame ('I am bad') (Tangney, unverified, needs direct read). Body text only says children 'may internalize' it, weaker than the 'often emotionally abusive' opener. Clip targets the atonement generally, not Mormonism. Turnabout: none safe; do not use 'religious trauma isn't a DSM diagnosis'.

### Claim 2
Do not claim D&C 68:27 is backed by child-development research. Innocence doctrine (Moroni 8, D&C 29:46-47) is a different point from teaching the Atonement to under-eights.
- **Anchor context**: 0:20-0:34 read in full with neighbours; she asserts this as certain and never cites a study.
- **Research**: Philosopher (partial): preschoolers attribute fallible human-like minds to God (Lane, Wellman & Evans 2010; Heiphetz 2016) and reason concretely about death (Bering & Bjorklund 2004), but 'do not have the capacity' is too categorical (Barrett et al. 2001; Kelemen 2004 'intuitive theists'). No study found on whether children misread the atonement as 'I am bad, someone died because of me': that step is her uncited inference. Double standard: Evans 2001 shows evolution is also non-intuitive and beliefs track the teaching community (comprehension comparison only). LDS side: the Church says to teach children 'simply and clearly' (Teaching, No Greater Call 1999); children under eight are innocent (Packer 2002, verified exact); Lybbert 1994 and Burton 2012 verified.
- **Grading**: inference (partly true, overstated). Lane 2010 vs Barrett 2001 disagreement not reconciled; Piaget/Fowler not verified.
- **Doctrine tier**: Tier 3-4 (current curriculum, leader talks 1994-2025). 1994 'rock in a bag' lesson is tier 5, not current.
- **Adversarial Q1** — Concrete-thinking children hear 'Jesus died for your sins' as 'I caused someone's suffering' (Piaget immanent justice); D&C 68:25-27 puts accountability at eight, where children move to concrete operations.
- **Adversarial Q2** — Guilt is adaptive and shame maladaptive in children (Tangney; unverified, lead only). Her body text is weaker than her opener. Primary line 'Because Jesus suffered and died for me, I can repent and be forgiven' (Come Follow Me) and Jones 2021 'we cannot begin too soon' are what the critic targets: weak points for the LDS side. Most useful missing fact: primary curriculum text teaching under-eights that Jesus suffered for their sins.

### Claim 3
**SENSITIVE**: sec 11 applies. Concede the shame problem is real; do not overclaim what LDS children are actually taught. Unverified: Primary/Come Follow Me/Songbook text, Bednar/Nelson talks aimed at children.
- **Anchor context**: 0:34-1:02 read in full; 'may internalize' is a psychological prediction, not a doctrinal claim about what the Church teaches.
- **Research**: Scripture frame is love-first (John 3:16-17, Rom 5:8, 1 John 4:19; Moses 6:54, D&C 93:38, Mosiah 3:16). Leaders speak against shame: Nelson Apr 2019 ('Too many people consider repentance as punishment'), Renlund 2016, Holland Oct 2017 (toxic perfectionism), Runia Apr 2025: all verified, all addressed to adults/youth, not young children. Feeds the critic: Burton Oct 2012 quotes a 1997 Primary lesson pairing repentance, obedience and 'worthy' with the Atonement (verified); baptism interview at eight (Handbook 38.2.8.2, summary only). Packer's 1977 'The Mediator' is a debt parable in official LDS wording (not verified as used with young children). No Church curriculum found teaching young children the 'your badness caused someone to suffer' framing.
- **Grading**: disputed (psychological prediction; leader statements cannot settle it). Text claims = fact.
- **Doctrine tier**: Tier 1 (Moroni 8); tier 4 (anti-shame leader talks 2016-2025); tier 3 (baptism at eight, current handbook).
- **Adversarial Q1** — The Moroni 8 rebuttal establishes infant innocence; accountability at eight is the concrete-thinking age; the response gives no evidence about how LDS children actually experience the teaching (scrupulosity, worthiness interviews).
- **Adversarial Q2** — Her alternative ('accountability and repair') requires guilt cognition; guilt vs shame distinction. Do not cite Holland 2016 'guilt trips' (a home-teaching joke, not about children or the Atonement). Renlund/Runia warning against shame suggests it is a live problem in the culture.
