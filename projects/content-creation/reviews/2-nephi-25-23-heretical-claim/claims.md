## Per-claim notes (adversarial review required for every "Chosen: yes" claim)

### Claim 2
- **Anchor context**: 0:00-0:35 read in full (transcript and srt). She reads the verse, asserts it "seems as though" works are a prerequisite, then moves to 2 Timothy 1:9.
- **Research**: The verse names grace as what saves; it says nothing about works having been done (scripture-theologian). McClellan (JBMS 29, 2020, read) shows "after all (that) X can do" was a stock 1710-1840 idiom meaning "despite all X can do", used by Protestant writers; he concedes the temporal sense is "not precluded". Church leaders have read it both ways: works-then-grace (Romney 1955, Lee 1970, Packer 1995, Oaks 1998) and concessive (Uchtdorf 2015, "after does not equal because"; Christofferson 2014 mediating). Spencer (2014) gives a third reading. Protestant parallels were seen only through McClellan's quotations, not opened.
- **Grading**: disputed (all three claim-2 briefs agree). That leaders taught both readings is fact; that "despite" is the original sense is one scholar's inference.
- **Doctrine tier**: verse = tier 1; each reading = tier 4 (leader teaching, dated). Do not say "the Church teaches" either reading. No official statement adopts "despite".
- **Adversarial Q1**: Even if "after" means "despite", LDS teaching still makes final salvation depend on obedience, covenants and ordinances (Moroni 10:32, 2 Nephi 31:20, Article of Faith 3), and the Bible Dictionary says grace "cannot suffice without total effort". The script therefore concedes that conditionality is a real difference and does not claim LDS and evangelical teaching are the same.
- **Adversarial Q2**: The reel omits that leaders (Lee, Packer, Bible Dictionary) taught the works-first reading for decades, which the script states plainly in n03, and it omits the rest of 2 Nephi 25 (law of Moses context, v. 24-25).
- **Turnabout**: Matthew Bates (evangelical NT scholar) writes that every major tradition except "free-gracers" holds that good works are necessary for final salvation, so a "heresy" test on that point would condemn Lutherans, Baptists, Presbyterians and Wesleyans. Used lightly in n05/n99 via the Westminster Confession; the Bates quote itself is NOT used on screen (not recorded in sources.yaml).

### Claim 3
- **Anchor context**: 0:22-1:01 read in full. She reads 2 Timothy 1:9 in ESV-family wording, infers God "extended his grace and mercy to you" before creation, and concludes works are "not a prerequisite for grace and salvation".
- **Research**: In the NRSVue the verse says God "saved us and called us with a holy calling, not according to our works but according to his own purpose and grace, and this grace was given to us in Christ Jesus before the ages began" (page-read). The video reads the verse correctly as far as ground and origin go. The inference "so works are not a prerequisite" silently turns "prerequisite" into "ground". The same epistles (Eph 2:9-10, Titus 3:5-8, Rom 2:6, 2 Cor 5:10, Matt 25, Phil 2:12-13, James 2) tie works to following, accompanying or being judged in relation to salvation. Reformed interpreters (Gaffin via Piper, Ridley, Cartwright, WCF 11.2 and 16.2) call works necessary evidence and fruit, not ground. Current Conference teaching says works do not earn salvation (Uchtdorf 2015, Bednar 2015, Holland 2017), so "works are a prerequisite for grace" misstates current LDS teaching; but the Church also teaches that obedience, covenants and ordinances are conditions for receiving grace's effects, and Faust (2001) said "We cannot be saved by grace alone".
- **Grading**: disputed.
- **Doctrine tier**: "works do not earn salvation" = tier 4 resting on tier 1; conditionality of grace's effects = tiers 3-4; final judgment "according to their works" = tier 4 (Oaks 2019; paraphrase only, not used on screen).
- **Adversarial Q1**: Even granting "after does not equal because", Packer and Lee taught the prerequisite reading for decades; Alma 13:3 grounds a premortal "holy calling" in "exceeding faith and good works"; and exaltation still depends on ordinances, so the script must answer conditionality, not just one word. The script's n06 states the conditionality openly rather than dodging it. A Reformed viewer will also say post-conversion works are excluded too (Dutch Annotations, Dordt) and that LDS ordinance-requirements reintroduce works as a condition of justification; the script does not claim to refute that.
- **Adversarial Q2**: The reel omits the rest of 2 Timothy (2:12, 4:7-8, 4:14), the wording difference between her translation and the NRSVue, and the context of 2 Tim 1:8-10 (Paul urging costly obedience).
- **Turnabout**: Her one-verse-against-one-verse method would make James ("not by faith alone") heretical first. Used as the closing line through Philippians, Ephesians and the Westminster Confession; the Luther "epistle of straw" line is NOT used because it is not in sources.yaml.
