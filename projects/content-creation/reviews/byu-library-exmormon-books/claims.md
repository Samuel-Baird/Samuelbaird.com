### Claim 1
Do not argue the books are rare/fragile. Do not flatly deny that someone watches you read. Unverified: catalog records, whether Brodie/Krakauer circulate at BYU, current version of the Americana and Restriction policies (undated/2021).
- **Anchor context**: whole 2:05 source read in full; the creator's on-screen catalog screenshots are the only evidence for the on-site-use-only status.
- **Research**: Could NOT confirm the catalog records for Bad Mormon and Devout (BYU catalog is JavaScript; fetch tool cannot read it). BYU's own Americana FAQ confirms the rule is by subject ('Mormonism, Utah and the American West'), and 'because these books are being kept for preservation purposes, they may not be checked out'. Special Collections reading room is open to the general public (registration; request required). No page I opened says readers are watched (her claim is uncorroborated, don't flatly deny). BYU's Americana policy says Mormon-related books are 'collected comprehensively' including 'Anti-Mormon works'.
- **Grading**: inference / partly unverified: the restriction is real per her screenshots and BYU's FAQ, monitoring uncorroborated.
- **Doctrine tier**: n/a (library policy, not doctrine).
- **Adversarial Q1** — Even if all Mormon-related books go to Special Collections, that explains where they are shelved, not why a trade memoir is non-circulating; Special Collections' own purpose is 'materials requiring great care' and its philosophy page speaks of 'strengthening faith'. No comparison shows faithful recent trade books get the same tier.
- **Adversarial Q2** — She never checks whether faithful recent Mormon books get the same restriction or whether a circulating copy exists elsewhere; the reading room is open to the public, not only BYU students; she treats her own book's 'no record found' as benign. **No jab found.**

### Claim 2
**Possible escalation trigger 6**: concede the access friction is real with context rather than a clean rebuttal unless catalog screenshots show faithful recent Mormon titles are also non-circulating (the decisive missing comparison). Unverified: BYU stack availability of Brodie/Krakauer, a BYU page giving an age-independent rationale, a broader library survey (LSU, Vanderbilt, Delaware appeared only as search snippets).
- **Anchor context**: 1:32-2:05 read in full; motive is asserted as 'pretty clear'.
- **Research**: Philosopher: the claim bundles a real access barrier (on screen) with an unproven motive; rival explanations (comprehensive collecting policy, anti-theft/high-demand handling, suppression) are never tested. BYU's policy cuts against suppression (it buys them, lists them openly in a searchable catalog, collects anti-Mormon works) but fits recent trade books poorly ('too rare to be housed in the regular stacks'). Baseline: UNF Special Collections treats books 'by local authors, about local history' as non-circulating separately from 'rare, old and/or expensive'; Utah State lists Mormon history and culture as a core Special Collections area; University of Utah runs its reading room by appointment (2 business days' notice).
- **Grading**: inference. Mechanism documented at one comparator; motive unshown either way.
- **Doctrine tier**: n/a (library policy).
- **Adversarial Q1** — A benign collecting policy is still an institutional choice whose effect is that the two best-known critical memoirs are harder to read than a comparable open-stack book; a response can prove a rule but not a reason.
- **Adversarial Q2** — **Turnabout (modest, one flat sentence, no accusation)**: the University of Utah, her friendlier comparator, also runs its Special Collections by appointment; this shows the mechanism she calls sinister is standard, not that Utah shelves mass-market memoirs there. BYU's Americana list includes 20th/21st-century Mormon manuscripts, so the 'antiques' premise is weak.
