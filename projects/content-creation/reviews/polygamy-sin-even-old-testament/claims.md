## Per-claim notes (adversarial review required for every "Chosen: yes" claim)

### Claims 1–2 — the frame

- **Anchor context**: 0:00–1:49 read in full. Claim 1 is an admission; claim 2 is the creator's own conditional hinge ("if it's not, then Christians can't really use polygamy itself to critique Smith"). He then says "Full disclosure, I do believe that polygamy is wrong."
- **Research**: none needed beyond the transcript; the script quotes him and restates the hinge.
- **Grading**: fact (what he said).
- **Doctrine tier**: n/a.
- **Adversarial Q1** — strongest objection after the response: "You are spinning an honest admission; he said no verse says abomination, but he never claimed an explicit verse was necessary; he argues the Bible teaches it implicitly and evangelical scholars like Wenham agree implicit narrative ethics is how the Hebrew Bible teaches." The script's wording ("in so many words") is deliberately narrow, and n11b / n29a concede the narrative-ethics point is a live scholarly view.
- **Adversarial Q2** — what the clip leaves out: he does go on to say Scripture "doesn't always teach ethics by explicitly stating restrictions" (7:52). The script does not claim he denies this; it tests whether the implicit case carries.
- **Turnabout**: the standard "a verse must say it" is not the creator's standard; the standard is "the Bible teaches it by inference." That means the script cannot simply say "no verse" and stop; it must (and does) answer the inferences.
- **Briefs**: none.

### Claims 3–4 — Genesis 2 / creation / sufficiency

- **Anchor context**: 2:03–4:42 read. He uses Jesus' Genesis-2 argument about divorce (Matthew 19) as the template: "Using that same logic."
- **Research**: Matthew 19:9 (NRSVue) is about the man who divorces and marries another. Stephens deliberately excludes Gen 2:24 and says (n.16) the New Testament moves toward monogamy; the JETS reviewer notes the omission. Wenham (via Stephens p.17): "the law tolerates bigamy" though the creation text presents monogamy as the divine intention. 1 Cor 6:16 applies "the two shall be one flesh" to a man and a prostitute.
- **Grading**: inference (his) vs fact (what Matthew 19 and 1 Cor 6 say).
- **Doctrine tier**: Jacob 2:27, 30 (scripture, tier 1); Gospel Topics essay on monogamy as the Lord's "standing law of marriage" (tier 2, official statement).
- **Adversarial Q1**: "Jesus appeals to creation to override a Mosaic concession; that is exactly the hermeneutic that would override a polygamy concession too." Strongest version of his case, and it is held by serious scholars. The script does not deny the pattern; it says one more premise is required and names it as his.
- **Adversarial Q2**: Matthew 19:8 (hardness of heart) was not retrievable on the page fetched; the script cites it only through his reading.
- **Turnabout**: Paul uses the same Genesis phrase for a union with a prostitute (1 Cor 6:16), so "the two" cannot be read as a ceiling on unions a man may have. Labelled as inference on screen ("describes what a union is"). None found against his own tradition beyond that.
- **Briefs**: none.

### Claims 10–15 — New Testament

- **Anchor context**: 10:33–16:15 read, including the 1 Corinthians 7 and Ephesians 5 readings.
- **Research**: Towner (NICNT; PDF p.302, fn.42): "Various considerations make these specific interpretations less than likely"; polygamy prohibition would have been unnecessary because monogamy was "generally accepted norm"; a polygamy reading would force a polyandry reading of 1 Tim 5:9. Towner, Titus 1:6: "does not specifically delve into matters of polygamy or remarriage." Knight (NIGTC pp.157–159): polygamy "certainly ruled out" but "two considerations make it doubtful that polygamy is exclusively in view"; notes polygamy existed among Jews of the NT age (Josephus, Antiquities 17.14; Bellum Judaicum 1.477) and that Theodosius legislated against Jewish polygamy in AD 393. NRSVue 1 Tim 3:2: "married only once"; 3:6: "must not be a recent convert". Ex 21:10: marital rights owed to the first wife when a second is taken.
- **Grading**: disputed (1 Tim 3:2 scope); inference (1 Cor 7, Eph 5).
- **Doctrine tier**: n/a.
- **Adversarial Q1**: "Knight, a major evangelical commentator, says polygamy is certainly ruled out by the phrase; you are overselling Towner." Answered: the script reports both (e01, e02) and says the scholars split, claiming only that "makes clear" overstates agreement.
- **Adversarial Q2**: the creator's list of qualifications includes 'above reproach' etc. (he reads all). The 'recent convert' reductio uses the same list he cites.
- **Turnabout**: Towner (non-LDS, evangelical) is the commentator who argues the specific polygamy reading is less than likely and who notes a polyandry parallel; the script uses him as the creator's own side's scholarship. Used in the text, not as a jab.
- **Briefs**: none.

## Corrections to the user-supplied brief (pasted in chat)

- **Waltke on Lamech** was listed as a non-LDS source for the response. Waltke (p.82) actually reads Lamech's polygamy as part of the decline and "debase[s] God's ideal for marriage by polygamy"; he supports the creator's literary reading. Script uses him only to acknowledge that reading.
- **Stephens**: the brief says he "specifically challenges the modern evangelical interpretation" and that the JETS review "praises ... as persuasive." True for the Law; but the same review says Stephens is "much less successful" on the narratives, notes the New Covenant is "expected to move away from" polygamy (Stephens' own words), and asks how his view differs from the concession view. All three points are in the script. 'Baptist' Gateway Seminary: the review says only 'Gateway Seminary'; the script says only that.
- **Partridge sisters**: the brief says a ceremony was "subsequently performed" and, elsewhere, that the repeat was in September 1843; the verified record (Hales vol.2 p.32) is the **11 May 1843** repeat in Emma's presence. The Hales Emma page does not mention a September ceremony. Script uses 11 May.
- **Brigham Young on Joseph objecting to the 1835 statement**: not verified in this run; omitted.
- **Ruth Vose Sayers**: Hales' own article calls the account "late and at least secondhand"; omitted from the script.
- **Josephine Lyon DNA**: the brief says the DNA 'indicates' she was not Joseph's child; Perego's own wording is 'appears' with a stated limit (not a true paternity test). Script uses 'appears'.
- **Number of sealings to married women**: brief and Hales say fourteen; the Church essay says 12–14. Script uses 'fourteen' as Hales's list and does not state the Church's number.
- **Sylvia Sessions and "no evidence of sexual relations"**: the brief says there is no unambiguous evidence of sexual relations with any of the fourteen. Hales's own 2013 Appendix E (vol.2 pp.208, 211) counts Sylvia Sessions and (by elimination) Mary Heron among the twelve wives with *probable* sexual relations, while finding convincing evidence lacking for "all polyandrous wives who were experiencing conjugality with their legal husbands". Sylvia's case rested on her daughter Josephine's 1915 affidavit, which Perego's 2019 DNA study undercuts. The script says exactly this (n43, n43b, n48, n49) instead of the brief's blanket statement.
- **Ryan Stephens on Leviticus 18:18**: the brief did not mention that Stephens reads it as the restriction to 'wives who are not sisters'. That supports the creator's strongest text against the sister pairs; the script says so.
- **Evangelical consensus**: the brief's framing implies the creator is out of line with evangelical scholarship. Stephens p.17–19 shows the creator's reading is the 'second' group (law tolerates, not the creation ideal) and the 'fourth' group (law prohibits) of four, both substantial. The script says 'evangelical scholars disagree'.
