## Per-claim notes (adversarial review required for every "Chosen: yes" claim)

### Claim 1
- **Anchor context**: 0:00-0:25 read in full. The clip merges the angel visit (1823), the plates (1827) and a Masonic label that only applies from 1842.
- **Research**: Smith petitioned the Nauvoo lodge on 30 Dec 1841 and was initiated 15 Mar 1842 (Dialogue 34:1, page-read). An earlier "Joseph Smith" raised in Ontario Lodge No. 23 in 1817-18 is probably Joseph Sr., and that identification is debated (Vogel: "unlikely"). The 1826 South Bainbridge hearing is real; the charge was a "disorderly person" misdemeanor (Quinn); outcome is disputed: Benton 1831 "condemned", Cowdery 1835 a different outcome; the docket does not survive (Madsen argues for a single-justice examination with no conviction). "Gold painter": no source found. Treasure-seeking and seer-stone use are documented and acknowledged in the Church's own essay.
- **Grading**: disputed (chronology claim is fact-wrong; the "con man" label is a contested inference).
- **Doctrine tier**: n/a (history).
- **Adversarial Q1**: Even with the dates fixed, the pattern remains: a paid treasure-seer whose stone appears in the translation story; the script has to reach that inference and not only fix chronology. n02 concedes the practice is documented and says it does not settle the label's verdict; a hostile viewer will say that is thin. Not fully answered in a short.
- **Adversarial Q2**: The clip omits that seer stones were common among churchgoing New Yorkers in the 1820s, and omits the 1826 fee bills and Hyrum's 1820s lodge membership, which cut against the response; the script does not deny any of these.
- **Turnabout**: hostile-witness standard (Matt 27:63 "that impostor") and Protestant treasure-seekers (Willard Chase, later a Methodist minister). NOT used: Matt 27:63 is not recorded in sources.yaml, and only one jab is allowed in a short.

### Claim 3
- **Anchor context**: 0:26-0:35 read in full.
- **Research**: The Church's Gospel Topics essay: "Careful estimates put the number between 30 and 40" (page-read). Compton lists 33 (11 married to other men at the time; 10 under age 20); Bushman 32; Hales 35, with evidence running from 29 to 52; others 36-52 without documentation. Hales sorts his 35 into 3 time-only, 13 eternity-only, 19 time-and-eternity, which is inference. Whether any of the youngest sealings involved sexual relations is not established.
- **Grading**: fact for the 30-40 count; inference for individual classifications.
- **Doctrine tier**: n/a (history).
- **Adversarial Q1**: Even if OT polygamy shows God can command plural marriage, Smith's own practice has to meet his revelation's conditions (D&C 132:61 vs. women with living husbands), the Church's essay describes secrecy even from Emma, and he publicly denied it. The script concedes the count and says the rightness question is a separate argument; it does not defend the practice.
- **Adversarial Q2**: The clip omits ages, whether women were already married, and sealing categories; the script supplies Compton's and Hales's numbers rather than minimizing.
- **Turnabout**: Luther's counsel on Philip of Hesse is available but would read as moral equivalence; NOT used (WELS says Luther's counsel "is not one with which we can agree").
- **Briefs**: `research/briefs/claim3-capy-historian.md`, `claim3-capy-red-team.md`.

### Claim 4
- **Anchor context**: 0:31-1:19 read in full. The clip also dates the vision to 1823; JS-H 1:14 dates the First Vision to spring 1820 (the angel visit is 1823).
- **Research**: The NRSVue records both "No one has ever seen God" (John 1:18; 1 John 4:12) and Exod 24:10-11 ("they saw the God of Israel"), Gen 32:30, Isa 6:5, Num 12:8. Abraham is only "appeared to". Rev 4 never says the four creatures see God; John sees "one seated on the throne" (4:2). The reel's description of the creatures as having "human bodies and animal heads" does not match Rev 4:7. The First Vision accounts differ on who appeared (1832 "the Lord"; 1838 "two personages"), acknowledged by the Church's essay and by Harper. Reformed/evangelical harmonization: Christophany / mediated vision.
- **Grading**: disputed (the "nobody" premise is false as stated; the First Vision is not authenticated by it).
- **Doctrine tier**: scripture (tier 1) for the Bible texts; the First Vision accounts are primary historical documents.
- **Adversarial Q1**: Granted the overstatement, every biblical theophany can be read as the Son or mediated glory (John 1:18, 1 Tim 6:16), and the 1832 account describes only "the Lord"; the part beyond the Bible, a visible Father, appears only in later retellings. The script's n06 says the accounts differ and calls that a better argument than the clip's; it does not claim the vision is authenticated.
- **Adversarial Q2**: The clip omits Exod 24:10-11, Gen 32:30, Isa 6:5, Rev 4:2; it also merges 1820 and 1823.
- **Turnabout**: "which rule you bring to the text" (Exod 24 vs. John 1:18) is used in the closing. The Westminster Confession "invisible" line is NOT used (unverified).
- **Briefs**: `research/briefs/claim4-capy-scripture-theologian.md`, `claim4-capy-historian.md`, `claim4-capy-red-team.md`.
- **Red-team persona**: protestant (evangelical) plus believing-member.
