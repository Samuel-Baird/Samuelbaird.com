### Claim 1

- **Anchor context**: read the full 2:18 transcript; the speaker is cordial and says at 1:59 this 'isn't necessarily an argument for or against that theology'.
- **Research**: Official position: the Church says Latter-day Saints are Christians (Hinckley 1998, 'Of course we are Christians. We believe in Christ. We worship Christ.'; Holland 2007; Nelson 2018 'the name of the Church is not negotiable'). NT confessional markers (Rom 10:9, 1 Cor 12:3, 1 John 4:2) are short Christological confessions, not a creed; Nicene points (ex nihilo, Godhead, theosis) are later tests. 'Christian' is rare in the NT and probably began as an outsiders' label (contested). Catholic teaching: UR 3 and CCC 818 say baptized non-Catholics 'have a right to be called Christians', but the CDF answered 'negative' on 5 June 2001 on whether LDS baptism is valid (Ladaria note: not 'Christian Baptism'), so Rome's test is valid Trinitarian baptism, not doctrinal agreement.
- **Grading**: disputed: textual facts solid; whether the label is fixed by NT confession or by later creed is the real dispute.
- **Doctrine tier**: Tier 2 (official statements) and tier 4 (leader teaching) for 'we are Christians'; tier 4 (Holland) for rejecting the creeds.
- **Adversarial Q1** — Sincerity and self-identification don't settle it: across Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant churches 'Christian' has a working meaning defined by the Trinity and creation from nothing, and LDS apostles openly reject both, so the LDS side asks everyone else to change the word.
- **Adversarial Q2** — The 'not Christians' framing contradicts the video's own named source (see claim 2); the speaker's real argument is the next line (ex nihilo). **Turnabout (one dry line, with its limits)**: CCC 460, 'the Son of God became man so that we might become God' (verified), and CCC 818, baptismal test not a checklist. Limit: Catholics don't treat LDS baptism as valid and Catholic theosis differs from LDS exaltation.
- **Briefs**: `research/briefs/claim1-capy-doctrine-scholar.md`, `claim1-capy-scripture-theologian.md`, `claim1-capy-red-team.md`
- **Red-team persona**: catholic
- **Unverified / cautions**: Do NOT say 'Rome calls you Christians': UR 3/CCC 818 require valid baptism, which the CDF denies. CCC 460/818 and UR 3 wording came via summarizing fetches. Holland's Trinity sentence quotes Harper's Bible Dictionary; his 'incomprehensible' lines are paraphrase-only. Gospel Topics page: re-read before quoting.

### Claim 2

- **Anchor context**: read the full 2:18 transcript; the speaker is cordial and says at 1:59 this 'isn't necessarily an argument for or against that theology'.
- **Research**: Deseret News (Mariya Manzhos, 2026-05-04, on Hansen's talk with Allie Beth Stuckey) quotes him: 'We would consider ourselves Christians, but we will not consider ourselves Creedal Christians.' He claims the label and rejects only the creedal definition; he affirms the Apostles' Creed and most of Nicene except on the nature relation of Father and Son. So the video's 'will claim that they are not Christians themselves' is misleading, though its underlying point survives for a Catholic audience (Hansen concedes LDS belief fails the creedal test). The official Church position is that Latter-day Saints are Christians; no official source says otherwise.
- **Grading**: disputed, provisional: one secondary news report, not Hansen's own recording.
- **Doctrine tier**: Tier 6 (individual apologist, May 2026) for Hansen's phrasing.
- **Adversarial Q1** — Grant that Hansen says 'we are Christians': he also rejects the creedal definition and parts from Nicaea, so the response only shows the video's wording was loose, not that its conclusion is wrong by the test Catholics have used for centuries.
- **Adversarial Q2** — The strongest point against the video is that its 'not Christians' wording contradicts its own named source. **Turnabout weak / not recommended**: UR 3 treats validly baptized non-Catholics as Christians without requiring agreement on every dogma, but the CDF 2001 ruling defeats it.

### Claim 3

- **Anchor context**: read the full 2:18 transcript; the speaker is cordial and says at 1:59 this 'isn't necessarily an argument for or against that theology'.
- **Research**: LDS: God organized the world from existing material (Joseph Smith, April 1844: 'create' means 'organize', God 'had materials to organize from'; Hugh B. Brown 1964; Nelson 2000 'It did not come ex nihilo'; Maxwell 1985 calls ex nihilo 'false doctrine'). The video is loose: scripture says the elements are eternal (D&C 93:33) and intelligence was 'not created or made' (D&C 93:29); bodies are organized from earth's elements (Abr 4:26-27). 'Christians believe' is true of later orthodoxy but not of the earliest: May: ex nihilo is not an OT theory; Justin wrote of 'formless matter'; Theophilus (c.180) is the first church theologian to use it unambiguously (against Hermogenes); educated Christians held pre-existent matter into the early 3rd century; Levenson: 'out of nothing' doesn't fit most biblical creation texts; Clifford (Jesuit): 'largely the result of second- and third-century debates with Gnosticism' yet 'a good biblical basis'. 2 Macc 7:28 is the strongest text; Wisdom 11:17 ('formless matter') is in the Catholic canon. Catholic side: defined dogma (Lateran IV 1215; CCC 296).
- **Grading**: fact for the core claim (each belief as stated); 'made us out of eternal matter' is misleading; 'always the Christian view' is disputed (inference).
- **Doctrine tier**: Tier 1 (D&C 93, 131:7-8, Abr 3:22-24, 4:1); tier 2 (2016 Gospel Topics 'Becoming Like God', consistent); tier 3-4 (Brown, Nelson, Maxwell); tier 4 (King Follett Discourse, never canonized).
- **Adversarial Q1** — Even if Justin's Platonist 'unformed matter' leaves room for shaping, Theophilus states creation 'out of nothing' in settled terms and May reads him as drawing on an older tradition; the LDS need 'the true doctrine was lost', not 'some early Christians used Platonist language'. No Father puts God in the same category as eternal matter.
- **Adversarial Q2** — This is a real doctrinal gap, not a misunderstanding (concede it to a Catholic audience). **Turnabout**: Hughes' Hebrews commentary says ex nihilo first appeared as a credal article only at Lateran IV (1215); a Catholic who appeals to development of doctrine can't use 'the Fathers didn't dogmatize this' against the LDS restoration claim. Justin check: ACW editor's note says Justin had 'no particular theory of the origin and nature of matter'.
- **Briefs**: `research/briefs/claim3-capy-doctrine-scholar.md`, `claim3-capy-scripture-theologian.md`, `claim3-capy-historian.md`, `claim3-capy-red-team.md`
- **Red-team persona**: catholic
- **Unverified / cautions**: Unverified: Irenaeus/Tertullian/Theophilus in their own words, Justin First Apology 10, Plato's Timaeus, CCC 296-297 and Lateran IV (vatican.va fetches returned wrong sections), Joseph Fielding Smith (0 conference hits), Brigham Young 1870/Pratts, King Follett version comparison, Joseph's 'baurau' gloss (don't present as correct), the Ostler source is partisan, FAIR page is a lead only (its second-century ex nihilo claim needs a scholarly check).

### Claim 4

- **Anchor context**: read the full 2:18 transcript; the speaker is cordial and says at 1:59 this 'isn't necessarily an argument for or against that theology'.
- **Research**: 'Can't pin them down' fails: the 2014 Gospel Topics essay 'Becoming Like God' says people are 'divine in origin, nature, and potential' and calls the doctrine central; D&C 132:20 'then shall they be gods'; Family Proclamation 'a divine nature and destiny'; Snow (1894, verified exact) 'As God now is, man may be'. Limits the Church sets: progression 'will never change His identity as our Father and our God', children 'will always worship Him', the couplet 'has never been canonized'. 'Rule a planet': no tier 1-3 source; the essay itself calls having your own planet a media caricature (search incomplete). Ontology (change of essence): no official text defines it. Catholic side: theosis is real (2 Pet 1:4; Ps 82:6/John 10:34; Rom 8:17; 1 John 3:2; CCC 460, 1265; Aquinas ST I-II Q112 A1 'a partaking of the Divine Nature'; Athanasius via Givens/Pugh), but as participation in God's energies, not essence; even the LDS-friendly handbook concedes Irenaeus and Athanasius saw God and humans as different kinds of being. Nauvoo-era deification is real (King Follett 1844; Snow couplet claimed 1840 origin).
- **Grading**: disputed: 'becoming like God' half = fact; 'leaders won't commit' half = false; planet detail unsupported as official doctrine.
- **Doctrine tier**: Tier 2 (Gospel Topics 2014/2016); tier 1 (D&C 132:20); tier 5 for the planet image (inferred, not tool-verified).
- **Adversarial Q1** — Catholic theosis (CCC 460) is becoming gods by participation, only the uncreated Trinity being God by nature; LDS teaching that the Father was once a man and that humans are the same kind of being collapses that distinction. Sharing the word 'god' doesn't show the doctrine is shared; the Fathers must be shown to teach it.
- **Adversarial Q2** — Omitted: the essay's limits (worship, Father's identity unchanged). **Turnabout (one dry line)**: CCC 460, 'the Son of God became man so that we might become God' (verified against the Archive's Catechism p. 229) means 'humans become God' can't itself be the disqualifier; the real dispute is narrower, whether the Father was ever a man.

### Claim 5

- **Anchor context**: read the full 2:18 transcript; the speaker is cordial and says at 1:59 this 'isn't necessarily an argument for or against that theology'.
- **Research**: The video's description is accurate as a summary of a leader teaching that was never canonized. Joseph Smith, King Follett Discourse 7 April 1844 (all four scribes recorded it: Bullock 'dwelt on a Earth same as J C himself did', Clayton 'was on a planet as Jesus was in the flesh', Woodruff 'was once on an earth like us', Richards omits it); the familiar 'God himself was once as we are now' comes from later composites (Times and Seasons 15 Aug 1844; Grimshaw 1855 until Larson 1978). Snow 1886 'As man now is, God once was'; Lee 1969, Ashton 1976, Tanner 1978 repeated it; Hinckley 1994 called it a 'grand and incomparable concept' that 'in no way diminishes God the Eternal Father'; Hinckley 1997 (Time, Chronicle; secondary sources only): 'I don't know that we teach it', 'more of a couplet than anything else'. 2016 Gospel Topics essay: couplet 'has never been canonized', the sermon text 'is not canonized'. 'He had his own God' is NOT in the scribal text (nearest: 'Jesus treads in the track of his Father'; 'God Himself found Himself in the midst of spirits and glory'). God 'unchangeable' (D&C 20:17) is the LDS account of immutability.
- **Grading**: fact for the 1844 teaching and its non-canonical status; speculation/unverified for 'his own God'; disputed overall.
- **Doctrine tier**: Tier 4/5, dated (1844, 1886). 'His own God': tier 5-6 at most. D&C 20:17 'unchangeable': tier 1. Never say 'the Church teaches' this without that qualifier.
- **Adversarial Q1** — Even granting 'little revealed, not emphasized', the Church has never said the first half of the couplet is false, and Apostles preached 'as man is, God once was' as late as 1978, so the reply defends the tone and leaves the metaphysical question open: was God ever not God?
- **Adversarial Q2** — The video's main description is true (escalation trigger 6 in part): only the 'own God' extension, the canonical status and the ontology framing are open to rebuttal. **Turnabout (weak, at most one dry line)**: CCC 460 and the Creed's 'was made man'; the Catholic answer is that the change is in the assumed human nature, not the divine, so the disagreement is what 'became' means, not whether the word is allowed. Near-miss on trigger 5: a beat saying 'the Church teaches God had a God' would present an inference as fact.
