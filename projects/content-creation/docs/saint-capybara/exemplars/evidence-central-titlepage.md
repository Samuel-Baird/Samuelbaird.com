## The passage

A short, structurally-focused piece: the Book of Mormon's title page is analyzed as
two parallel halves with matched sub-elements, attributed to John W. Welch's
scholarship on the text's internal design. It never claims the parallelism "proves"
anything beyond itself — the piece's own conclusion is deliberately modest, calling
the Title Page simply "a complicated text," and, separately, "meticulously
balanced." The evidence is presented, its scope is stated, and the piece stops
there.

## Take

- **State the evidence's actual scope, not its maximum implication.** "This is a
  complicated, deliberately structured text" is the claim the evidence supports; the
  piece doesn't reach for "this proves ancient authorship," which the structural
  observation alone doesn't establish.
- **Calibration as confidence, not hedging** — this is `apologetics-playbook.md`'s
  "Don't overclaim" certainty scale in practice: state exactly what the evidence
  earns (here: "demonstrated" that the text is structured; "supported," not
  "demonstrated," for what that structure implies about origin).

## Do NOT take

- The piece's pacing (build the structural case slowly, conclude softly) is a
  long-form-article rhythm; a Short's evidence beat needs the structural claim and
  its scope stated in one or two sentences, not a paragraph-by-paragraph unpacking.

## Beat translation

*"Two matching halves, point for point — that's not an accident, and calling it
'meticulously structured' isn't overselling it. It just doesn't tell you who wrote
it."*
