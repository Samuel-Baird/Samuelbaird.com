## The passage

The same Campenhausen passage used in `succession-catholic.md`, read from the other
angle: Irenaeus defends the Church's teaching "primarily with the help of Holy
Scripture" — but when gnostic teachers refuse to accept the authority of that
scripture and appeal to their own secret oral traditions instead, Irenaeus has no
scripture-alone answer available. He has to reach for the succession of teachers back
to the apostles to establish which reading of scripture is authoritative in the first
place. Campenhausen's account shows scripture and an external chain of authoritative
interpretation working together from the earliest record we have — not scripture
functioning alone, self-interpreting, self-authenticating.

## Take

- **Opening frame**: name the assumption sola scriptura actually requires — that
  scripture is self-attesting and self-interpreting without any external authority to
  settle disputed readings — before arguing about it.
- **Turnabout**: the earliest post-apostolic defender of scripture's authority
  (Irenaeus) didn't argue from scripture alone; he needed an external, authoritative
  interpretive chain to use scripture against rivals who also claimed a scriptural
  reading. If that's true of the second century, "scripture alone, from the start" is
  the harder claim to defend, not the easier one.

## Do NOT take

- This doesn't establish that the *content* of what Irenaeus appealed to (episcopal
  succession as later understood) matches the LDS concept of continuing revelation
  and prophetic authority — it establishes only that scripture-alone was never the
  operative model, which is the narrower, defensible claim.

## Beat translation

*"Scripture alone" sounds simple until you ask: alone, interpreted by whom? Even the
earliest Christians who defended the Bible against rival readings didn't argue
scripture alone — they argued scripture plus a living chain of people authorized to
say what it meant."*
