## The passage

The book's core move against "Mormons aren't Christian": name the actual (unstated)
rule the critics are using — "If we don't believe it or do it, it isn't Christian" —
then show the rule is both extrabiblical and inconsistently applied, quoting a critic
who states the rule almost that bluntly himself: any doctrine that "contradict[s]
mine" is, in his own words, "by definition not Christian." The turnabout asks by what
authority any one group gets to "bestow or deny" that title at all — then, on the
appeal to a supposed expert consensus, reaches for a historical parallel: "'Informed
opinion' in Salem knew that witchcraft was a serious community problem." The same
point is repeated for Rome's opinion of early Christians. "Truth is not established
by opinion polls," the book concludes, "and not even by surveying 'experts.'" It
closes by listing what Latter-day Saints actually do (worship, baptize, and pray in
Christ's name) rather than only rebutting the critics' claim.

## Take

- **Define the term before arguing about it.** The essay's whole argument depends on
  first naming the hidden rule ("if we don't believe it, it isn't Christian") — this
  is playbook step 2/3 (name and test the assumption) done in one clean move.
- **Turnabout by analogy, not by insult**: Salem witchcraft and Roman opinion of early
  Christians are both cases where "informed consensus" was simply wrong — the
  analogy does the work, no adjective needed.
- **Close on the positive case** (what LDS worship actually contains), not only on
  the rebuttal — this is the playbook's "positive apologetics, not just rebuttal."

## Do NOT take

- The book states this over several pages with heavy academic apparatus (footnotes,
  a dozen named critics quoted at length). A Short needs the compressed version: one
  named standard, one turnabout analogy, one line of positive case.

## Beat translation

Opening frame: *"There's one rule behind every 'Mormons aren't Christian' claim: if
we don't believe it or do it, it isn't Christian. That's not a Bible verse. That's
just a description of themselves."*

Turnabout: *"'Informed opinion' in Salem was sure about witchcraft too. Consensus
isn't evidence — it's a headcount."*
