## The passage

A satirical "rulebook" (30 rules) for writing a profitable anti-Mormon book,
illustrated with real quotations from a specific critic (Irving Wallace) and Ann Eliza
Young. Rule 1 opens: "Don't be modest!" — the critic must present himself as
uniquely qualified to judge. Rule 2 mocks a critic's own self-undermining phrasing
("The prose was marred by unremitting hysteria" — said of the source the critic then
depends on for "nearly all of his history"). Rule 8's advice is blunt: "Avoid
footnotes!" Each rule names a real rhetorical trick, quotes the critic doing it, then
lets the absurdity speak.

## Take

- **Naming the rhetorical pattern, not just the instance**, is what makes a method
  critique land beyond one video — "here's the move being made" outlives "here's one
  guy being wrong."
- **Quoting the critic against himself** (their own words revealing the trick) is
  stronger than asserting the trick exists.

## Do NOT take — this is the ceiling, not a target

- **The register is sustained mockery of named, real people**, not an isolated dry
  line about a standard. Nibley calls a critic's motives into open question across
  paragraphs, at one point asking outright whether the critic is really as interested
  in his subject as he claims. Saint Capybara's jab
  rule caps at one sentence, targets a standard or a tradition's record, and never a
  named living critic's character or motives.
- **This length and density would blow a Short's runtime and its charitable-to-people
  rule several times over.** If a script's jab starts to feel like it's building
  toward this register, that is the signal to cut it back to one line — this file is
  the wall, not a rung to climb toward.
