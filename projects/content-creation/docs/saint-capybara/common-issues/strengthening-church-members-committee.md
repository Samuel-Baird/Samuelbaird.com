# Strengthening Church Members Committee

## General response

D. Michael Quinn's *The Mormon Hierarchy: Extensions of Power* documents
a Church committee (active from the 1980s) that compiled files on
members whose public statements or writings were considered critical of
Church leadership or doctrine, sometimes forwarding information to local
leaders. State this as a documented historical practice per Quinn's
research, while noting the committee's current status and scope is not
publicly confirmed by the Church in comparable detail.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Institutional monitoring of internal critics and potential heterodoxy has
precedent in other traditions (various historical church oversight bodies
tracking clergy and theologian statements) — the more useful comparison
engages the specific practice (centralized file-compilation on ordinary
members' public statements) rather than assuming monitoring itself is
unique to LDS practice.

## Exmo/atheist response

Give the documented picture directly and note its limits: Quinn's account
remains the primary substantive source, is now decades old, and the
Church has not offered a detailed public confirmation or denial of the
committee's current activities — say plainly that current, up-to-date
information is genuinely limited, rather than asserting confidently that
the same practice continues unchanged today or has ended entirely.
