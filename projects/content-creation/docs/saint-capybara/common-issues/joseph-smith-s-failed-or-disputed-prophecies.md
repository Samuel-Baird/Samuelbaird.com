# Joseph Smith's failed or disputed prophecies

## General response

The most commonly cited case: D&C 84:4-5 (1832) states a temple "shall
be reared" in Independence, Missouri, "in this generation" — a temple
that was never built there in that timeframe due to the Saints' violent
expulsion from Jackson County shortly after. State the specific
prophecy and its actual text precisely (D&C 84:31-32 conditions this
on the Saints not being cast out, which is contested exegetically —
see below) rather than the vaguer popular framing.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Both traditions have their own history of disputed or reinterpreted
prophetic/apocalyptic expectations (early Christian expectation of an
imminent Second Coming within the apostles' lifetime, per passages like
Mark 9:1 and 1 Thessalonians 4:15-17, which generated centuries of
reinterpretation) — a genuine parallel worth raising, not a deflection,
since it shows unfulfilled-on-schedule prophetic expectation followed by
reinterpretation isn't unique to Joseph Smith.

## Exmo/atheist response

Engage the Missouri temple prophecy directly and specifically, since it's
the strongest case:

- FAIR's direct treatment, ["Alleged false prophecies of Joseph
  Smith"](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/alleged-false-prophecies-of-joseph-smith-04fe/6),
  argues the revelation's own text conditions the temple's construction
  on the Saints' obedience and that the Missouri persecution (which
  "forced a move of locations for the building of a
  temple" — [source](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/alleged-false-prophecies-of-joseph-smith-04fe/7))
  is itself what prevented fulfillment, consistent with other
  conditional prophecies in scripture (e.g., Jonah's Nineveh prophecy).
- Terryl Givens (a serious LDS scholar, not simply an apologist) treats
  the "this generation" temple revelation candidly as a real prophetic
  claim about the near future that did not come to pass as originally
  expected — [*Feeding the
  Flock*, p. 301](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/givens-feeding-the-flock-foundations-of-mormon-a395/301)
  — worth citing for its candor rather than only citing the more
  defensive FAIR treatment.

**Certainty level:** the temple was not built in Missouri in that
generation is `fact`; whether the prophecy's own conditional language
adequately accounts for that (versus reading as post-hoc rationalization)
is `disputed` — this is a genuinely contested exegetical and historical
question, and the response should reflect that rather than declaring the
conditional-prophecy defense fully dispositive.
