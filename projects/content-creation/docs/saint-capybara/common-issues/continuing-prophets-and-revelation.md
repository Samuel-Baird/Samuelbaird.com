# Continuing prophets and revelation

## General response

Amos 3:7 ("surely the Lord God will do nothing, but he revealeth his
secret unto his servants the prophets") is the standard proof text for
the *principle*; Articles of Faith 1:9 extends it forward ("He will yet
reveal many great and important things"). The LDS claim is a living
apostolic/prophetic pattern (First Presidency and Quorum of the Twelve),
not revelation as free-floating individual claim.

## Catholic response

Catholicism affirms a form of continuing guidance too (the magisterium's
ongoing teaching authority, ex cathedra papal definitions) while denying
new revelation can add doctrinal content beyond what's already implicit
in the "deposit of faith" closed at the apostles' deaths. Name that
precise distinction (continuing *interpretive* authority vs. continuing
*revelatory* authority) rather than treating Catholic and Protestant
objections as identical — the Catholic already grants the *category* of
living authoritative guidance; the dispute is its scope and source.

## Protestant response

Ask what basis restricts revelation strictly to the apostolic era rather
than being a real, if less frequent, ongoing phenomenon — many
Protestant traditions themselves affirm forms of ongoing divine guidance
(personal leading, charismatic prophecy in Pentecostal/charismatic
streams) without extending it to binding doctrinal authority. The
distinctive LDS claim isn't "revelation continues" (many Protestants
already grant some version of that) — it's that continuing revelation can
carry binding, canonizable doctrinal authority through a specific
office.

## Exmo/atheist response

The sharpest version of this objection is the correction-mechanism
problem — see `changes-in-prophetic-teachings-without-clear-mechanisms-
for-correction.md` and `prophetic-fallibility-versus-prophetic-
authority.md` for the detailed sourcing on how past prophetic statements
(race and priesthood, Adam-God, blood atonement) have been revised or
abandoned without a clear, consistently-applied process for distinguishing
binding revelation from personal opinion in real time. Route the
discussion there rather than answering the abstract claim alone — the
abstract claim isn't usually what's actually driving the objection.
