# Different understanding of original sin

## General response

LDS doctrine (2nd Article of Faith; Moses 6:54) explicitly denies
inherited guilt from Adam's transgression — people are punished for their
own sins, not Adam's. This is one of the sharpest, most textually
explicit LDS departures from mainstream Western Christian doctrine and
should be presented as such rather than softened.

## Catholic response

Augustine's doctrine of original sin as inherited guilt (not just
inherited mortality/corruption) is the specific target — engage its
actual history rather than treating it as timeless Christian consensus:

- Terryl Givens, *Wrestling the Angel*, notes "those who attribute to
  Augustine an uncompromising assertion of original sin and human
  depravity seldom
  note" the nuances/later hardening of his position
  ([p. 236](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/givens-wrestling-the-angel-foundations-of-88a3/236)).
- "Augustine was the first to add the concept of inherited guilt" within
  original sin doctrine — [Augustine of
  Hippo biography, p. 12](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/augustine-of-hippo-cd20/12); a
  non-LDS biographical source making the historical-development point
  independently.
- The Pelagian controversy itself (condemned at the Council of Carthage,
  418, and Ephesus, 431) shows this was a genuinely contested, actively
  argued doctrine in its formative period, not simply received apostolic
  teaching — Jackson, *From Apostasy to Restoration*, p. 63-64.

**Certainty level: supported.** This shows Augustine's specific formulation
of inherited *guilt* (as opposed to inherited mortality/weakness, which
LDS theology does affirm via the Fall) was a developed, contested, and
historically datable doctrine — it doesn't show the early church at large
agreed with the LDS position either. Keep both halves honest on screen.

## Protestant response

Reformed/Calvinist total depravity intensifies Augustine's inherited-guilt
doctrine further (federal headship, imputed guilt) — this is actually the
Protestant tradition with the *widest* gap from LDS doctrine on this
point, more so than Catholicism in some respects. Key text to engage:
Romans 5:12, the central proof text on both sides — the Encyclopedia of
Early Christianity notes it as "a crucial scriptural text in debates over
original sin"
([p. 1776](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/encyclopedia-of-early-christianity-2nd-edition-e1fa/1776)),
meaning the text itself has genuinely sustained multiple readings across
Christian history, not just an LDS-convenient one.

## Exmo/atheist response

The relevant angle for this audience is less the doctrine's truth than
its function: LDS rejection of inherited guilt underwrites the Church's
relatively optimistic view of human nature and salvation for those who
die without full gospel knowledge or as children — which is also the
doctrinal basis for `baptism-for-the-dead.md` and vicarious ordinance
work. If the objection is really "your soteriology seems too convenient/
universalist," engage that framing directly rather than re-arguing
Augustine.
