# Tithing requirements

## General response

A full 10% of income is required for a temple recommend (see
`temple-recommend-interviews.md`); Pew's 2011 survey found 80% of
Mormons overall report paying a full tithe, rising to over 99% among the
"very active" — [Pew, 2011 National Survey of
Mormons](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2012/01/12/mormons-in-america-executive-summary/).
State this compliance-rate data directly rather than assuming tithing is
widely resisted or resented, while still engaging the substantive
criticisms below.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Most Christian traditions teach some form of tithing as an ideal or
strong encouragement (Malachi 3:8-10 is the standard proof text across
traditions) without tying it to sacramental/temple access specifically —
the distinctive LDS feature isn't the tithing expectation itself but
gating temple recommend status (and by extension family sealing
attendance, see `eternal-marriage-and-sealing.md`) on compliance. Name
that specific structural difference precisely rather than treating
tithing itself as the point of departure.

## Exmo/atheist response

Engage the gating mechanism directly, since that's the sharper version
of this objection: tying temple access (including attending a family
member's wedding sealing) to financial compliance functions as real
social and financial pressure, particularly for lower-income members —
worth acknowledging plainly rather than only defending the doctrine of
tithing in principle. Route to `church-finances.md` for how tithing funds
are used and disclosed, since that's usually the actual substance behind
this objection when raised by this audience specifically.
