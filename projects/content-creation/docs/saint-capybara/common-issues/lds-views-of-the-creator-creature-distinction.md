# LDS views of the Creator–creature distinction

## General response

LDS theology denies an absolute ontological gulf between God and humans
— humans are the same species of being as God (spirit offspring of
divine parents, cf. Acts 17:28-29 "we are the offspring of God"), capable
of exaltation into the divine nature itself (see
`exaltation-and-humans-becoming-gods.md`), rather than remaining
permanently and categorically other than God as in classical theism.
This is the underlying premise beneath several other objections in this
folder — `god-once-being-as-humans-are-divine-progression.md`,
`rejection-of-creation-ex-nihilo.md`, and `preexistence-of-human-spirits.md`
are downstream of this one point, so treat it as the root issue when an
objection seems to be circling several of these at once.

## Catholic response

Classical theism's Creator/creature distinction is a load-bearing
metaphysical commitment (grounds analogy of being, divine simplicity,
and the impossibility of creatures becoming God by nature). Engage Blake
Ostler's *Exploring Mormon Thought* vol. 3 (*Of God and Gods*) directly —
it's the most sustained LDS philosophical argument that this distinction,
as classically formulated, generates its own unresolved problems
(relating a timeless simple being to a temporal world) rather than being
a cost-free default.

## Protestant response

Ask where the sharp ontological Creator/creature line is actually taught
as opposed to assumed from Greek metaphysics — then bring the theosis
material from `exaltation-and-humans-becoming-gods.md` (2 Peter 1:4,
Litwa, Gorman) back in: if humans can be "partakers of the divine nature"
in any real sense, the line is already less absolute than the objection
assumes.

## Exmo/atheist response

This is usually not raised as its own objection by this audience directly
— it's the philosophical substrate under other complaints ("Mormons
think they'll become gods," "Mormon God isn't really God"). When it
surfaces, name it explicitly and redirect to whichever concrete doctrine
(embodiment, progression, exaltation) is actually being objected to,
rather than arguing metaphysics in the abstract — see the playbook's
note that generic answers read weaker than targeted ones.
