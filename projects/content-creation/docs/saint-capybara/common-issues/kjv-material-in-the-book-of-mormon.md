# KJV material in the Book of Mormon

## General response

Extended Isaiah quotations in 2 Nephi (and other biblical passages
throughout) often match the King James Version wording closely, including
KJV-specific translation choices and even 1769 KJV edition-specific
readings — Jeremy Runnells' *CES Letter* raises this directly: "what are
1769 King James Version edition errors doing in the Book of
Mormon?" ([source](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/runnells-ces-letter-my-search-for-answers-to-my-3058/9)).
State the observation plainly — it's a documented textual fact, not a
disputed allegation.

## Catholic and Protestant response

The LDS-scholarly response engages this at the level of translation
theory rather than disputing the textual overlap: Royal Skousen's
critical text work (*Analysis of Textual Variants of the Book of
Mormon*) documents how the KJV was used as a base text during dictation
for passages Joseph Smith understood to closely parallel existing
biblical text, with the Prophet correcting/adjusting from it rather than
receiving the English wording independently word-for-word throughout —
[source](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/1-1-nephi-1-2-nephi-10-15cd/591).
This reframes translation as functional/dynamic rather than a
literalistic, mechanical rendering — a defensible model of translation
generally, worth explaining rather than assuming the audience already
grants it.

## Exmo/atheist response

Give the fullest, most direct treatment here, since this is one of the
better-documented and harder-to-wave-away textual objections:

- FAIR's dedicated analysis, ["KJV translation errors in the Book of
  Mormon"](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/kjv-translation-errors-in-the-book-of-mormon-92c1/2),
  works through specific claimed cases and separates genuine KJV-specific
  errors from cases that aren't actually translation errors, or are
  independently-attested ancient readings that happen to also appear in
  the Book of Mormon (p. 7's point that "many of the Book of Mormon's
  translation variants of Isaiah have ancient
  support" is the strongest counter-argument and should be led with, not
  buried).
- Some KJV-specific wording (including italicized KJV translator
  insertions and at least one 1769-edition-specific reading) does appear
  to have been carried into the Book of Mormon text, and that is exactly
  what Skousen's base-text model predicts: it is difficult to explain
  under a model where Joseph Smith is reading an independent ancient
  text letter for letter, and easy to explain under a model where he had
  significant latitude in rendering wording for passages already
  familiar to him from the Bible. The critic's objection only works
  against the letter-for-letter model, which is not the one the
  scholarship defends.

**Certainty level: fact** for the textual overlap itself; **disputed**
for what it implies about the translation process. Keep those separate
on screen.
