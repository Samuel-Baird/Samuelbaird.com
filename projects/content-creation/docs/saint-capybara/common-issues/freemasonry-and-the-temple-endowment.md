# Freemasonry and the temple endowment

## General response

Don't dispute the well-documented history — engage it directly. Joseph
Smith applied for and received Masonic membership in the Nauvoo lodge in
December 1841, and the Nauvoo endowment was introduced roughly six weeks
later, with documented similarities in structure and symbolism —
Richard Bushman, *Joseph Smith: Rough Stone Rolling*, notes "the Nauvoo
endowment was first bestowed just six weeks after Joseph's induction. The
similarities were..." [obvious to contemporaries](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/bushman-joseph-smith-rough-stone-rolling-bb34/621).
This is `fact`-graded, not disputed, and shouldn't be treated as a "gotcha"
to deflect.

## Catholic and Protestant response

The relevant theological question isn't "did Joseph borrow ceremonial
forms" (he plainly did, in part) but "does borrowing form invalidate
revealed content" — most religious ritual borrows and adapts existing
cultural forms (early Christian liturgy adapted Jewish synagogue and
Greco-Roman forms; Catholic sacramental vestments and architecture draw
on Roman civic and imperial forms). High-level sourcing on the
LDS-Masonry relationship specifically, treated seriously rather than
defensively:

- Michael Homer's scholarship and Cheryl Bruno, Joe Steve Swick III, and
  Nicholas Literski, *Method Infinite: Freemasonry and the Mormon
  Restoration* — the most thorough recent academic treatment.
- Jeffrey E. Bradshaw, *Freemasonry and the Origins of Latter-day Saint
  Temple Ordinances* — an LDS-affiliated scholar directly engaging the
  question of why Joseph Smith encouraged Masonic involvement in Nauvoo
  ([source](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/bradshaw-freemasonry-and-the-origins-of-latter-ec95/54)).
- Bushman also notes counter-arguments that architectural/structural
  similarities have been overstated by some critics
  ([p. 943](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/bushman-joseph-smith-rough-stone-rolling-bb34/943))
  — cite both the concession and the pushback, don't cherry-pick one
  side.

## Exmo/atheist response

This is one of the more evidentially solid critical points in the whole
canon of LDS objections — treat it that way rather than minimizing.
Concede fully: the timeline (initiation → endowment six weeks later) and
structural parallels are well documented and not seriously disputed by
faithful LDS historians either (Bushman himself, a faithful LDS
biographer, states it plainly). The actual disagreement is entirely at
the level of interpretation — "borrowed ritual form used to convey
revealed content" versus "wholesale invention with religious packaging" —
not at the level of the underlying facts. Keep that distinction explicit
on screen rather than contesting facts that aren't actually in dispute.
