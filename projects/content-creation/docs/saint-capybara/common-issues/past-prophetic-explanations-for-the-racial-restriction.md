# Past prophetic explanations for the racial restriction

## General response

Multiple, mutually distinct theological explanations were offered by
different Church leaders over more than a century to justify the
priesthood/temple restriction (see `race-and-the-priesthood-temple-
restriction.md`) — "curse of Cain," "curse of Ham," and premortal
"fence-sitting" in the War in Heaven were all articulated at various
points by leaders speaking as though delivering settled doctrine. The
2013 Gospel Topics Essay explicitly and formally disavows all of these
explanations as theories without scriptural or doctrinal foundation.
State this plainly rather than only citing the disavowal without naming
what specifically was disavowed.

## Catholic and Protestant response

The relevant theological question for both traditions: what does it mean
for prophetic authority when multiple leaders, speaking with apparent
confidence and apparent doctrinal authority, offer theological
justifications that a later prophetic body formally repudiates as
baseless? Engage this directly as a real problem for models of prophetic
inerrancy specifically, while distinguishing it from a broader claim
against the possibility of prophetic authority as such — see
`prophetic-fallibility-versus-prophetic-authority.md` for the fuller
treatment of that distinction.

## Exmo/atheist response

Give the direct documentary record without softening:

- A 1978-era example of a leader personally explaining the premortal
  "fence-sitters" theory directly to a Black member is documented in
  Dialogue: "We tried to explain how he and his people had been
  fence-sitters in the
  premortal[existence]" ([source](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/v51n02-051d/43))
  — a striking, specific primary account worth citing directly rather
  than only discussing the theory in the abstract.
- Matthew Harris's *Second-Class Saints* documents the specific
  chronology of when and by whom each theory was articulated, and how
  they sometimes conflicted with each other even among contemporaries.

**Certainty level:** that these specific theories were taught by general
authorities as though authoritative, and were later formally disavowed,
is `fact`, stated by the Church's own current materials. Whether that
pattern is compatible with a strong model of prophetic inerrancy is a
`disputed` theological question this file and
`prophetic-fallibility-versus-prophetic-authority.md` should both engage
honestly rather than resolve by assertion.
