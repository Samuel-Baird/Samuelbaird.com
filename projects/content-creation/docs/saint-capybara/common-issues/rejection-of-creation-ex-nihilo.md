# Rejection of creation ex nihilo

## General response

LDS doctrine holds that matter (and intelligence/spirit) is eternal and
uncreated — God organized existing matter rather than creating from
absolute nothing (Abraham 3:24, "we will go down... and we will organize
them"). Frame the assumption-test directly: the objection assumes creatio
ex nihilo is the biblically required position, which is exactly the
premise in dispute.

## Catholic response

Creatio ex nihilo is defined Catholic dogma (Fourth Lateran Council,
1215) — don't understate how load-bearing it is to Catholic theology
(grounds divine sovereignty, aseity, and the creature/Creator
distinction). Engage the historical-development argument rather than
just asserting Genesis 1 is ambiguous:

- Anderson & Bockmuehl (eds.), *Creation Ex Nihilo: Origins, Development,
  Contemporary Challenges* — the standard modern academic volume; even
  contributors sympathetic to the doctrine concede its full formulation
  is post-biblical: "most contemporary biblical scholars recognize that
  the formulation of the Christian doctrine of creatio ex nihilo..." [is
  a later development](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/anderson-and-bockemehl-creation-ex-nihilo-a2a3/64)
  (p. 64), while also noting the doctrine is "certainly biblical" even if
  not verbally present (p. 49) — cite both halves; don't quote-mine only
  the half that helps.
- Genesis 1:2's "formless and void" (tohu wa-bohu) language, read by many
  scholars as describing pre-existing unformed material rather than
  absolute nothingness — this is the actual exegetical crux, not a side
  issue.

## Protestant response

Same sola scriptura challenge pattern as the other Godhead topics: ask
for the specific text that teaches creation from *absolute* nothing,
rather than divine ordering/organizing of chaotic pre-existing material.
FAIR's [collected treatment](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/creation-out-of-nothing-creatio-ex-nihilo-4e6f/14)
notes serious non-LDS scholarship agreeing "there is no question here of
a creatio ex nihilo" in the primary Genesis text itself — a useful
non-LDS-sourced concession per the source-hierarchy rule.

## Exmo/atheist response

This topic rarely lands as a standalone objection for this audience — it
mostly surfaces as one data point in a larger "LDS theology is just
19th-century American folk metaphysics" argument. Treat it that way:
the fact that Joseph Smith's cosmology (eternal matter, organized rather
than created) fits recognizable currents in early 19th-century American
religious thought shows only that it has a milieu, which every
tradition's theology has — Nicene creatio ex nihilo has its own
identifiable philosophical milieu (Middle Platonism/Aristotelian
metaphysics) — and fitting a historical milieu doesn't by itself make a
claim false.
