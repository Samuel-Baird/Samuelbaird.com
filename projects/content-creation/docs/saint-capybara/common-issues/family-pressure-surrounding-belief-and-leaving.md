# Family pressure surrounding belief and leaving

## General response

Eternal family/sealing theology (see `eternal-marriage-and-sealing.md`)
raises the emotional and relational stakes of belief divergence within
LDS families higher than in many other traditions — a family member's
faith transition can be experienced as threatening eternal, not just
present, family bonds. Acknowledge this directly as a real structural
feature of the theology, not merely a matter of individual family
members handling things poorly.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Distinguish two separate things worth naming precisely: the *theology*
of eternal sealing (a genuine doctrinal claim, defensible or not on its
own terms) versus the *pastoral/relational fallout* when that theology is
applied punitively or fear-based within specific families — the second
is a real critique of practice and culture, not simply of doctrine, and
deserves separate acknowledgment.

## Exmo/atheist response

This is a place for empathy first, argument second. Acknowledge plainly:
many people report significant family strain, guilt, and grief connected
to leaving, including from well-meaning family members whose own fear (of
eternal separation, per the sealing theology) can manifest as pressure or
distancing. Church-level resources exist encouraging continued love and
relationship regardless of belief status (recent General Conference
messaging has explicitly urged members not to distance from family who
leave), but individual family and local-culture behavior often falls
short of that messaging — name both the official guidance and the real
gap between it and lived experience, rather than citing only one side.
