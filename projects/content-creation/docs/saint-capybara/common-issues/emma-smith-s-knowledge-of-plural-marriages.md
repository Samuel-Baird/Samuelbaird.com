# Emma Smith's knowledge of plural marriages

## General response

Emma Smith's relationship to plural marriage was complex and shifted over
time — periods of apparent acceptance/participation in approving some
marriages, alternating with strong, documented opposition and confrontations
with Joseph — Richard Bushman, *Rough Stone Rolling*, notes Emma's
children grew up with "little knowledge of their father's teachings —
and none about plural
marriage" ([p. 754](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/bushman-joseph-smith-rough-stone-rolling-bb34/754)),
indicating how compartmentalized even family knowledge was. John Turner's
*The Mormon Jesus* identifies Emma's "growing opposition to her husband's
plural marriages" as itself a significant pressure on the Nauvoo
community ([p. 113](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/turner-the-mormon-jesus-a-biography-9a9c/113)).

## Catholic and Protestant response

The relevant question for both audiences: what does a founding prophet's
own spouse's documented opposition and apparent deception say about the
claimed source of the practice? Engage directly rather than assuming
Emma's later public denials (she denied Joseph practiced polygamy at all,
for the rest of her life after his death) settle the question either way
— her documented private knowledge and involvement, and her later public
denial, are in tension and both deserve acknowledgment.

## Exmo/atheist response

Give the specific documented complexity rather than a simplified
narrative in either direction:

- Some accounts describe Emma approving specific marriages (including,
  by some accounts, selecting wives herself at points) before later
  withdrawing that approval and opposing the practice more forcefully —
  Leonard, *Nauvoo: A Place of Peace, a People of Promise*, [p.
  376](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/leonard-nauvoo-a-place-of-peace-a-people-of-5ae1/376).
- Bruno's *Secret Covenants* notes candidly that "the possibility that
  Emma never fully" accepted or was told the full extent of the practice
  remains live in the sources
  ([p. 211](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/bruno-secret-covenants-new-insights-on-early-fcb7/211)).
- Emma's later public position (as RLDS Church president, she denied to
  her death that Joseph practiced polygamy at all) is now understood by
  essentially all serious historians, faithful and critical alike, as
  factually incorrect — a rare point of full historiographical
  consensus worth naming directly rather than avoiding out of deference
  to Emma's memory.

**Certainty level:** Joseph practiced polygamy and Emma had significant
(though incomplete and shifting) knowledge of it is `fact`, per
essentially unanimous current historical consensus; the precise
timeline and extent of what she knew and approved at each point is
`disputed` in the primary sources themselves.
