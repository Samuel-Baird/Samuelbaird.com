# Gender hierarchy in Church leadership

## General response

All top governing bodies (First Presidency, Quorum of the Twelve, area
authorities) are exclusively male; women's organizational leadership
(Relief Society, Young Women, Primary general presidencies) does not sit
within the priesthood governance structure directly, though recent
changes (women's participation in ward and stake councils, women
witnessing baptisms and temple ordinances since 2019) have somewhat
narrowed practical gaps without changing ordination itself. State the
current, specific structure accurately rather than a dated or
oversimplified picture.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Route the ordination-specific theology to `women-and-priesthood.md`. This
file's distinct angle: governance/decision-making authority specifically
— ask whether a tradition's own governance structure (all-male
magisterium and college of cardinals in Catholicism; varying by
denomination in Protestantism) faces the same critique, and engage
honestly rather than assuming LDS structure is uniquely hierarchical
along gender lines.

## Exmo/atheist response

Name the practical asymmetry directly: final doctrinal and administrative
authority rests entirely with male leadership bodies, and women's
influence on decisions, however real informally, has no formal structural
parity. Recent changes (2019 temple/ordinance changes, women in councils)
are real and worth citing as genuine, incremental change rather than
either dismissing them as meaningless or treating them as having resolved
the underlying structural asymmetry.
