# Rejection of justification by faith alone

## General response

2 Nephi 25:23 ("for we know that it is by grace that we are saved, after
all we can do") is the standard anchor — grace-enabled, works-inclusive
salvation, not earned salvation. Frame precisely: LDS theology affirms
grace as necessary and primary, and denies faith-alone (sola fide) as
sufficient by itself, which is a different, narrower claim than "LDS
denies grace" — don't let the objection collapse those two claims.

## Catholic response

Catholic soteriology also formally rejects sola fide (Council of Trent,
Session 6) in favor of faith formed by love and cooperant grace — genuine
common ground to name explicitly before drawing the LDS-Catholic
distinction (nature of merit, sacramental economy, degree of human
cooperation).

## Protestant response

Don't just proof-text James 2:24 back — engage Paul's actual target.
Ask: why should Paul's polemic against "works of the law" (in Galatians/
Romans, aimed specifically at Judaizing requirements like circumcision
and dietary law) be generalized into a universal opposition between grace
and *all* covenantal obedience, including baptism, ordinances, and
enduring faithfulness? High-level sourcing on the actual exegetical
debate:

- The classic faith-alone prooftext debate is treated directly in
  commentaries on James: "the first justification is based on faith
  alone; the second, however, is based on
  works" [(Pillar NT Commentary, James, p. 59)](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/james-54de/59)
  — shows mainstream (non-LDS) scholarship already recognizes Paul and
  James use "justification" in different senses, which is exactly the
  kind of nuance a flat sola fide reading misses.
- On Galatians specifically, interpreters have "occasionally questioned"
  whether "works of the law" in Paul means the same thing as "good works"
  generally — [Baker Exegetical Commentary on Galatians, p.
  195](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/galatians-7a99/195) — a live,
  non-LDS academic debate, not an LDS-invented dodge.

Then give the positive LDS case: covenant, ordinance, and enduring
faithfulness as the *form* grace-enabled salvation takes, not a
competing payment for salvation alongside grace.

## Exmo/atheist response

This rarely surfaces as a direct objection from this audience — when it
does, it's usually repackaged as "Mormonism is a works-based, checklist
religion" (temple recommend worthiness, tithing, callings). Route to
`worthiness-interviews.md`, `temple-recommend-interviews.md`, and
`high-demand-religion-control-criticisms.md` for that version rather than
re-running the Reformation-era theological argument, which isn't what's
actually being objected to.
