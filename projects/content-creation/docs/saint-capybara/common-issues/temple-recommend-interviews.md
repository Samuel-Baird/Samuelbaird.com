# Temple recommend interviews

## General response

A temple recommend requires a periodic interview with a bishop and
member of the stake presidency, covering testimony, tithing compliance,
Word of Wisdom observance, sexual morality, and support for Church
leadership. Route the specific youth-interview controversy to
`worthiness-interviews.md`; this file covers the general adult-recommend
process.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Compare to sacramental confession's role as a gate to communion in
Catholic practice, and to church-membership/good-standing requirements in
many Protestant congregations (some require a public profession or
pastoral interview before communion or baptism) — the general pattern of
gating sacred participation on a leader's assessment of a member's
standing isn't unique to LDS practice, though the specific breadth of
topics covered (financial, dietary, sexual, political-loyalty-adjacent
"sustaining leaders" questions together) is unusually comprehensive
compared to most single-tradition equivalents.

## Exmo/atheist response

Engage the specific structural criticism directly: because recommend
status gates attendance at family members' temple weddings (see
`eternal-marriage-and-sealing.md`), a member who cannot or will not meet
the interview's requirements can be formally excluded from a close family
member's wedding ceremony — a frequently cited, concrete example of how
doctrinal/behavioral requirements translate into real family and social
consequences. The Church has partially addressed this specific pain
point by permitting a period after a temple sealing where a civil
ceremony can be held first, allowing non-recommend-holding family to
attend that portion — cite this concrete policy accommodation directly
as a real (if partial) institutional response to the criticism, rather
than presenting the underlying tension as fully unaddressed.
