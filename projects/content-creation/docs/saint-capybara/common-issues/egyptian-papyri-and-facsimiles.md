# Egyptian papyri and facsimiles

## General response

The three facsimiles printed with the Book of Abraham are reproductions
of vignettes from the actual papyri Joseph Smith owned; Egyptologists
identify their original context and meaning as standard Egyptian funerary
imagery (e.g. Facsimile 1 as an embalming scene), differing substantially
from Joseph Smith's published explanations of the figures. Route the
translation-theory question itself to `book-of-abraham-translation.md` —
this file covers the facsimiles specifically.

## Catholic and Protestant response

The facsimiles raise the same "literal philological accuracy vs.
revelatory reinterpretation of a physical artifact" question as the
translation broadly — most productively discussed together with that
file rather than as a separate theological dispute.

## Exmo/atheist response

Be specific and direct about Facsimile 1 in particular, since it's the
most-discussed: FAIR itself walks through "interesting parallels to
Facsimile 1 from the Book of Abraham" found in comparable Egyptian
vignettes elsewhere, while conceding the standard Egyptological reading
differs from Joseph Smith's captions
([source](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/book-of-abraham-201-papyri-revelation-and-6ad4/13)).
Also note the restoration/reconstruction history is itself contested —
missing portions of Facsimile 1 were reconstructed by a later engraver
based on other Egyptian sources, and critics and defenders dispute how
accurate that 19th-century reconstruction was — a genuinely separate
question from what Joseph Smith's captions claim about the completed
image, and worth distinguishing on screen (reconstruction accuracy is
`disputed` on different grounds than caption accuracy).
