# Additional scripture beyond the Bible

## General response

Book of Mormon, D&C, Pearl of Great Price stand alongside the Bible as
canonized scripture (Eighth Article of Faith affirms the Bible "as far as
it is translated correctly," then adds the Book of Mormon without that
qualifier). Frame the assumption directly: the objection assumes the
biblical canon is closed by divine decree rather than by a historical,
human process of selection — that process is the actual subject to
examine, not simply assume closed.

## Catholic response

Catholicism itself has a larger canon than most Protestant Bibles
(deuterocanonical/apocryphal books) and holds tradition alongside
scripture as authoritative — so the objection "you added books" cannot be
run by a Catholic without qualification. The live disagreement is about
which additional authoritative material is legitimate, not whether any
exists.

## Protestant response

Push the historical-canon question: the New Testament canon itself wasn't
finally, universally fixed until centuries after the apostles (Athanasius'
367 Easter Letter is the first surviving list matching the current 27
books; full uniform reception took longer still) — see Lee McDonald, *The
Formation of the Biblical Canon* vol. 2, on how gradual and contested
that process was even for material *already* claimed as apostolic
(["mid-second century after the eyewitnesses...were
gone"](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/mcdonald-the-formation-of-the-biblical-canon-29cb/35)).
If a centuries-long human process was needed to recognize the existing
canon, "the canon is closed and can never be added to" needs its own
positive argument, not an assumption.

## Exmo/atheist response

The more effective challenge from this audience is usually textual/
historical (Book of Mormon anachronisms, KJV borrowings, 19th-century
influences — routed to those specific files) rather than the abstract
"why do you have more scripture" question, which mostly lands as a
theological in-house dispute this audience doesn't have a stake in.
Redirect quickly to the concrete evidentiary question actually being
raised.
