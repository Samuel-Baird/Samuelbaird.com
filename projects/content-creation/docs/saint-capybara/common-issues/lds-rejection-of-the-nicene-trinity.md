# LDS rejection of the Nicene Trinity

## General response

LDS teaching is that the Godhead is three separate, distinct beings —
Father, Son, and Holy Ghost — unified in purpose but not in substance.
This is stated plainly and repeatedly in modern General Conference
teaching, not just implied:

- "As indicated, they are three separate beings, but they are one in
  purpose." — N. Eldon Tanner, ["A Basis for Faith in the Living
  God"](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/1978-10-tanner-a-basis-for-faith-in-the-living-god/#p25)
  (1978)
- "We testify that these three members of the Godhead are three separate
  and distinct beings." — Dallin H. Oaks, ["Apostasy and
  Restoration"](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/1995-04-oaks-apostasy-and-restoration/#p5)
  (1995)
- "The Godhead is three separate and distinct beings, unified in
  purpose." — Robert D. Hales, ["Being a More Christian
  Christian"](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/2012-10-hales-being-a-more-christian-christian/#p6)
  (2012)
- "We know that the three members of the Godhead are separate and
  distinct beings." — Dallin H. Oaks, ["The Godhead and the Plan of
  Salvation"](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/2017-04-oaks-the-godhead-and-the-plan-of-salvation/#p6)
  (2017)

Assumption to test (playbook step 3): the objection assumes that
"Christian" requires affirming the specific 4th/5th-century philosophical
formulation (one *ousia*, three *hypostases*) rather than affirming the
biblical Father, Son, and Holy Ghost as such. That assumption is itself
the thing under dispute, not a starting point either side gets to assume.

## Catholic response

Don't reduce this to "Catholics added extra philosophy" — that's the weak
version of the argument and an informed Catholic will have an answer
ready. The stronger, historically defensible LDS move is to **distinguish
"this existed early" from "this existed from the apostles in the later,
fully creedal form"** (see `apologetics-playbook.md`'s Catholic section).

Pre-Nicene Christian writers did not uniformly hold the fully developed
homoousion Trinity later defined at Nicaea (325) and Constantinople (381):

- J.N.D. Kelly (a standard patristics reference) describes pre-Nicene
  fathers' subordinationist tendencies in describing the divine Spirit/
  Logos — [Kelly, *Early Christian
  Doctrines*, p. 116](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/kelly-early-christian-doctrines-1b9b/116).
- "In the pre-Nicene period these two terms were used interchangeably and
  would not be distinguished [in the later technical sense]" — Young,
  [*Standing Apart: Mormon Historical Consciousness and the Great
  Apostasy*, p. 230](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/young-standing-apart-mormon-historical-ef42/230).
- Many pre-Nicene writers "referred to Jesus as a 'second God'" — Barry
  Bickmore, [*Restoring the Ancient Church: Joseph Smith and Early
  Christianity*, p. 60](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/bickmore-restoring-the-ancient-church-joseph-f5b1/60).

**Certainty level: supported, not demonstrated.** This shows the
theological landscape before Nicaea was doctrinally broader and less
settled than later creedal boundaries suggest — it does not show "the
early church believed exactly what Latter-day Saints believe." Don't
overclaim that equivalence on screen (see playbook "Don't overclaim").

**Argue on the authority frame, not sola scriptura:** Catholicism's
premise that Christianity isn't just private interpretation of a text,
that doctrine develops under real authority claims, is a premise LDS
theology shares, which moves the actual dispute to *whether Rome
correctly continued apostolic authority*, not whether authority matters
at all. That's a stronger frame for LDS apologetics than defending sola
scriptura would be.

## Protestant response

Don't just proof-text back — attack the framework. Ask directly: **where
does the Bible state, in its own language, that God is one being/essence
in three persons**, using *ousia*/*hypostasis*-type categories, rather
than three persons who are one in will and purpose? The technical
vocabulary (*homoousios*, "one substance") is 4th-century conciliar
language, not biblical language — the question for a sola scriptura
Protestant is why that later technical resolution should be read back
into the text as required orthodoxy.

Then give the positive LDS case, not just the rebuttal: the biblical
pattern of Father and Son as distinct persons who pray to one another,
are baptismally distinguished (Matthew 3:16-17), and are together with
the Spirit "one" in the same relational sense the New Testament uses for
believers' unity (John 17:20-22) is itself a coherent reading — arguably
the more natural one before later technical vocabulary is imported.

## Exmo/atheist response

This audience's version of the objection is usually framed as "Mormons
worship a different Jesus" or "Mormons aren't really Christian" — a
boundary/definition claim more than a metaphysics claim. Address the
trust/definition move directly rather than re-running the philosophical
argument: who gets to stipulate that Nicene trinitarianism is the
necessary and sufficient condition for the word "Christian," and on what
authority?

- "The true Christian doctrine that the Godhead consists of three
  separate beings was known in biblical times" — Gary J. Coleman, ["Mom,
  Are We
  Christians?"](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/2007-04-coleman-mom-are-we-christians/#p4)
  (2007), which also states plainly that the Trinity formula emerged
  later "where men declared that instead of three separate beings, the
  Godhead was three persons in one God" — see [paragraph
  5](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/2007-04-coleman-mom-are-we-christians/#p5).

For an atheist specifically, the Trinity dispute is rarely the load-
bearing objection — it's usually a proxy for a broader claim that LDS
doctrine is *ad hoc* or invented wholesale. The more effective response
is often to redirect to whichever underlying issue (continuing
revelation, doctrinal development, epistemology) is actually driving the
objection, rather than re-litigating patristic Christology with someone
who doesn't accept any of the tradition's authority claims to begin with.
