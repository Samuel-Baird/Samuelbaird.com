# Preexistence of human spirits

## General response

Abraham 3:22-23 ("Now the Lord had shown unto me... the intelligences
that were organized before the world was") and Jeremiah 1:5 ("before I
formed thee in the belly I knew thee") are the standard anchors. LDS
doctrine holds all human spirits existed as literal spirit offspring of
Heavenly Parents before mortal birth — connects directly to
`god-once-being-as-humans-are-divine-progression.md` and
`exaltation-and-humans-becoming-gods.md`'s framework of eternal,
progressing intelligence.

## Catholic and Protestant response

This is not an LDS-invented idea historically, even though most orthodox
Christian traditions reject it (condemned specifically at the Second
Council of Constantinople, 553, targeting Origenism):

- Origen (3rd century) taught a real doctrine of premortal souls making
  moral choices before embodiment —
  [Anchor Bible Dictionary, "the doctrine of the preexistence of souls
  was Origen"](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/6-si-z-2785/201).
- Terryl Givens's *When Souls Had Wings: Premortal Existence in Western
  Thought* (Oxford) is the definitive scholarly history of this idea
  across Jewish, Greek, and Christian thought — not an apologetic tract,
  a serious academic monograph, useful precisely because it treats the
  idea's history evenhandedly rather than arguing for LDS doctrine
  specifically.
- BYU Studies notes evidence for "the real premortal existence of human
  souls" drawn from ["Jewish and early Jewish Christian apocalyptic
  literature"](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/62-1-e672/53) — i.e., this
  wasn't only a Greek philosophical import, it has plausible Second
  Temple Jewish roots too.

**Certainty level: supported.** The idea has real, documented pre-LDS
history across multiple traditions; it does not follow that the specific
LDS content (spirit offspring of literal divine parents, a full plan of
salvation before birth) matches Origen's or any earlier version exactly —
say so rather than implying continuity of content, only of the broader
concept.

## Exmo/atheist response

Not usually pressed hard as an independent objection by this audience
except as one more data point supporting "Joseph Smith synthesized ideas
already circulating in 19th-century America" — a fair historical
observation. The stronger response leans on Givens's material above: the
idea's *deep* history (back through Origen to Second Temple Judaism)
undercuts the specific claim that this was purely a product of Joseph
Smith's immediate 1830s-40s environment, even if his particular framing
of it plainly was.
