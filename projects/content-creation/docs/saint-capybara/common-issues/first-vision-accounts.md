# First Vision accounts

## General response

Four primary accounts survive from Joseph Smith himself (1832, 1835,
1838, 1842), plus several secondhand accounts — Steven Harper, *Joseph
Smith's First Vision: A Guide to the Historical Accounts*, and *First
Vision: Memory and Mormon Origins*, are the standard scholarly treatments
(non-polemical, published by Deseret Book/Maxwell Institute). The
canonized 1838 account (JS-H) is the one most members know; the earlier
1832 account differs in some emphasis and detail. See
`differences-among-first-vision-accounts.md` for the specific
discrepancies and their treatment.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Multiple, evolving accounts of a single formative religious experience,
recorded years apart with differing emphasis, is a common feature of
religious autobiography generally (compare Paul's three differing
accounts of his Damascus road experience in Acts 9, 22, and 26) — a
useful, genuinely parallel case to raise rather than only an analogy of
convenience, since Acts itself preserves the variation without treating
it as disqualifying.

## Exmo/atheist response

Route the specific discrepancies (who appeared, the sequence, the stated
motive for praying) to `differences-among-first-vision-accounts.md` for
full sourcing — this file's job is orienting to the account landscape,
not re-litigating each variant. The honest framing across both files: the
existence of multiple, non-identical accounts recorded over Joseph
Smith's life is a documented fact the Church itself now publishes and
discusses openly (Gospel Topics Essays, Joseph Smith Papers Project) —
FAIR's dedicated page on this exists precisely because it's a
well-known, frequently-raised point, not a suppressed one.
