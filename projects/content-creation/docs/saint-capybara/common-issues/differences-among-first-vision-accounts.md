# Differences among First Vision accounts

## General response

The 1832 account (Joseph Smith's earliest, in his own hand) emphasizes
personal forgiveness of sins and describes seeing "the Lord" — read by
some as referring to one divine personage — while the canonized 1838
account describes two distinct personages, the Father and the Son,
appearing together, and centers on which church to join rather than
personal forgiveness. State the actual textual difference precisely
rather than vaguely; FAIR's ["Differences in First Vision
accounts"](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/differences-in-first-vision-accounts-7f4f/50)
compares the 1832 and 1838 texts directly.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Route to `first-vision-accounts.md`'s Acts 9/22/26 comparison — differing
emphasis and detail across retellings of the same event, separated by
years, is not unique to Joseph Smith and doesn't automatically indicate
fabrication in either case.

## Exmo/atheist response

Give the direct, non-defensive account of the strongest version of this
objection:

- Oliver Cowdery had access to and used the 1832 account's language when
  he wrote a published history series, meaning the "two personages"
  detail wasn't simply absent from early tradition by oversight — the
  1832 wording itself doesn't mention two separate beings the way 1838
  does — [FAIR discussion,
  p. 48](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/differences-in-first-vision-accounts-7f4f/48).
- Richard Bushman (a serious, non-polemical LDS historian) is candid that
  "differences in the accounts were" real and worth taking seriously as
  historical development, not just apologetic noise — cited in BYU
  Studies 59-2 and Harper's *First Vision: Memory and Mormon Origins*
  ([p. 262](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/harper-first-vision-memory-and-mormon-origins-2426/262)).
- The strongest LDS response is Harper's memory-and-narrative-development
  framework: personal memory of formative events genuinely does shift
  emphasis over retellings depending on audience and purpose, without
  that requiring the underlying event be fabricated — this is a real
  finding in memory research generally, not an ad hoc excuse invented for
  this case specifically.

**Certainty level:** the textual differences are `fact`; whether they
indicate a real but evolving/refined memory of one experience versus
invention is `disputed` — keep the two claims separate and don't let the
first do the work of settling the second.
