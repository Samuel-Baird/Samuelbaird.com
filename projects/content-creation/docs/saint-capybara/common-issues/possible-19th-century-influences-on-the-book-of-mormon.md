# Possible 19th-century influences on the Book of Mormon

## General response

Critics propose the Book of Mormon reflects 19th-century American
religious and cultural concerns (anti-Masonry, Native American origin
theories, revivalist religious debates, View of the Hebrews-style
Israelite-origin speculation) rather than an ancient American record.
State the strongest form of this argument (parallels to Ethan Smith's
*View of the Hebrews* and Solomon Spalding theories are the two most
substantial versions historically proposed) before responding.

## Catholic and Protestant response

The relevant comparison: virtually every scripture-producing figure
writes using the conceptual vocabulary and concerns of their own era —
this doesn't by itself disprove revelatory content, though it's a
legitimate methodological question for evaluating any claimed
revelation, applied consistently. Ask what standard of "not reflecting
the author's own era at all" is being demanded, and whether any
scriptural text, including the objector's own, could meet it.

## Exmo/atheist response

Engage the specific historical arguments directly and honestly:

- The *View of the Hebrews* parallel (a popular 1823 book proposing
  Native Americans descended from Israelites) has been argued at length
  by both critics (B.H. Roberts himself, in an internal, later-published
  1985 study, raised serious questions about whether Joseph Smith could
  have been unaware of it) and LDS scholars disputing the extent of
  actual textual dependency — cite B.H. Roberts's own internal critical
  study directly, since a faithful, senior LDS leader taking the
  parallel seriously is a stronger and more surprising citation than a
  critic's version of the same argument.
- The Spalding-Rigdon theory (a discarded manuscript by Solomon Spalding
  as the Book of Mormon's true source, mediated through Sidney Rigdon)
  has much less current scholarly support, including among most
  critical historians, and shouldn't be presented as equally strong to
  the View of the Hebrews parallel — be precise about which theories
  currently carry more evidentiary weight rather than treating "19th-
  century influence theories" as one undifferentiated bucket.

**Certainty level:** thematic and cultural parallels to 19th-century
American religious concerns are `supported`; direct textual dependency
sufficient to explain authorship is `disputed`, with serious scholars
(including B.H. Roberts, from inside the faith) taking the question
seriously without it being considered settled either way.
