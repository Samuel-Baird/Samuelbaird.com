# Plurality of gods

## General response

Boyd K. Packer states plainly that LDS theology "teaches a plurality of
Gods" —
[1984](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/1984-10-packer-the-pattern-of-our-parentage/#p69).
Orson Pratt taught it extensively in the 19th century, including an
argument from the Hebrew plural *Elohim* —
[1867](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/1867-10-pratt-temples-in-ancient-america-the-god-of-mankind/#p16).
Key distinction to make immediately on screen: LDS plurality-of-Gods
teaching is not polytheism in the classical sense of competing,
independent deities — it describes an ordered, unified divine council
under the Father, whom Latter-day Saints worship exclusively (monolatry),
not a pantheon among which worship is distributed.

## Catholic response

Catholic monotheism rests on strict numerical unity of the divine
substance/essence. Rather than defending polytheism, reframe: LDS
theology is closer to a form of divine-council monotheism found in Second
Temple Judaism and the Hebrew Bible itself (Psalm 82:1, 6; Deuteronomy
32:8-9 in the Dead Sea Scrolls/Septuagint reading) than to Greco-Roman
polytheism. Michael Heiser's *The Unseen Realm* (evangelical, not LDS,
but widely read) makes the biblical divine-council case independently —
useful precisely because it's not an LDS apologetic source, which
strengthens its evidentiary value per the playbook's source-hierarchy
rule (non-LDS scholarship conceding the point carries more weight on
screen than an LDS source asserting it).

## Protestant response

Push on the same Psalm 82 material directly — Jesus himself cites Psalm
82:6 ("I said, ye are gods") in John 10:34-36 as a live argument, not a
dead metaphor. Ask a sola scriptura Protestant to explain why Jesus's own
argumentative use of that psalm should be read as purely rhetorical while
insisting the passage carries no ontological content. FAIR's article on
["Mormonism and the nature of God: Polytheism"](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/mormonism-and-the-nature-of-god-polytheism-2fe5/1)
collects this case and the Elohim-plural argument together.

## Exmo/atheist response

The strongest version of this objection from this audience isn't
philosophical, it's historical: plurality-of-Gods teaching (Orson Pratt's
especially) went through real doctrinal correction and internal
Church dispute in the 1860s (Brigham Young's own qualifications of
Pratt's speculative theology). That's worth conceding directly —
19th-century LDS theology on this point was actively worked out in real
time, not handed down as a finished system, and speculative excess by
individual leaders (Pratt) was later reined in by other leaders (Young).
This is a case where "the doctrine developed and got corrected" is itself
the honest and sufficient answer, rather than pretending the teaching
was always stable.
