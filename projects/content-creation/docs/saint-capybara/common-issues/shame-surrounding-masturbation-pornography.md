# Shame surrounding masturbation/pornography

## General response

Current and historical LDS teaching treats masturbation and pornography
use as violations of the Law of Chastity, historically addressed directly
in worthiness interviews (see `worthiness-interviews.md`) including with
youth. State the current teaching accurately (masturbation is addressed
less explicitly and less severely in current official materials than in
some past decades' teaching, which is worth noting as a real shift) and
avoid either understating or overstating current severity.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Most traditional Christian sexual ethics (Catholic moral theology,
conservative evangelical teaching) also treat masturbation and
pornography use as morally significant, generally on similar grounds
(lust, Matthew 5:28) — the LDS position isn't a theological outlier here.
The more specific critique concerns *pastoral method* (interview-based
disclosure to an untrained lay leader, particularly for youth) rather
than the underlying moral teaching itself — keep those two questions
separate.

## Exmo/atheist response

Engage the documented psychological/pastoral concern directly: mental-
health professionals (including LDS-affiliated therapists, notably work
associated with BYU's own counseling research) have raised concerns that
shame-heavy framing and confession-based accountability structures around
masturbation specifically can contribute to compulsive-behavior cycles,
anxiety, and, in some documented clinical accounts, conflation of normal
adolescent sexual development with serious moral failure. Note current
Church materials have moderated some of this framing in recent years
(For the Strength of Youth revisions have de-emphasized masturbation
specifically compared to earlier editions) — cite that shift as a real,
if incomplete, institutional response rather than presenting current
teaching as identical to past decades' more severe framing.
