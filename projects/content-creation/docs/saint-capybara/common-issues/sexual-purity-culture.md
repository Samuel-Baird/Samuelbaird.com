# Sexual purity culture

## General response

LDS teaching (the "Law of Chastity") restricts sexual activity to
heterosexual marriage and has historically used strong metaphorical
language (chewed gum, licked cupcakes, and similar object-lesson analogies
used in some past youth teaching materials) that critics argue conflated
personal worth with sexual history, particularly for young women. State
this specific pattern directly — it's well documented in Church-produced
youth materials from past decades, not an exaggerated claim.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Purity-culture criticism is a broader Christian phenomenon, not
LDS-specific — evangelical purity culture (purity rings, purity balls,
similar object-lesson teaching) has generated extensive internal
Christian critique and reckoning (e.g., Josh Harris's public
disavowal of his own influential purity-culture book, *I Kissed Dating
Goodbye*) well before LDS-specific criticism became prominent. Use this
to situate the LDS critique within a wider, already-acknowledged
Christian conversation rather than treating it as a uniquely LDS problem.

## Exmo/atheist response

Engage the specific, documented harm concern directly: worth-conflation
teaching methods (the "chewed gum"/"licked cupcake" analogies
specifically) have been criticized by LDS-affiliated therapists and
educators themselves, not only outside critics, for contributing to
shame and distorted self-worth, particularly around sexual assault
survivors (the analogies imply diminished worth regardless of consent).
Note that current official curriculum has moved away from these specific
analogies in more recent materials — cite that shift directly as
evidence the institution has recognized and partially corrected the
specific pedagogical problem, while noting the shift is recent and
uneven in local-leader practice.
