# Unfalsifiability of truth claims

## General response

The objection: core LDS truth claims (the Book of Mormon's divine
authorship, continuing revelation's authenticity) are structured so that
no conceivable evidence could count against them — any contrary evidence
gets absorbed (apostasy explains away inconvenient early Christian
history, per `great-apostasy-and-restoration-claims.md`'s
unfalsifiability concern; faithful feelings confirm regardless of
historical difficulty). Take this philosophy-of-science-style objection
(descending from Karl Popper's falsifiability criterion) at full
strength.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Most traditional theistic and Christian claims share this same structural
feature to some degree (the problem of evil's many theodicies function
similarly; core claims like the resurrection or scriptural inspiration
aren't straightforwardly falsifiable by ordinary empirical means either)
— name this as a shared feature of revealed religion and classical
theism generally, not an LDS-specific defect, while still taking the
underlying challenge seriously rather than only using it as a deflection.

## Exmo/atheist response

Give the objection its full weight and respond with a real methodological
commitment rather than a rhetorical dodge: the corrective isn't claiming
LDS truth claims *are* strictly falsifiable in Popper's technical sense
(few historical or theological claims are), but committing to specific,
genuine vulnerabilities the claims do have — concrete historical and
textual claims (Book of Mormon anachronisms, DNA evidence, Book of
Abraham papyri correspondence, documented prophetic teaching reversals)
that *could* in principle be decisively falsified by evidence, and
engaging those honestly rather than treating every difficulty as
absorbable. The playbook's certainty-scale discipline
(possible→plausible→supported→probable→demonstrated) is the practical
tool for keeping this honest on screen — claims that can't be
downgraded when evidence is weak aren't being held to any real standard.
