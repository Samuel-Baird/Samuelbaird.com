# God once being as humans are / divine progression

## General response

The Lorenzo Snow couplet is the standard reference point: "As man now is,
God once was; as God now is, man may be" —
[1894](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/1894-10-snow-remarks/#p25),
reaffirmed by Marion G. Romney as late as
[1964](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/1964-10-romney-my-beloved-brethren-and-sisters-seen-and/#p15)
("This teaching is peculiar to the Church"). Joseph Smith's own 1844
King Follett formulation: "God himself was once as we are now, and is an
exalted Man" —
[source](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/1844-04-smith-character-and-being-of-god/#p13).
Note the framing difference worth using on screen: the Church officially
treats the *second half* (man may become as God is) as core doctrine with
far more confidence and frequency than it treats the *first half*
(God's own prior progression) — useful for calibrating certainty
correctly rather than overclaiming either clause.

## Catholic response

This is the sharpest collision with classical theism's doctrine of divine
immutability and aseity (God as unchanging, uncaused cause) — engage that
directly rather than dismissing it. Point out the actual dispute is
whether immutability in the strong Thomistic sense is itself biblically
or philosophically required, or whether it's a Hellenistic philosophical
import layered onto the text (see `rejection-of-creation-ex-nihilo.md`
for the parallel argument about creatio ex nihilo). Blake Ostler's
*Exploring Mormon Thought* vol. 3 (*Of God and Gods*) is the most
sustained LDS-side philosophical treatment of this exact tension.

## Protestant response

Distinguish the doctrine from caricature immediately — "elevating God to
man's level" is not the claim; the claim is that humans can be brought
into the divine nature/glory Christ already possesses (2 Peter 1:4,
"partakers of the divine nature"). Ask what a Protestant does with 2
Peter 1:4 and Romans 8:17 ("joint-heirs with Christ") if not some real
participation in divine glory — then show that patristic theosis
theology answers that question in ways structurally similar to (if not
identical to) LDS exaltation. See `exaltation-and-humans-becoming-gods.md`
for the theosis sourcing in depth.

## Exmo/atheist response

Concede plainly: God's own past progression (as opposed to human
exaltation) is one of the least-emphasized and least-taught elements of
current LDS doctrine, is not canonized scripture, and rests heavily on a
single sermon (King Follett, 1844) whose textual reconstruction itself is
disputed (multiple partial contemporaneous notes, no single official
text) — this is a `disputed`/`reconstructed`-fidelity source, not a
`verbatim` one, and should be graded that way on screen. That's a fair
historical point and shouldn't be minimized. The stronger response is
distinguishing *doctrinal status* (highly speculative, not required for
salvation, rarely taught today) from *historical fact* (Joseph Smith did
teach a version of this) — conceding the fact, disputing that it
functions today the way critics imply.
