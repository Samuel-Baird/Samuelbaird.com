# Emotional elevation being mistaken for revelation

## General response

The objection: what's interpreted as the Holy Ghost's confirming witness
may simply be ordinary psychological/physiological responses to music,
social belonging, storytelling, and ritual — mechanisms well documented
in psychology and neuroscience independent of any specific religious
claim (e.g., "collective effervescence," a concept from sociologist
Émile Durkheim describing group-ritual emotional elevation across
cultures and traditions generally). Take the mechanism seriously rather
than dismissing the psychological account as automatically hostile to a
genuine spiritual referent.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Both traditions have long internal debates about distinguishing genuine
spiritual experience from psychologically-induced emotion (the Catholic
tradition's discernment-of-spirits literature, going back to Ignatius of
Loyola's *Spiritual Exercises*, exists precisely because this problem was
recognized centuries ago within Christianity itself) — this is a shared,
serious internal question for revealed religion generally, not a
gotcha unique to LDS testimony meetings.

## Exmo/atheist response

State the naturalistic explanation at full strength rather than
dismissing it: known psychological mechanisms plausibly explain at
least some, likely much, of what gets reported as spiritual confirmation
in testimony-meeting contexts (social conformity pressure, music's
well-documented emotional effects, narrative and ritual repetition). The
LDS response doesn't need to deny these mechanisms operate, because
their operation doesn't by itself prove *nothing else* is also happening,
which is a claim naturalistic explanation alone cannot establish either
(showing a natural mechanism is present is not the same as showing it is
the *sole* cause) — and the same fact that makes the felt-witness method
weaker as standalone evidence than many members are taught to treat it
is what places it in its proper role as corroborating rather than
founding evidence, consistent with
`circular-testimony-epistemology.md`'s framing.
