# Three degrees of glory / LDS afterlife

## General response

D&C 76 (the "Vision," 1832) is the founding revelation: celestial,
terrestrial, and telestial kingdoms, ordered by degree of glory —
Marion G. Romney,
[1975](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/1975-04-romney-easter-thoughts/#p75);
Dallin H. Oaks, ["Kingdoms of
Glory"](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/2023-10-oaks-kingdoms-of-glory/#p13)
(2023) — a full modern General Conference talk on the topic, useful as a
current-teaching anchor. Key distinguishing feature from most other
Christian afterlife models: near-universal salvation into *some* degree
of glory (only "sons of perdition" are excluded), which is a much less
binary heaven/hell model than most historic Christian eschatology.

## Catholic response

Structural parallel worth naming: Catholic theology also has a graded
afterlife (heaven, purgatory as a temporary purifying state, hell) rather
than a flat binary — engage that similarity before contrasting the
specifics (LDS degrees are eternal destinations reached after judgment,
not a temporary purgation).

## Protestant response

1 Corinthians 15:40-42 (Paul's own language of "celestial" and
"terrestrial" bodies, "one star differeth from another star in glory")
is the direct biblical anchor D&C 76 draws on — put that text in front of
a sola scriptura Protestant and ask why gradation-of-glory language in
the resurrection body shouldn't be read as gradation-of-glory in the
resurrected state generally, rather than only a metaphor for the physical
resurrection body.

## Exmo/atheist response

Less commonly attacked as false doctrine by this audience and more often
cited as evidence of theological improvisation (a system worked out fast,
in 1832, from a vision two men reported having simultaneously) — that's
a fair historical framing to concede (this doctrine, like the Nicene
Trinity centuries earlier, was formulated at a specific, dateable moment
in response to a real theological question, not handed down complete)
without treating rapid formulation as itself disqualifying.
