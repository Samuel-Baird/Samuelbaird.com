# Exaltation and humans becoming gods

## General response

D&C 132:20 ("then shall they be gods, because they have all power...")
is the canonical anchor. 19th-century sermons state it starkly — John
Taylor: humans "become the sons of God... and joint heirs" with Christ —
[1864](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/1864-11-15-taylor-spiritual-manifestations/#p1);
Daniel H. Wells: exaltation to "become like to himself, gods, even the
sons of God" —
[1872](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/1872-05-25-wells-the-plan-of-salvation/#p1).
Frame carefully on screen: this is participation in the Father's glory
*through* Christ and *by grace*, not independent self-deification — the
same qualifier the best patristic theosis sources use (see below).

## Catholic response

Don't let this collapse into "Mormons think they'll be independent
competing gods" — that's a straw man and Catholic theology has its own
robust doctrine of *theosis*/divinization (2 Peter 1:4), especially
strong in Eastern Catholic and Orthodox theology. High-level sources:

- Norman Russell's work on deification in the Greek patristic tradition
  (cited in BYU Studies 45-1, [p.
  90](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/45-1-dd2d/90))
- Stephen Finlan & Vladimir Kharlamov (eds.), *Theōsis: Deification in
  Christian Theology* — the standard modern academic anthology,
  referenced across multiple LDS and non-LDS treatments (e.g. Blake
  Ostler, [*Of God and
  Gods*, p. 179](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/3-of-god-and-gods-f0dd/179))
- David Paulsen, *Mormonism in Dialogue with Contemporary Christian
  Theologies*, ["deification in the Patristic
  Fathers"](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/paulsen-mormonism-in-dialogue-with-contemporary-acd1/543)

The real dispute with Catholic theosis is one of *degree and kind*
(participation in divine energies/nature vs. becoming ontologically
"a god" in the LDS sense) — name that precise disagreement instead of
implying there's no difference at all.

## Protestant response

Most productive move: show this isn't LDS invention by pointing to Pauline
and Petrine material read by serious non-LDS biblical scholars as
genuinely deification-flavored:

- Michael Gorman, *Inhabiting the Cruciform God: Kenosis, Justification,
  and Theosis in Paul's Narrative Soteriology*
- M. David Litwa, *We Are Being Transformed: Deification in Paul's
  Soteriology* (Litwa is a mainstream biblical scholar, not LDS or
  apologetic — strong source per the playbook's audience-matching rule)
- Grant Underwood, ["Justification, Theosis, and Grace in Early
  Christian, Lutheran, and Mormon
  Discourse"](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/2-spring-2009-af7f/212),
  *International Journal of Mormon Studies* 2 (2009) — directly compares
  Lutheran and LDS soteriology on this point, useful for a Lutheran-
  specific audience.

## Exmo/atheist response

The "humans becoming gods" framing is frequently used to caricature LDS
belief as megalomaniacal or self-worshiping. The honest, non-defensive
answer: yes, exaltation is a real and distinctive LDS doctrine, and no,
it isn't unique to Mormonism as a *category* of religious idea (theosis
traditions predate Joseph Smith by over a millennium) even though the
LDS *version* of it is distinctive in content. Keep the certainty-scale
discipline here too — "structurally similar to theosis" is `supported`;
"early Christians literally taught LDS exaltation doctrine" would be
overclaiming and should be avoided on screen.
