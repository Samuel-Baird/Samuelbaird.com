# Salvation requiring works, ordinances, and covenants

## General response

Distinguish two LDS concepts often conflated by critics: (1) general
salvation from death/sin, extended to essentially everyone through
Christ's atonement regardless of ordinance status (universal
resurrection, near-universal degree of glory per
`three-degrees-of-glory-lds-afterlife.md`), and (2) *exaltation*
specifically, which requires covenants and ordinances (baptism,
confirmation, temple endowment, sealing). Critics who say "LDS makes
salvation impossible without temple attendance" are usually equivocating
between these two senses — separate them explicitly on screen.

## Catholic response

Direct structural parallel to Catholic sacramental theology: grace
ordinarily works *through* instituted means (sacraments), not purely
apart from them — see the Catholic Catechism's sacramental economy.
Name this parallel rather than letting the objection imply LDS uniquely
"adds requirements" to grace.

## Protestant response

This is the same underlying dispute as
`rejection-of-justification-by-faith-alone.md` applied specifically to
ordinances — route the Galatians/James sourcing there. The additional
question worth raising here specifically: if baptism itself is required
(which most Protestant traditions, even sola fide ones, still practice
and treat as commanded, not optional), the "ordinances add nothing"
position is already inconsistent in most Protestant praxis, not just LDS
theology.

## Exmo/atheist response

The more concrete version of this objection concerns temple worthiness
requirements gating ordinance access for exaltation — see
`temple-recommend-interviews.md` and `worthiness-interviews.md` for the
"high-demand religion" framing and sourcing; that's where this objection's
real force lives for this audience, not in the abstract theology.
