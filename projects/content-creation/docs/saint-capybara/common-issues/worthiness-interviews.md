# Worthiness interviews

## General response

Regular one-on-one interviews with bishops/leaders assess compliance
with commandments (tithing, Word of Wisdom, sexual purity, etc.) as a
condition for callings, temple recommends, and youth ordinances. State
the specific practice accurately, including that youth interviews
historically could include direct questions about sexual conduct and
masturbation — a specific, documented practice, not an exaggerated
critical claim.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Compare directly to Catholic sacramental confession, which similarly
requires disclosure of sexual sin to a religious authority as a condition
of continued full participation (absolution, communion) — a real
structural parallel, though the specific practice of an adult
interviewing a minor alone about sexual matters (rather than a
confessional format with additional safeguards in most Catholic
practice) is a meaningfully different practice worth distinguishing
rather than treating the parallel as fully equivalent.

## Exmo/atheist response

Engage the specific, well-documented controversy directly: activist Sam
Young's "Protect LGBT Children"/worthiness-interview campaign (culminating
in a 2018 hunger strike and later excommunication) specifically targeted
one-on-one sexual-conduct questioning of minors by adult leaders without
a parent present, arguing it created abuse risk and psychological harm.
The Church subsequently updated guidance (2018) to recommend a parent or
another adult be present or nearby during youth interviews and to give
youth explicit ability to decline specific questions — cite this policy
change directly as evidence the underlying concern had real institutional
merit and was at least partially addressed, rather than treating the
original practice as beyond legitimate criticism.
