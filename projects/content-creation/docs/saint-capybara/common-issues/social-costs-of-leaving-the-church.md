# Social costs of leaving the Church

## General response

Leaving carries real social costs for many members — strained family
relationships, loss of community, changed marriages — this is a
documented, common experience and should be acknowledged directly rather
than minimized. Pew's 2011 National Survey of Mormons and Faith in Flux
data provide relevant context on religious switching generally: across
all religious switchers, only "mostly their own decision" (68%) drove
leaving a childhood religion, versus family/parental decision for a
notable minority (28%), and the most commonly cited reasons for leaving
any religion are gradual drift and unmet spiritual needs rather than one
dramatic trigger — [Pew, Faith in
Flux](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2009/04/27/faith-in-flux/).

## Catholic and Protestant response

Both traditions also impose real social costs on those who leave,
particularly in tight-knit congregational or family-centered communities
— this is not unique to LDS practice, though the degree of social
embeddedness (family sealing theology, ward-based social life,
missionary-era friendships) may be unusually high in LDS contexts
specifically. Acknowledge that LDS community structure plausibly does
raise the stakes higher than many other traditions, rather than only
citing that other traditions have some version of the same cost.

## Exmo/atheist response

Take this seriously as a lived-experience claim, not merely a doctrinal
dispute — this is one of the areas where empathetic, non-defensive
engagement matters more than argument. Acknowledge specifically:
strained or severed family relationships, changed marriages (including
divorce risk when spouses' faith diverges), loss of a built-in social and
support network, and genuine grief processes similar to those described
in exit-based religious literature generally (not unique to LDS
experience, but real and worth naming without minimizing). Route
family-specific dynamics to `family-pressure-surrounding-belief-and-
leaving.md`.
