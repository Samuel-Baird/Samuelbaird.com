# Social costs of leaving the Church

## General response

Leaving carries real social costs for many members — strained family
relationships, loss of community, changed marriages — a documented,
common experience; state it once, precisely, and in the same beat put
it in the context Pew's 2011 National Survey of Mormons and Faith in
Flux data give for religious switching generally: across
all religious switchers, only "mostly their own decision" (68%) drove
leaving a childhood religion, versus family/parental decision for a
notable minority (28%), and the most commonly cited reasons for leaving
any religion are gradual drift and unmet spiritual needs rather than one
dramatic trigger — [Pew, Faith in
Flux](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2009/04/27/faith-in-flux/).

## Catholic and Protestant response

Both traditions also impose real social costs on those who leave,
particularly in tight-knit congregational or family-centered communities
— this is not unique to LDS practice. Where the degree of social
embeddedness (family sealing theology, ward-based social life,
missionary-era friendships) is unusually high in LDS contexts
specifically, say so in the same sentence as the shared pattern: the
higher stakes LDS community structure plausibly carries follow from the
depth of that community, not from anything peculiar to leaving it, so
name them as part of the shared pattern rather than only citing that
other traditions have some version of the same cost.

## Exmo/atheist response

Take this seriously as a lived-experience claim, not merely a doctrinal
dispute — this is one of the areas where empathetic, non-defensive
engagement matters more than argument. Name the specifics once,
precisely, and in the same breath place them in the pattern exit-based
religious literature already documents: strained or severed family
relationships, changed marriages (including divorce risk when spouses'
faith diverges), loss of a built-in social and support network, and
genuine grief processes are the same ones described for leaving any
high-commitment religion (not unique to LDS experience), which is the
context to give them in rather than either minimizing them or letting
them stand as an LDS-specific charge. Route
family-specific dynamics to `family-pressure-surrounding-belief-and-
leaving.md`.
