# Book of Mormon race issues: "skin of blackness" and "white and delightsome"

## General response

The critical claim: the Book of Mormon teaches that God cursed a people
with dark skin (2 Nephi 5:21-23; Alma 3:6-19) and promised that
converted Lamanites would become "white and delightsome" (2 Nephi 30:6,
earlier editions; now "pure"). Do not deny that the text says these
things (see `race-and-the-priesthood-temple-restriction.md` and
`past-prophetic-explanations-for-the-racial-restriction.md` for how
leaders later read them). The response is about what the language
*means*: whether black and white skin here describe ancestry, or a
condition and standing.

**Hebraism point (user-supplied, 2026-10-02).** In biblical Hebrew,
"black/dark" and "white" applied to skin or faces are figures for a
*condition* (suffering, famine, disease, terror), not ethnicity. The
strongest parallels, all re-read in NRSVue (Bible Gateway) on 2026-10-02:

- **Lamentations 4:7-8** (best example; a clear before/after in one
  passage): "Her princes were purer than snow, whiter than milk; their
  bodies were more ruddy than coral, their form cut like sapphire. Now
  their visage is blacker than soot; they are not recognized in the
  streets. Their skin has shriveled on their bones; it has become as dry
  as wood." White to black, caused by the siege, not by descent.
- **Lamentations 5:10** (names the cause): "Our skin is black as an oven
  from the scorching heat of famine."
- **Job 30:30** (illness): "My skin turns black and falls from me, and my
  bones burn with heat."

Use these as a *parallel in idiom*, not as proof of what 2 Nephi 5:21
means: that is an inference, and LDS scholars disagree among themselves
on literal versus figurative skin. Say so rather than overstating.

**Sources the user supplied that should NOT be used as-is:**

- **Nahum 2:10 and Joel 2:6.** The KJV has "the faces of them all gather
  blackness" / "all faces shall gather blackness" (terror), but NRSVue
  renders both "all faces grow pale" (checked 2026-10-02). Citing them
  for a *darkening* idiom works only from the KJV and the underlying
  Hebrew is disputed; if used, say that, and quote the KJV not NRSVue.
- **Song of Songs 1:5-6.** Literal sun-tanning, attributed to the sun in
  the verse itself. A parallel for literal complexion, not for a
  figurative or status use; do not use it for the Hebraism argument.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Frame as an interpretive question both traditions already handle: Bible
language about color and bodily condition is idiom, and "curse of Ham"
readings are the cautionary case of reading such language as ethnicity
(see `race-and-the-priesthood-temple-restriction.md`). The Lamentations
and Job passages are in every Christian canon, so the parallel does not
depend on any LDS premise.

## Exmo/atheist response

The strongest counter to expect: the text itself ties the darkening to
"that they might not be enticing unto my people" (2 Nephi 5:21) and to
preventing mixing (2 Nephi 5:23; Alma 3), and leaders from Brigham Young
to Spencer W. Kimball (1947, 1960) read it as literal pigment. Concede
that reading existed and was taught. The Hebraism point answers *what the
idiom can mean*, not *what leaders said*. Do not claim it settles the
text; claim it shows the literal-race reading is not forced by the
language.
