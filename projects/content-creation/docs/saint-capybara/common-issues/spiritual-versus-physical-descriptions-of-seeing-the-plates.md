# Spiritual versus physical descriptions of seeing the plates

## General response

Some witness language (particularly around the Three Witnesses'
experience, which involved prayer and an angelic visitation) uses
phrasing like seeing "with the eyes of our understanding," which critics
read as describing a visionary/spiritual experience rather than
straightforward physical sight — distinct from the Eight Witnesses, whose
statements describe direct physical handling in an ordinary setting with
no angelic or visionary component claimed. Keep the two witness groups'
experiences distinct rather than treating "the witnesses" as one uniform
data point — this is the same distinction `three-and-eight-witnesses.md`
raises but developed further here.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Christian tradition already has a category for meaningful, non-physical
"seeing" that participants regard as fully real and evidentially
significant — Paul's Damascus road experience and various prophetic
visions in scripture involve exactly this kind of visionary-but-real
seeing. The relevant theological point: a vision being spiritually
mediated doesn't make it subjective or worthless as testimony within a
framework that already accepts genuine divine visionary communication —
which most Christian traditions do accept as a category, even while
debating specific instances.

## Exmo/atheist response

Take the strongest form of the skeptical argument seriously: if the
Three Witnesses' experience was visionary rather than straightforwardly
physical, that's meaningfully different evidence than "eleven people
handled solid metal plates," and conflating the two witness experiences
in casual apologetic summaries overstates the evidentiary case. The
honest, precise response: the Eight Witnesses' testimony is the stronger
physical-evidence claim and should be led with for that purpose; the
Three Witnesses' testimony is a strong claim of a shared, external,
angelically-mediated vision, which is a different (not necessarily
weaker) category of evidence, and should be presented as such rather than
folded into "they physically saw the plates too."
