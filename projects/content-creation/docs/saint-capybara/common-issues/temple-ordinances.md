# Temple ordinances

## General response

Endowment, sealing, and vicarious ordinances (see `baptism-for-the-
dead.md`) are the core temple ordinances, framed as instruction and
covenant-making necessary for exaltation (see `salvation-requiring-
works-ordinances-and-covenants.md`). For the specific history of the
endowment's development and its relationship to Freemasonry, route to
`freemasonry-and-the-temple-endowment.md`; for the ceremony's documented
changes over time, route to `historical-temple-ceremony-changes.md` —
this file is for the general "why does salvation require secret temple
rituals at all" framing.

## Catholic response

Direct structural parallel: Catholic sacramental theology also holds
that certain graces are ordinarily available only through specific,
authoritatively-administered rites (confirmation, holy orders, marriage)
performed in consecrated spaces — the "why does this need a special
building/rite" objection proves too much against Catholic sacramentalism
too. The live disagreement is again about which rites and whose
authority, not whether sacred/restricted ritual as a category is
legitimate.

## Protestant response

For traditions with minimal or no sacramental theology, the deeper
disagreement is whether *any* physical ordinance carries real spiritual
efficacy beyond symbolic remembrance — that's a bigger and older debate
(memorialist vs. sacramental views of the Lord's Supper, for instance)
that predates and extends well beyond LDS practice. Locate the objection
there rather than treating temple ordinances as a uniquely LDS oddity.

## Exmo/atheist response

The "secrecy" framing (rather than "sacredness") is usually the load-
bearing part of this objection for this audience — much of that overlaps
with `historical-temple-ceremony-changes.md`'s documented history of the
pre-1990 oath of secrecy. The Church's own shift in language from
"secret" to "sacred," and the removal of the literal secrecy oath in
1990, is worth citing directly as evidence the institution itself
recognized and responded to this exact criticism, rather than only
defending the original framing.
