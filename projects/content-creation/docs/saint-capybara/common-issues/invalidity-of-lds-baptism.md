# Invalidity of LDS baptism

## General response

LDS requires baptism by immersion, performed by one holding proper
priesthood authority, for the remission of sins — stated as a fixed
formula across a century of teaching: John Taylor
[1884](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/1884-05-taylor-manifestations-to-be-looked-for/#p3);
Joseph Fielding Smith,
["by immersion in water, the ordinance being performed by one having
authority"](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/1915-04-33-joseph-f-smith-jr-remarks-33/#p2)
(1915). The authority requirement (not just the mode) is the actual
point of dispute with other Christian traditions, not immersion itself.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Both traditions' rejection of LDS baptism (requiring convert
re-baptism) rests on denying LDS priesthood authority specifically —
this is not a separate argument, it's a direct application of
`rejection-of-apostolic-succession.md` and `lds-priesthood-authority.md`.
Answer it there: whether LDS baptism is valid is entirely downstream of
whether the restored priesthood claim is credited, so don't treat this as
requiring new sourcing — redirect to those files' arguments.

## Exmo/atheist response

This objection rarely appears from this audience on its own merits (they
typically don't accept *any* tradition's authority-based validity claims)
— it surfaces mainly when discussing interfaith relations or Catholic/
Protestant treatment of LDS converts. Use it as an opportunity to point
out the irony: multiple Christian traditions each deny each other's
baptisms are valid on authority grounds (most Protestants don't accept
that only Catholic/Orthodox priesthood is valid either) — LDS exclusivity
on this point is not structurally unusual within Christianity, just
differently grounded.
