# Changes in doctrine over time

## General response

This is the broadest umbrella file in the folder — nearly every other
file in this collection (Godhead formulations, temple ceremony, race and
priesthood, LGBTQ policy, women's roles, translation methods) documents
some specific instance of doctrinal or practical change. When an
objection is raised at this general level, ask which specific change is
actually driving the concern and route there — a generic "doctrine keeps
changing" framing is both too vague to answer well and, per the
playbook's audience-matching guidance, reads as weaker than addressing
the specific case.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Both traditions have their own doctrinal development histories (see
`lds-rejection-of-the-nicene-trinity.md`'s pre-Nicene material, and
Vatican II's substantial revisions to Catholic practice and some
teaching emphasis) — most Christian traditions hold some version of
"development of doctrine" as compatible with continuity of essential
truth (Newman's *An Essay on the Development of Christian Doctrine* is
the classic Catholic theological treatment of exactly this question).
Use that shared framework rather than assuming LDS change is
categorically different in kind from doctrinal development elsewhere.

## Exmo/atheist response

The honest distinction to draw for this audience: "development" implies
continuity and refinement of a stable core, while critics argue some LDS
changes (particularly the race-based priesthood restriction's theorized
justifications, and the 2015/2019 LGBTQ policy reversal) look more like
correction of serious past error than refinement of an already-sound
core. Engage that distinction directly rather than defaulting to
"development of doctrine" language for every case — some cases genuinely
fit the development model better than others, and conflating them weakens
the response's credibility with an audience already skeptical of
apologetic reframing.
