# Excommunications of prominent members

## General response

High-profile cases include the September Six (1993, several LDS
intellectuals disciplined for scholarship and public statements
perceived as challenging Church positions), Kate Kelly (2014, Ordain
Women founder — see `women-and-priesthood.md`), and various historians
and podcast/blog figures over subsequent decades. State the specific,
named cases rather than a vague "the Church punishes questioners"
framing — precision matters since the actual triggers (organized public
advocacy/opposition versus private belief or scholarship alone) vary
case by case.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Both traditions have their own history of disciplining or marginalizing
prominent theologians and scholars for positions church authorities
deemed incompatible with orthodoxy (various 20th-century Catholic
theologians silenced or investigated by the CDF; numerous Protestant
seminary professors dismissed for doctrinal positions) — name specific
parallel cases rather than a vague gesture, to keep the comparison
concrete and credible.

## Exmo/atheist response

Engage the pattern directly rather than defending each case individually:
critics argue a recurring feature across these cases is that organized,
public opposition or embarrassment to leadership (not merely holding
unorthodox private views or even publishing critical scholarship quietly)
is what most reliably triggers formal discipline — worth stating as the
apparent pattern the documented cases show, while noting individual case
details vary and not every disciplined person fits this pattern equally
well. This is a case where naming the pattern precisely, rather than
either over- or under-stating it, is more credible than a blanket claim
in either direction.
