# Joseph Smith as a false prophet

## General response

This is the umbrella claim beneath most of the specific historical
objections in this folder — `joseph-smith-s-failed-or-disputed-
prophecies.md`, `book-of-abraham-translation.md`, `kinderhook-plates.md`,
`joseph-smith-s-polygamy.md`, `seer-stone-hat-translation-method.md`,
`possible-19th-century-influences-on-the-book-of-mormon.md`, and
`differences-among-first-vision-accounts.md`. When an objection is posed
at this general level, identify which specific claim is actually driving
it and route there — a generic defense of Joseph Smith's character reads
weaker than engaging the concrete evidence directly, per the playbook's
audience-matching guidance.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Ask what standard of prophetic perfection is actually being applied.
Biblical prophets (Moses, Jonah, David, Peter) are shown with serious
moral failures and even doctrinal missteps without the text treating this
as disqualifying their prophetic office — 1 Peter's Peter denies Christ
three times and is later rebuked by Paul (Galatians 2:11-14) yet remains
recognized as an apostle in both traditions. If the standard is "any
serious moral failure disqualifies prophetic claims," that standard
would disqualify most biblical prophets too — name that inconsistency
directly.

## Exmo/atheist response

Don't answer with the "no true prophet is perfect" move alone — that can
read as a blanket excuse if not paired with actually engaging the
specific evidence. Route to the concrete sub-topics above and treat each
on its own evidentiary merits, using the certainty-scale discipline
(fact/disputed/plausible) rather than reflexively defending every
episode. Terryl Givens's *By the Hand of Mormon* and Richard Bushman's
*Rough Stone Rolling* are the standard model for how to hold "genuinely
remarkable religious founder" and "flawed, sometimes seriously so, human
being operating under real historical constraints" together without
collapsing into either hagiography or dismissal — that's the tone to aim
for here.
