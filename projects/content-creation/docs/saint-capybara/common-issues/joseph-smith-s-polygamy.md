# Joseph Smith's polygamy

## General response

Joseph Smith married an estimated 30-40 women (sources disagree on exact
count), beginning secretly in the mid-1830s and continuing through his
death in 1844, before the practice was publicly acknowledged in 1852 —
state this without minimizing. Brian Hales's multi-volume *Joseph
Smith's Polygamy* is the most exhaustive documentary treatment, from a
faithful LDS historian who does not shy from the difficult specifics.
Route detailed sub-questions to `teenage-plural-wives.md`,
`secrecy-surrounding-plural-marriage.md`,
`emma-smith-s-knowledge-of-plural-marriages.md`, and
`public-denials-of-polygamy.md`.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Engage the direct theological problem rather than deflecting to Old
Testament polygamy alone — the more relevant question for Christian
interlocutors is whether new, binding moral commands can come through
prophetic revelation at all (which most Christians already accept in
some form regarding Old Testament figures like Abraham, Jacob, David,
and Solomon, all polygamists under a covenant relationship with God), and
if so, what distinguishes a legitimate claim to such a command from an
illegitimate one. That's a harder and more honest question than simply
citing Old Testament precedent as if it settles the matter.

## Exmo/atheist response

Don't lean on Old Testament parallels as a full answer for this
audience — they'll correctly note that doesn't resolve the specific,
serious ethical questions this practice raises (deception of Emma and the
public, marriages to already-married women, marriages to very young
women — see the dedicated sub-files). Give the direct, unminimized
historical picture and let the sub-files carry the specific evidentiary
weight; a defensive, minimizing tone on this topic specifically reads as
the least credible response in the entire apologetics playbook and
should be avoided more carefully here than almost anywhere else.
