# Correlation and institutional control

## General response

"Correlation" refers to the mid-20th-century centralization of Church
curriculum, publications, and programs under unified oversight (largely
completed by the 1960s-70s), replacing more locally/auxiliary-varied
practice. Critics argue this produced a more uniform, less
intellectually diverse, more centrally-controlled institutional culture.
Matthew Bowman's *The Mormon People* covers this institutional history
directly and non-polemically.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Compare to the general historical pattern of religious institutions
professionalizing and centralizing administration in the 20th century
(most large Christian denominations underwent similar
bureaucratization) — the specific LDS correlation program has a
particular history and effect worth engaging on its own terms rather than
assuming centralization itself is uniquely concerning.

## Exmo/atheist response

Engage the specific "high-demand religion" framing this often connects
to directly — route to `high-demand-religion-control-criticisms.md` for
the sociological framework and sourcing. This file's specific angle:
correlation is credited (by scholars across the faithful/critical divide)
with increasing doctrinal consistency and institutional efficiency while
also reducing space for local variation, intellectual diversity among
Church-produced materials, and grassroots theological experimentation
that characterized earlier eras (compare 19th-century diversity of
leader teaching, e.g. `adam-god-teachings.md`, to today's much more
uniform correlated curriculum) — name both the efficiency gain and the
diversity cost rather than only one side.
