# Public denials of polygamy

## General response

Joseph Smith and other Church leaders issued explicit public denials that
plural marriage was being practiced, including statements in Church
publications (e.g. the Times and Seasons), while it was privately
practiced — a documented historical fact, not a disputed allegation.
FAIR's own history notes the leadership "realized that the introduction
of plural marriage would inevitably invite severe
criticism" ([source](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/the-church-of-jesus-christ-and-plural-marriage-today-7ca9/22))
as the stated reason for concealment and denial.

## Catholic and Protestant response

The direct theological question: can a prophet issue knowingly false
public statements for what he considers good institutional reasons
without that undermining trust in his other prophetic claims? This
deserves a real answer, not a dismissal — the honest LDS response
generally distinguishes between prudential/protective deception in a
specific fraught circumstance and doctrinal/revelatory integrity, while
acknowledging that distinction is itself uncomfortable and doesn't fully
dissolve the tension.

## Exmo/atheist response

Give this one directly and without minimizing — public denial of a
practice one is privately engaged in is a much harder case than mere
secrecy (`secrecy-surrounding-plural-marriage.md`), since it involves
affirmative false statements, not merely withholding information. Name
that distinction explicitly rather than treating "secrecy" and "denial"
as interchangeable, and don't offer a resolution that understates the
seriousness of deliberate false public statements — the more credible
response concedes this was a real ethical failure in how the practice
was handled publicly, separate from whatever judgment is reached about
the underlying practice or revelation claim itself.
