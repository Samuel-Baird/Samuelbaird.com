# LDS priesthood authority

## General response

Two priesthoods, two restoration events: Aaronic priesthood restored by
John the Baptist (May 1829, JS-H 1:68-72), Melchizedek priesthood
restored by Peter, James, and John (undated precisely, traditionally
placed shortly after). Modern teaching frames priesthood as the authority
to act in God's name, not merely an internal spiritual gift — see
`rejection-of-apostolic-succession.md` for the historical argument this
rests on and `development-of-priesthood-restoration-narratives.md` for
the textual-history complications in how and when this claim was first
recorded.

## Catholic response

The comparison Catholics will draw is to holy orders and the sacramental
theology of ordination (an indelible character conferred through
apostolic succession). Rather than dismissing that framework, note the
structural similarity: both traditions agree ordination confers real,
transferable authority that cannot simply be self-declared — the dispute
is which chain of transmission is valid, which is `rejection-of-
apostolic-succession.md`'s question, not a fresh one.

## Protestant response

Many Protestant traditions (especially low-church/congregationalist) have
a real ambiguity here: they affirm the "priesthood of all believers" (1
Peter 2:9) but still, in practice, restrict baptism/communion/ordination
to formally recognized ministers. Ask what grounds that restriction if
not some doctrine of transmitted authority — which reopens the same
question sola-fide/low-church Protestantism often hasn't fully answered.

## Exmo/atheist response

The historically interesting material here is textual, not
theological: contemporary 1829-30 documents (early Book of Mormon
printer's manuscript, early revelations, contemporary correspondence)
don't unambiguously corroborate the Aaronic/Melchizedek restoration
narrative in the specific, dated form it's told in later — this is
exactly `development-of-priesthood-restoration-narratives.md`'s topic and
should be routed there for the primary-source detail rather than answered
in generalities here.
