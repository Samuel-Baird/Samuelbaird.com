# Joseph Smith's Egyptian Alphabet and Grammar materials

## General response

The Kirtland-era "Egyptian Alphabet" documents and "Grammar and Alphabet
of the Egyptian Language" (produced by Joseph Smith with Cowdery, Phelps,
and Parrish, ca. 1835) attempt to correlate specific Egyptian characters
with English word/phrase meanings and portions of the Book of Abraham
text — now fully published and analyzed by the Joseph Smith Papers
Project. State plainly that these documents exist and are studied openly,
not hidden.

## Catholic and Protestant response

The theological stakes mirror `book-of-abraham-translation.md`: do these
documents show Joseph Smith attempting (and by Egyptological standards,
failing at) a genuine linguistic decipherment, or were they a separate,
secondary project (an attempt to reverse-engineer a grammar from
revealed text) rather than the actual source of the translation? This is
the live scholarly question, not a settled embarrassment either way.

## Exmo/atheist response

Give the direct historical/scholarly state of the question:

- Most LDS scholars now treat the Egyptian Alphabet documents as a
  "backwards translation" attempt — reconstructing a hypothetical
  Egyptian grammar to match the already-produced English Book of Abraham
  text, rather than the forward source of that text — [FAIR: "Most LDS
  scholars believe that the Kirtland Egyptian Papers are an example of
  backwards
  translation"](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/book-of-abraham-201-papyri-revelation-and-6ad4/9).
- Critics (and some LDS historians) argue the relationship runs the
  other direction — that Abraham 1:1-3's specific wording is dependent on
  and derived from the Egyptian Alphabet and Grammar document, which
  would make the grammar-document earlier and generative rather than
  reconstructive — see the Interpreter/BYU Studies debate cited in
  Givens & Hauglid, ["The Dependence of Abraham 1:1-3 on the Egyptian
  Alphabet and
  Grammar"](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/givens-and-hauglid-the-pearl-of-greatest-price-9afe/227).

**Certainty level: disputed**, and actively so among faithful LDS
scholars themselves (this is an internal LDS academic debate, not simply
faithful-vs-critical) — present it as a genuinely open historical
question about textual dependency and direction, which it currently is.
