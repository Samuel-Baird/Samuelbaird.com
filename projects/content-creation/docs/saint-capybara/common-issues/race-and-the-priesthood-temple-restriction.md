# Race and the priesthood/temple restriction

## General response

From the mid-1800s until June 1978, men of Black African descent were
barred from priesthood ordination and, with their families, from temple
ordinances. This is a fact the Church now describes openly and without
minimization in its own materials — the 2013 Gospel Topics Essay "Race
and the Priesthood" explicitly disavows the theories once used to
justify it. Lead with that disavowal, not with the historical theories
themselves.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Both traditions have their own serious historical entanglements with
race-based theology and practice (biblical "curse of Ham" justifications
for slavery were widespread across many Christian traditions well into
the 19th and 20th centuries; segregated congregations and denominational
splits over slavery and civil rights are documented history for most
major American denominations) — name this directly rather than treating
LDS history as uniquely compromised. The comparison isn't a deflection;
it's relevant context for how this kind of theological error has
functioned across American Christianity generally.

## Exmo/atheist response

Engage the full, direct history rather than only the doctrinal
resolution:

- Matthew Harris, *Second-Class Saints: Black Mormons and the Struggle
  for Racial Equality*, is the standard current academic history —
  candid, well-documented, and unsparing about the extent and duration of
  the restriction and the theological rationales offered for it over
  time.
- Route the specific justifications given by past leaders (curse of
  Cain/Ham, premortal "fence-sitting" in the war in heaven — see
  [V51N02](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/v51n02-051d/43) for a
  documented example of a leader articulating that specific theory to a
  Black member) to `past-prophetic-explanations-for-the-racial-
  restriction.md` for full treatment.
- State plainly: no fully satisfying historical or theological
  explanation exists for why the restriction began, persisted for over a
  century, or specifically ended in 1978 rather than earlier — the
  Church's own essay does not offer one; it disavows past explanations
  without offering a replacement causal account. That absence is itself
  the honest answer, not a gap to paper over.
