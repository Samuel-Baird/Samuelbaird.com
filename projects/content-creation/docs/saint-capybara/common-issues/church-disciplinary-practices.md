# Church disciplinary practices

## General response

Formal Church discipline (informal probation up through excommunication,
now called "membership councils" and "loss of membership" since 2019
handbook language changes) is conducted by local lay leaders (bishops,
stake presidents) rather than a centralized tribunal, typically for
serious sin or, less commonly, organized public apostasy/opposition to
Church leadership. Route specific high-profile cases to
`excommunications-of-prominent-members.md`.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Both traditions have formal discipline and excommunication processes of
their own (Catholic canon law's excommunication categories; many
Protestant congregations practice formal church discipline and
disfellowshipping per Matthew 18:15-17) — the existence of a discipline
process isn't itself unusual; the specific procedures, appeal rights, and
transparency of the LDS process are the more substantive points of
comparison worth engaging directly.

## Exmo/atheist response

Engage the specific procedural criticisms directly: councils are
conducted by local lay leaders without formal legal training, the accused
has limited procedural rights compared to a civil legal proceeding (no
formal right to cross-examine witnesses in the way a civil trial would
allow, for instance), and outcomes can vary significantly by
jurisdiction and leader discretion for similar underlying conduct. These
are fair process-design criticisms distinct from whether discipline as a
category is legitimate — engage them as such rather than only defending
the legitimacy of discipline in the abstract.
