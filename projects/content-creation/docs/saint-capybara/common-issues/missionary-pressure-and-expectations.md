# Missionary pressure and expectations

## General response

Full-time missionary service (historically ages 18-19+ for men, 19+ for
women, now with some flexibility) carries strong social and family
expectation, particularly for young men, with baptism/convert numbers
sometimes used (formally or informally) as a success metric that critics
argue creates pressure toward less-than-fully-informed convert
conversions. Pew's 2011 data shows a real gap in mission service rates by
convert status (31% of lifelong Mormons served missions vs. 16% of
converts) — [Pew, 2011 National Survey of
Mormons](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2012/01/12/mormons-in-america-executive-summary/)
— useful concrete data on scope, though it describes who serves rather
than directly measuring pressure itself.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Compare to other traditions' vocational and missionary expectations
(Catholic religious vocation discernment culture, Evangelical short and
long-term mission culture) — social and family expectation around
religious service exists broadly across traditions; the specific
intensity and near-universality of the expectation for young LDS men in
particular is a meaningful point of difference worth naming rather than
assuming full equivalence.

## Exmo/atheist response

Engage the specific, documented convert-quality concern directly: critics
argue numeric goals and social pressure on missionaries (documented in
various journalistic and academic accounts, and acknowledged informally
within mission culture itself) can incentivize baptizing under-informed
or under-prepared converts, contributing to high early-convert
inactivity/attrition rates. This is a fair, evidence-adjacent concern
about program design and incentive structure, distinct from questioning
whether missionary work itself is legitimate — engage it at that level of
specificity.
