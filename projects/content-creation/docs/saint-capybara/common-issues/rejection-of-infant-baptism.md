# Rejection of infant baptism

## General response

Moroni 8 (in the Book of Mormon) is the sharpest statement: infant
baptism is denounced as a "solemn mockery before God," since little
children are alive in Christ and incapable of sin. General Conference
teaching states the premise plainly: "sinless children have no need of
repentance [or baptism]" — Charles H. Hart,
[1928](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/1928-10-12-charles-h-hart-remarks-13/#p5).
Age of accountability is fixed at 8 (D&C 68:27).

## Catholic response

This is a direct collision with the Catholic doctrine of original sin as
inherited guilt requiring sacramental remission even in infants — engage
Augustine's actual argument rather than dismissing infant baptism as
obviously wrong. High-level sourcing: Augustine's doctrine developed
specifically against Pelagius, who denied inherited guilt — see
MacCulloch, *Christianity: The First Three Thousand Years*, on how "a
guilt inherited from Adam which they termed 'original sin'... likewise
seemed to Pelagius" indefensible
([source](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/macculloch-christianity-the-first-three-3802/336)).
Note for calibration: this doctrine was actively, formally contested
within the early church (the Pelagian controversy, resolved by councils
against Pelagius) — it wasn't simply the unanimous, unchallenged apostolic
position from the start. See `different-understanding-of-original-sin.md`
for the fuller doctrinal sourcing.

## Protestant response

Many Protestant traditions (Reformed, Lutheran, Anglican) also practice
infant baptism/christening on covenantal grounds distinct from Catholic
original-guilt theology — so don't treat "no infant baptism" as a purely
Catholic-vs-LDS fight. For baptistic/credobaptist Protestants who already
reject infant baptism on believer's-baptism grounds, LDS and Baptist
practice actually converge here (immersion, post-accountability,
professed faith) — a useful point of genuine common ground rather than
conflict, worth naming explicitly rather than skipping past.

## Exmo/atheist response

Rarely a live objection from this audience directly; typically only
surfaces as a side note in broader "is Mormonism really Christian"
discussions. If raised, the efficient move is naming the actual dispute
(inherited guilt theology) and redirecting there rather than treating
infant baptism itself as the substantive issue.
