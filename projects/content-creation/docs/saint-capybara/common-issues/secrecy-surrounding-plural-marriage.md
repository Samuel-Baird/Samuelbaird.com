# Secrecy surrounding plural marriage

## General response

Plural marriage was practiced secretly for roughly two decades (mid-1830s
to 1852) before public acknowledgment, including active public denials
(see `public-denials-of-polygamy.md`) even as it was privately practiced
by Church leadership. State this timeline plainly.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Engage the theological tension directly: does secrecy about a claimed
divine commandment undermine the claim, or is confidential, staged
implementation of a difficult new practice compatible with genuine
revelation (compare the gradual, non-immediately-public rollout of other
significant early-Christian internal practices/disputes, e.g. the
Jerusalem council's internal deliberation in Acts 15 before public
resolution)? The parallel is partial at best — the scale and duration of
LDS secrecy, plus the active public denials, go well beyond ordinary
internal deliberation — name that difference rather than overstating the
parallel.

## Exmo/atheist response

Give the direct account: Brian Hales's *Joseph Smith's Polygamy* and
Cheryl Bruno's *Secret Covenants: New Insights on Early Mormon Polygamy*
document the systematic secrecy in detail, including compartmentalized
knowledge even within Joseph Smith's own family (Hyrum Smith reportedly
was not informed for a time —
[Quinn, *The Mormon Hierarchy*, p. 70](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/quinn-the-mormon-hierarchy-origins-of-power-d9a7/70)).
The honest framing: this level of sustained secrecy, including
deception of close family members and the general Church membership, is
a serious and legitimate historical criticism that doesn't have a fully
satisfying apologetic resolution — the best available response
acknowledges the deception plainly and situates it within the broader,
also-troubling history of the practice's introduction, rather than
minimizing it as simple prudence.
