# Rejection of sola scriptura

## General response

LDS position: scripture is authoritative but not exhaustive or
self-interpreting apart from living prophetic authority and continuing
revelation (Articles of Faith 1:9, "we believe all that God has revealed,
all that He does now reveal, and... He will yet reveal many great and
important things"). Robert Boylan's *Not By Scripture Alone: A
Latter-day Saint Refutation of Sola Scriptura* is the most developed
book-length LDS treatment of this exact question.

## Catholic response

Common ground: Catholicism also formally rejects sola scriptura in favor
of scripture-plus-tradition-plus-magisterium. Use this to build a
shared premise against a Protestant interlocutor, then separate from
Catholicism on *which* extra-biblical authority is legitimate (living
apostles/prophets vs. an unbroken magisterium) — same underlying
authority question as `rejection-of-apostolic-succession.md`.

## Protestant response

Don't just list extra-biblical LDS practices — attack sola scriptura's
own internal coherence first, per the playbook's explicit guidance:
*where does Scripture itself teach that, after the apostles die, the
Bible alone becomes the sole infallible rule?* That claim is not
self-evidently derivable from any single text and is itself a
theological conclusion imported from outside scripture. Sources:

- Mark P. Shea and other convert-apologetic literature aside, the more
  citable academic material: Gerald Bray, *Biblical Interpretation: Past
  and Present*, notes that ["once a doctrine of sola Scriptura took
  root, the nature of..."](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/bray-biblical-interpretation-past-and-present-37b9/197)
  biblical interpretation itself had to be worked out by later
  interpretive communities — i.e., sola scriptura doesn't eliminate the
  need for an interpretive authority, it just relocates the argument
  about whose interpretation is authoritative.
- Michael Peppard, *How Catholics Encounter the Bible*, makes the
  parallel point from the Catholic side: sola scriptura "has not resolved
  the problems of interpreting scripture" [despite its
  benefits](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/peppard-how-catholics-encounter-the-bible-51cf/33)
  — a non-LDS scholarly source conceding the practical problem, useful
  per the source-hierarchy rule.

## Exmo/atheist response

For this audience, reframe from doctrine to epistemology: the real
question isn't "should there be a closed canon" but "how do you know
which claimed continuing revelation is genuine, given that unfettered
continuing revelation has no obvious stopping point or check." That's a
fair question and deserves a direct epistemic answer (prophetic checks
and balances — common consent, First Presidency/Quorum of the Twelve
unanimity requirements, canonization process) rather than a purely
scriptural rebuttal, since this audience typically isn't granting
scriptural authority as a starting premise anyway.
