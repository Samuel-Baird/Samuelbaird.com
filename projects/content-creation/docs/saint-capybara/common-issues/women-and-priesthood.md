# Women and priesthood

## General response

Priesthood ordination is restricted to men in current LDS practice;
women hold significant organizational leadership (Relief Society, Young
Women, Primary presidencies) without priesthood ordination itself. The
Ordain Women movement (founded 2013) advocated directly for change;
co-founder Kate Kelly's June 2014 excommunication for apostasy after
organized public advocacy is a major, widely documented flashpoint —
Matthew Bowman's *American Zion* and multiple academic treatments (Park,
*American Zion*, [p. 419](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/park-american-zion-a-new-history-of-mormonism-9e22/419))
cover this directly.

## Catholic response

Catholicism also restricts priestly ordination to men (citing Christ's
choice of only male apostles) and has its own internal debate literature
and occasional high-profile disciplinary actions against advocates for
change — a genuine structural parallel worth naming, while noting the
theological grounding differs (LDS priesthood theology ties male
ordination to a specific temple/family-order theology rather than
apostolic-succession arguments).

## Protestant response

Varies enormously by denomination — many mainline Protestant traditions
now ordain women, while others (many Baptist, complementarian evangelical
traditions) restrict pastoral leadership to men on scriptural grounds (1
Timothy 2:12 is the standard proof text on both sides of the wider
Christian debate). Locate the specific interlocutor's own tradition's
position before assuming either full parity or full restriction as the
baseline comparison.

## Exmo/atheist response

Give the direct, current picture: as of Pew's 2011 National Survey of
Mormons, only 11% of Mormons overall supported ordaining women, with
notable variation by activity level (45% among the least active versus
3% among the most active) — [Pew, 2011 National Survey of
Mormons](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2012/01/12/mormons-in-america-executive-summary/)
— useful for showing this isn't a fringe question even if it's a
minority position within current membership. Kate Kelly's
excommunication specifically for organized public advocacy (not private
belief) is the sharpest documented case connecting this issue to
`treatment-of-dissenters-and-critics.md` — route there for the
disciplinary-practice angle.
