# "Different Jesus" / different Christology

## General response

The claim usually bundles several distinct sub-claims (Jesus as a
literal, begotten, embodied Son of the Father rather than one being of
the Trinity; Jesus as "spirit brother" of Lucifer via premortal spirit
creation; a Christology tied to the LDS Godhead view) — separate them
before answering, since conflating them makes the response vaguer than
it needs to be. Gary J. Coleman, ["Mom, Are We
Christians?"](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/2007-04-coleman-mom-are-we-christians/#p4)
(2007) is the standard General Conference direct response to this exact
charge.

## Catholic response

Catholic Christology (Chalcedonian: one person, two natures, of one
substance with the Father) is the historical target of this objection.
Route this into `lds-rejection-of-the-nicene-trinity.md`'s "early belief
vs. later creedal form" argument rather than re-deriving it — the
Christological question and the Trinity question are the same
underlying dispute about how much later conciliar precision is required
for the label "Christian."

## Protestant response

Ask what minimal Christological content a sola scriptura Protestant
actually requires from the text itself (divine Sonship, virgin birth,
atoning death, bodily resurrection, exclusive mediatorship — 1 Timothy
2:5) versus what requires the added technical vocabulary of Chalcedon.
LDS affirms every one of the biblically-stated claims; the dispute is
almost entirely over post-biblical metaphysical vocabulary, which is the
same fault line as the Trinity objection.

## Exmo/atheist response

For this audience the "different Jesus" language is usually lifted
directly from evangelical anti-Mormon rhetoric (2 Corinthians 11:4, "if
he that cometh preacheth another Jesus") and repurposed as a
"Mormonism isn't even real Christianity" argument, without independent
force of its own. Name that lineage plainly, then redirect to whatever
underlying historical or epistemic objection (Joseph Smith's authority,
continuing revelation) is actually motivating the claim — see the
playbook's audience note that ex-Mormon objections are usually
trust-claims in disguise.
