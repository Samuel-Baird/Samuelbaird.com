# Transgender policies

## General response

Current Church handbook guidance restricts full participation (temple
ordinances, certain callings) for members who have undergone gender
transition, and frames gender as an eternal, binary characteristic
(consistent with "The Family: A Proclamation to the World," 1995, which
predates the current wave of trans-specific policy but is regularly cited
as its doctrinal basis). State the specific current policy accurately
rather than a vague characterization.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Most traditional Christian traditions (Catholic and most evangelical
Protestant bodies) hold similar theological positions on gender as
binary and biologically grounded — this isn't a doctrinally isolated LDS
position, though specific handbook-level restrictions and their pastoral
administration vary significantly by tradition and deserve their own
scrutiny rather than assuming uniformity.

## Exmo/atheist response

Acknowledge directly that this is an area of real, current tension and
documented harm concerns (elevated mental-health risk for LGBTQ and
especially transgender youth is well-established in general population
research, independent of any specific religious context, and is
frequently cited by critics as a reason institutional policy in this area
deserves particular scrutiny and care). Engage this as a live pastoral
and ethical concern rather than only a doctrinal dispute — this is a
topic where leading with data-driven empathy is more persuasive and more
honest than leading with doctrinal defense.
