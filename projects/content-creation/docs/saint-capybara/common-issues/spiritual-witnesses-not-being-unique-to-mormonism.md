# Spiritual witnesses not being unique to Mormonism

## General response

People across essentially every religious tradition (and some
non-religious contexts) report similarly strong, subjectively convincing
spiritual/emotional confirmation experiences for mutually incompatible
truth claims — Muslims report burning-in-the-bosom-type confirmation of
the Quran, Evangelicals of the inerrant Bible, and so on. State this
directly as a real and serious problem for using the feeling alone as a
sufficient truth-test, rather than disputing that it happens.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Both traditions face the identical structural problem for their own
inner-witness doctrines (see `circular-testimony-epistemology.md`'s
Calvin reference) — this is a shared epistemological challenge across
revealed religion generally, not an LDS-specific vulnerability, and
should be framed that way rather than conceded as unique to LDS
apologetics.

## Exmo/atheist response

Engage the full force of the objection: if a phenomenologically similar
experience confirms mutually exclusive claims across traditions, the
experience itself cannot be doing the evidential work of distinguishing
which claim is true — that's a valid point about the *limits* of
spiritual confirmation as a standalone method. The more defensible LDS
position (see `circular-testimony-epistemology.md`) narrows the claim:
spiritual confirmation may reliably indicate something real is
happening at the level of encountering the divine or transcendent
generally, without being a reliable sole method for adjudicating between
specific, mutually exclusive doctrinal claims — which then requires
combining it with historical, textual, and rational evidence (the
approach `research-protocol.md` and this whole reference folder assume)
rather than resting the whole case on the felt experience alone.
