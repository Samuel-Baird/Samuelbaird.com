# Joseph Smith's 1826 glass-looking hearing ("convicted felon" claim)

Recurring claim: a court in Bainbridge, NY (March 1826) convicted Joseph Smith
of "glass-looking" fraud, so he was a "convicted felon" and cannot be a
prophet. Seen in e.g. Be Bold Apologetics' "Proof Joseph Smith is convicted
felon" (Facebook reel, Sept 2026 — see `joseph-smith-convicted-felon-1826`).

## General response

Lead with the record, not the rhetoric: the 1826 fee bills are real (found
by Wesley Walters in 1971 — not 1871), and Joseph *was* brought before Justice
Albert Neely on a "disorderly person" charge. What is disputed is the
outcome; the original docket does not survive and the accounts split
(acquitted / discharged / condemned-but-released). "Convicted felon" is not
supported by any account: the charge was a misdemeanor-level "disorderly
person" complaint, and felony trials went to a different court.

## Catholic and Protestant response

The standard implied — no one with a court record can be God's spokesman — would
disqualify Moses, David, Peter and Paul (Paul was jailed repeatedly). Name that
inconsistency once, then engage the evidence. The "occult, satanic" label for
glass-looking is a theological judgment layered on a folk practice that was
widespread among churchgoing New Yorkers; Joseph himself said money-digging
"was never very profitable" and confessed "foolish errors" (JSH 1838).

## Exmo/atheist response

Concede what is true: Joseph did work as a treasure-seer for Josiah Stowell,
was hauled before a magistrate in 1826, and later Church sources (1946
Deseret News calling the record "spurious") over-denied. Then hold the line on
"felon": scholars on both sides (Vogel, Walters vs. Madsen, Bushman) argue about
whether it was a trial and how it ended, none about felony. The stronger
argument for the critic is the seer-stone pattern, not the conviction —
route to `seer-stone-hat-translation-method.md`.
