# Baptism for the dead

## General response

D&C 127-128 establishes the modern practice; 1 Corinthians 15:29 ("else
what shall they do which are baptized for the dead...") is the biblical
anchor. Doctrinal basis: God is just and will not condemn those who never
had a fair opportunity to hear and accept the gospel — vicarious baptism
extends the ordinance to them, offered but not forced (consistent with
`different-understanding-of-original-sin.md`'s rejection of inherited
guilt and any note of universalist optimism about salvation).

## Catholic and Protestant response

The strongest move here is showing this isn't an LDS invention read into
an obscure verse — a wide range of non-LDS biblical scholarship agrees
1 Corinthians 15:29 describes a real, then-current Corinthian practice of
proxy baptism, even while disputing whether it should be normative today:

- Bart Ehrman (a prominent non-LDS/skeptical NT scholar) affirms the
  practice: "baptized on their behalf... it is altogether unclear what
  the Corinthians [believed]" but the practice itself is not in serious
  dispute — [*Heaven and Hell: A History of the
  Afterlife*, p. 206](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/ehrman-heaven-and-hell-a-history-of-the-7519/206).
  Ehrman explicitly not being an LDS-sympathetic source makes this a
  strong citation per the playbook's source-hierarchy rule.
- Early patristic evidence of the same practice or its memory: Tertullian
  addresses it directly (cited in Hugh Nibley, [*Mormonism & Early
  Christianity*, p. 356](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/4-mormonism-and-early-christianity-8ca8/356));
  Epiphanius describes others' interpretation of it centuries later
  ([p. 360](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/4-mormonism-and-early-christianity-8ca8/360)).
- Jeffrey Trumbower, *Rescue for the Dead: The Posthumous Salvation of
  Non-Christians in Early Christianity* (Oxford, 2001) — a serious
  academic (non-LDS) monograph on the broader early Christian interest in
  posthumous salvation, useful context for why the Corinthian practice
  isn't as anomalous as it first sounds.

**Certainty level: supported, not demonstrated.** This shows the practice
existed and that its early meaning is genuinely debated in scholarship —
it does not show 1 Corinthians 15:29 endorses ongoing normative proxy
baptism the way D&C 128 does. Say exactly that on screen rather than
overclaiming Paul's approval.

## Exmo/atheist response

The practical/PR angle (celebrity/Holocaust-victim proxy baptisms
controversy) is usually the live version of this objection for this
audience, more than the theology. Address directly: the Church has
apologized for and tightened restrictions around unauthorized submissions
of Jewish Holocaust victims' names specifically, following real
controversy and negotiated agreements with Jewish organizations in the
1990s-2000s — concede that history plainly rather than only defending the
doctrine in the abstract.
