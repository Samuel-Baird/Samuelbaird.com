# Eternal marriage and sealing

## General response

Marriage sealed in the temple under "the new and everlasting covenant" is
taught to endure beyond death, contingent on continued faithfulness —
Ulisses Soares,
[2022](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/2022-10-soares-in-partnership-with-the-lord/#p10);
George F. Richards,
[1942](https://pulpit.restorationcommons.org/talks/1942-10-richards-the-covenants-of-the-priesthood/#p5).
D&C 132 is the founding revelation (also the same section containing the
plural-marriage material — see `joseph-smith-s-polygamy.md` for the
historical tangle there).

## Catholic and Protestant response

Both traditions typically hold that "in the resurrection they neither
marry, nor are given in marriage" (Matthew 22:30) settles the question
against eternal marriage. Engage the text directly rather than avoiding
it: the LDS reading takes Jesus's statement as addressing the *manner* of
becoming married (no new marriage covenants entered into after death,
answering the Sadducees' specific trap question about a woman
sequentially married to seven brothers) rather than categorically
dissolving marriages already sealed by proper authority. This is a live
exegetical question, not a settled one — the verse's Sadducee-trap
context (Matthew 22:23-28) is doing real interpretive work either way.

## Exmo/atheist response

The practical objection this audience raises most is less about the
doctrine's plausibility and more about lived consequences: sealing
policy history around divorce, cancellation of sealings, and — most
often — the emotional weight the doctrine places on mixed-faith marriages
and members who leave. Engage that directly rather than only defending
the doctrine philosophically; `family-pressure-surrounding-belief-and-
leaving.md` and `social-costs-of-leaving-the-church.md` have the relevant
sourcing for that half of the objection.
