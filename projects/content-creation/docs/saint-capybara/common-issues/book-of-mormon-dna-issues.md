# Book of Mormon DNA issues

## General response

The critical argument (most associated with Thomas Murphy's "Lamanite
Genesis, Genealogy, and Genetics," in *American Apocrypha*) is that
Native American populations show predominantly Asian-derived mtDNA
haplogroups, not Middle Eastern markers a literal Lamanite-descent
reading of the Book of Mormon would predict. State the argument in its
strongest form before responding, per the pre-publish two-question test.

This is primarily an evidentiary/scientific dispute rather than a
doctrinal one, so the substance below is shared across audiences — the
Catholic/Protestant/exmo-atheist sections note how the framing shifts
rather than repeating separate arguments.

## Catholic response

Rarely raised independently by Catholic interlocutors; when it is, it
usually rides alongside a broader "the Book of Mormon isn't historical"
argument rather than standing alone. Treat it as evidentiary (below)
rather than routing it through Catholic-specific theology.

## Protestant response

Same as Catholic — treat as evidentiary. Where it does surface from an
evangelical apologetics context, it's frequently paired with Book of
Mormon anachronisms (`book-of-mormon-anachronisms.md`) as one combined
"the text doesn't match reality" argument; separate the two claims, since
the DNA argument and the anachronism argument have different evidentiary
structures and different LDS responses.

## Exmo/atheist response

This is where the objection lands hardest and most specifically, often
from people with direct exposure to the CES Letter's DNA section. Give
the full, direct engagement:

Key LDS-side responses, drawn from serious (not merely rhetorical)
engagement:

- Population genetics: a small founding population (Book of Mormon
  peoples arriving into an already densely populated pre-existing Native
  American population, per the Limited Geography Model — see
  `book-of-mormon-archaeology.md`) could be genetically undetectable
  today through simple genetic drift and dilution, independent of any
  question of historicity — this is a real population-genetics
  phenomenon, not an ad hoc excuse. FAIR's summary: "the majority of
  Native Americans carry largely Asian DNA" is not disputed, but doesn't
  by itself rule out a small additional Near Eastern population —
  [source](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/identity-of-the-lamanites-in-the-book-of-mormon-65ec/11).
- FARMS Review (BYU) engaged Murphy's article directly and repeatedly
  across several issues (15.2, 16.2, 18.1, 22.1) — cite the strongest
  counter-argument specifically rather than gesturing at "FARMS
  responded": Volume 18.1's [DNA and the Book of Mormon (Stewart)
  essay](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/volume-18-1-7e04/187) argues the
  DNA evidence available "predates the discovery of the structure of the
  DNA molecule" in terms of what the text itself actually claims about
  Lamanite population scope, i.e. the text doesn't require the
  genetically-totalizing claim critics are testing against.

**Certainty level: disputed, not resolved.** State plainly on screen that
this remains a genuinely contested question between LDS and non-LDS
population geneticists — Murphy (an anthropologist) and Simon Southerton
(*Losing a Lost Tribe*, a former LDS bishop turned geneticist-critic) are
serious, credentialed voices on the critical side, not fringe figures,
and deserve to be named as such rather than dismissed.
