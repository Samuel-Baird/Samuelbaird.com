# Learning-science glossary

Terms the playbook relies on, defined in our own words. Refs point to where the source books treat the idea (printed pages; full context in `books/<slug>.md`). Slugs: Chance, Olson, Mazur, Gluck, Domjan, DeHouwer.

## Learning and performance
- **Learning** — a lasting change in what an organism can do or how it behaves because of experience; it is inferred from behavior, and it can exist without showing up right away. (Chance pp. 20–24; Domjan pp. 6–8; DeHouwer pp. 3–5)
- **Performance vs learning** — what is displayed now depends on incentives and context as well as on what was learned; test with delayed and transfer tasks. (Olson pp. 6, 321–322; Mazur pp. 193–194; Domjan pp. 6–7)
- **Latent learning** — knowledge picked up without visible payoff that appears once a reward makes it useful. (Olson pp. 321–322; Mazur pp. 193–194)
- **Functional vs cognitive explanation** — "what arrangement of the environment changes behavior" versus "what mental process mediates it"; keep the two separate so advice survives theory change. (DeHouwer pp. 27, 31)

## Practice and memory
- **Retrieval practice / testing effect** — recalling material strengthens memory more than re-reading it. (Gluck pp. 278–280; Olson pp. 75, 144)
- **Desirable difficulty** — a harder-feeling practice condition that yields better long-term retention. (Gluck pp. 278–280)
- **Spacing effect (distributed practice)** — spreading study over time beats massing it for lasting retention. (Olson pp. 133–134; Gluck p. 319; DeHouwer p. 49)
- **Interleaved / variable practice** — mixing task types or conditions; usually worse during practice, better on later tests. (Mazur pp. 301–304; Gluck pp. 319–320)
- **Overlearning** — practising past first success; supports retention. (Chance pp. 382–386; Mazur pp. 251–261)
- **Fluency** — correct responses per unit time; a stronger sign of mastery than a single accurate pass. (Chance pp. 41, 368–369, 387)
- **Knowledge of results** — information after a response about how well it went; needed for skill improvement. (Gluck pp. 317–318; Mazur pp. 294–296)
- **Transfer-appropriate processing** — memory is best when the way you studied matches the way you will be tested. (Gluck pp. 277–278)
- **Identical-elements transfer** — skill carries over to a new task in proportion to shared components. (Olson pp. 68–70; Gluck p. 328)
- **Interference** — similar memories compete, so forgetting often reflects retrieval failure rather than loss. (Mazur pp. 251–261; Domjan pp. 235–239; Gluck pp. 281–288)
- **Reconsolidation** — a retrieved memory becomes modifiable again, so recall can update or distort it. (Domjan pp. 240–241; Gluck p. 287)
- **Working memory** — small-capacity system for holding and working with information now; chunking stretches it. (Gluck pp. 351–356)
- **Chunking** — grouping items into larger meaningful units to fit working memory. (Gluck pp. 354–356)
- **Metacognitive illusion (overconfidence)** — feeling of knowing runs ahead of actual recall. (Gluck pp. 288–289)

## Conditioning and skill building
- **Reinforcement** — a consequence that makes a behavior more likely; best when immediate and contingent. (Chance pp. 129–150; Domjan pp. 101–119)
- **Contingency** — how reliably an outcome depends on a response or cue, beyond mere pairing in time. (Domjan pp. 70–72; DeHouwer p. 119)
- **Conditioned (secondary) reinforcer** — a cue that has gained reinforcing power by association, e.g. tokens bridging a delay. (Chance pp. 142–145; Domjan pp. 110–111)
- **Shaping (successive approximation)** — reinforcing steps ever closer to the target. (Chance pp. 173–180; Domjan pp. 107–109)
- **Chaining** — building a sequence step by step, forward or backward. (Chance pp. 173–180; Mazur pp. 121–123)
- **Prompt fading** — removing hints gradually so the cue alone controls the response. (Mazur pp. 122–124)
- **Errorless learning** — introduce a hard discrimination gradually so mistakes are rare. (Mazur pp. 234–235; Chance pp. 336–337; Gluck pp. 229–230)
- **Schedule of reinforcement** — the rule for which responses are rewarded; ratio vs interval shape pace and pauses. (Chance pp. 197–235; Domjan pp. 121–137)
- **Partial reinforcement effect** — behavior rewarded only sometimes resists extinction longer. (Chance pp. 200–221; Domjan pp. 167–170)
- **Extinction** — responses decline when the reward stops; the original learning remains and can return. (Domjan pp. 155–171; Mazur p. 9)
- **Renewal / spontaneous recovery** — extinguished responses return after a context change or a delay. (Domjan pp. 159–164; DeHouwer pp. 88, 106)
- **Blocking** — an already-predictive cue prevents learning about a redundant new cue; surprise drives learning. (Domjan pp. 69–70, 89–91; Gluck p. 131)
- **Prediction error** — the gap between expected and actual outcome; the engine of updating. (Domjan pp. 89–91, 240–241)
- **Differential reinforcement of alternatives / other behavior (DRA/DRO)** — reward a replacement or any non-problem behavior rather than punishing the problem. (Mazur pp. 151–152, 184–185; DeHouwer pp. 138–139)
- **Premack principle** — a preferred activity can reinforce a less preferred one. (Mazur pp. 201–207; Domjan pp. 144–152)
- **Response deprivation** — restricting access to an activity below its usual level makes performing the contingent activity rewarding. (Domjan pp. 144–152)
- **Learned helplessness** — after inescapable failure, learners stop trying even when success is possible; mixed solvable problems protect against it. (Mazur p. 172; Chance pp. 193–194; Gluck p. 409)
- **Learned industriousness** — rewarded effort on hard tasks carries over as persistence. (Chance pp. 193–194, 263)
- **Observational learning (modelling)** — learning by watching, without needing to be rewarded oneself. (Chance pp. 287–323; Olson pp. 350–351; Mazur pp. 284–285)
- **Self-efficacy** — belief in one's ability to succeed at a task; predicts effort and persistence. (Olson pp. 354–355, 369)

## Generalization and discrimination
- **Generalization** — responding to a new stimulus because it resembles a trained one. (Chance pp. 324–356; Gluck pp. 213–266)
- **Discrimination training** — teaching different responses to similar stimuli, e.g. with labelled contrasting examples. (Gluck pp. 226–227; Domjan pp. 219–223)
- **Concept learning** — responding alike to a class of varied examples and differently to others; checked with unseen instances. (Mazur pp. 231, 235–237; Chance pp. 329–332)
- **Perceptual learning** — exposure to many similar items sharpens fine distinctions without explicit feedback. (Domjan pp. 221–222)
- **Stimulus control** — a behavior reliably tied to a particular cue or setting, including study habits. (Mazur pp. 241–244)

## Study design terms used in this repo
- **Worked example → guided → independent** — fade support across problem sets. (Playbook rule 5)
- **Predict-then-check** — the learner commits to an answer or a score estimate before seeing the key. (Playbook rules 1, 8)
- **Review problem** — an earlier-unit item inserted into a later unit to space practice. (Playbook rule 3)
