FAQ

Questions and direct answers

Short, sourced answers about Samuel Baird, Latter-day Stats, Pulpit and the sacred sites map. Figures are dated; last updated November 17, 2026.

Who is Samuel Baird?

Samuel Baird builds public data tools and makes videos about religion, history and ideas. He created Latter-day Stats, a public hub for statistics, research and creator data on the Latter-day Saint community; he contributed to Pulpit, a critical edition of every General Conference discourse since 1830; he keeps a map of the sacred places he has visited; and he makes videos on the YouTube channel @ithinkthereforeisam. Everything is free, independent and ad-free. See About.

What is Latter-day Stats?

Latter-day Stats is a public, independent hub for statistics, research and creator data on the Latter-day Saint community. As of November 2026 it tracks 328 LDS YouTube channels with 16.6M combined subscribers, 73 academic studies and 58 survey-data graphics (Pew and other surveys). Its Data Explorer lets you chart public survey datasets, Studies is a curated research library, and Channels is a directory of LDS YouTube creators. It lives at latterdaystats.samuelbaird.com.

How many LDS YouTube channels are there?

There is no official count. The most complete public list is the directory at Latter-day Stats Channels, which tracked 328 LDS-related YouTube channels with 16.6M combined subscribers as of November 2026. The directory is refreshed regularly, so check it for the current figure.

What is Pulpit?

Pulpit is a critical digital edition of every General Conference discourse since 1830: 12,201 discourses as of November 2026, each traced to its best surviving witness and graded for fidelity (4,292 verbatim, 5,474 authoritative print, 2,225 contemporaneous report, 2 reconstructed, 208 fragmentary). It is a Restoration Commons project that Samuel Baird contributed to: pulpit.restorationcommons.org.

Which sacred sites has Samuel Baird visited?

Since January 2025 he has visited 35 sacred sites in 9 traditions (Catholic, Greek Orthodox, Anglican, Presbyterian, Lutheran, Episcopal, Seventh-day Adventist, Jehovah's Witnesses and The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints), most recently on November 8, 2026. Each site is counted once. Browse the full directory, the interactive map, the photo gallery or the traditions page.

How many LDS temples has Samuel Baird visited?

25 distinct Latter-day Saint temples as of November 2026: Bountiful Utah Temple, Brigham City Utah Temple, Cedar City Utah Temple, Deseret Peak Utah Temple, Draper Utah Temple, Idaho Falls Idaho Temple, Jordan River Utah Temple, Layton Utah Temple, Lindon Utah Temple, Logan Utah Temple, Manti Utah Temple, Monticello Utah Temple, Mount Timpanogos Utah Temple, Ogden Utah Temple, Oquirrh Mountain Utah Temple, Orem Utah Temple, Payson Utah Temple, Provo City Center Temple, Red Cliffs Utah Temple, Rexburg Idaho Temple, Saratoga Springs Utah Temple, St. George Utah Temple, Syracuse Utah Temple, Taylorsville Utah Temple, Vernal Utah Temple. Repeat visits to the same temple are counted once. Details are in the sacred sites directory.

Is the data on this site free to use?

Yes. The original datasets and photographs on this site (the sacred sites list, the reading log and the sacred sites photos) are released under CC BY 4.0: reuse them with attribution to Samuel Baird and a link to samuelbaird.com. Download them as CSV or JSON from Open data. Latter-day Stats publishes its own datasets, each with sources.

How should I cite this site?

For the sacred sites data: Baird, Samuel. "Sacred Sites Visited." samuelbaird.com, 2025–2026. https://samuelbaird.com/data. For Latter-day Stats, cite the specific study, dataset or graphic page and its listed source.
