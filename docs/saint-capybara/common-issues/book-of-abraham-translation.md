# Book of Abraham translation

## General response

Joseph Smith produced the Book of Abraham (1835-42) claiming translation
from Egyptian papyri he'd acquired. When fragments of the actual papyri
resurfaced in 1967, professional Egyptologists (LDS and non-LDS alike)
identified them as standard Egyptian funerary texts (the "Book of
Breathings" and similar) with no textual relationship to the Book of
Abraham's content — state this directly rather than around it. Michael
Rhodes (BYU Egyptologist), ["The Book of Abraham: Dealing with the
Critics"](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/the-book-of-abraham-dealing-with-the-critics-f453/1),
is a faithful LDS Egyptologist's direct engagement with this fact, worth
citing precisely because it comes from someone qualified to evaluate the
Egyptological claim and still affiliated faithfully.

## Catholic and Protestant response

The relevant theological question, once the Egyptological facts are
granted (see below), is whether "translation" must mean philological
transliteration or can mean revelation occasioned by/associated with a
physical document without being a literal rendering of its text — a
model some LDS scholars (Givens & Hauglid, *The Pearl of Greatest Price*)
now argue for directly, distinguishing "translation" in Joseph Smith's
usage from the modern linguistic sense.

## Exmo/atheist response

Give the fullest, most direct engagement — this is one of the strongest
evidentiary objections in the entire list and deserves full candor:

- FAIR's own summary states plainly: "when scholars examined the
  rediscovered Joseph Smith papyri" they identified them as the Egyptian
  Book of Breathings, unrelated in content to the Book of Abraham text
  — [source](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/book-of-abraham-201-papyri-revelation-and-6ad4/8).
  Jim Bennett (LDS apologist) states outright: "Egyptologists state that
  Joseph Smith's translation of the papyri and facsimiles [is]
  gibberish" by normal philological standards — [p.
  148](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/jim-bennett-a-ces-letter-reply-faithful-answers-549e/148)
  — cite an LDS apologist conceding this bluntly, since it's a stronger
  citation than a critic making the same claim.
- The main current LDS scholarly response is the "catalyst theory" —
  the papyri prompted genuine revelation about Abraham without being a
  literal source text (analogous to how a photograph might prompt
  memories not contained in the photograph itself) — developed at length
  in Givens & Hauglid and defended at Interpreter/BYU Studies.
- A minority LDS position (the "missing papyrus theory") holds the
  Book of Abraham came from a now-lost portion of the papyri collection
  rather than the surviving fragments — weaker evidentially since it's
  unfalsifiable in principle, and should be flagged as such rather than
  presented with equal confidence to the catalyst theory.

**Certainty level:** the mismatch between the surviving papyri's actual
content and the Book of Abraham text is `fact`, confirmed by
Egyptologists across the faithful/critical divide; which theory best
explains that mismatch is `disputed`, and no explanation currently
enjoys anything close to LDS scholarly consensus — say so directly rather
than presenting the catalyst theory as a settled answer.
