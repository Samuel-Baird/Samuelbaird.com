# Historical temple ceremony changes

## General response

The endowment ceremony has changed multiple times since Nauvoo (1927,
1990, 2005, 2019, among others), a documented fact that is exactly what
continuing revelation predicts: revelation applies to *how* eternal
ordinances are administered, not only to new doctrine — see
`continuing-prophets-and-revelation.md`. State the fact once and put
the beat's weight on that frame.

## Catholic response

Direct parallel: the Catholic Mass changed substantially at Vatican II
(1962-65) after centuries of a largely fixed Tridentine form — a useful
analogy for "ritual form can develop under legitimate authority without
undermining the ordinance's validity." Name this parallel explicitly
rather than letting change-over-time read as unique to LDS practice.

## Protestant response

Most Protestant traditions have also substantially revised worship forms,
liturgy, and practice repeatedly since the Reformation — the "unchanging
ancient ritual" standard implicit in this objection isn't one most
traditions actually meet themselves. Ask what standard is being applied
and whether the objector's own tradition meets it.

## Exmo/atheist response

Give the specific documented history plainly, since vagueness here reads
as evasive to an audience that already knows some of it:

- 1927: the Church "removed the oath in which" members made certain
  penalty-related covenants — John Turner, *The Mormon Jesus: A
  Biography*, [p. 217](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/turner-the-mormon-jesus-a-biography-9a9c/217).
- 1990: the Los Angeles Times reported the endowment ceremony was
  changed, removing the specific penalty gestures and the oath of
  secrecy requirement — [Mormonr, "Changes to the Temple
  Endowment"](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/changes-to-the-temple-endowment-376e/10);
  FAIR's own summary confirms: "before 1990, the endowment ceremony
  required members to take an oath of
  secrecy..." [that was later changed](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/changes-to-the-temple-endowment-0fb1/17)
  — an LDS-affiliated apologetic source conceding the fact plainly, a
  stronger citation than a critical source alleging it.
- 2005 and 2019 saw further changes (streamlined ceremony length,
  removal/adjustment of specific covenant language and gestures,
  including changes affecting women's covenant wording).

"The practice has changed" is simply true and documented by the Church's
own apologetic arm, which is why the critic's argument has to carry a
second premise to land: that legitimate ongoing revelation cannot change
ritual form. That premise is the substantive question (general response
and Catholic-parallel arguments above), and it's the one the beat should
argue, not whether change happened.
