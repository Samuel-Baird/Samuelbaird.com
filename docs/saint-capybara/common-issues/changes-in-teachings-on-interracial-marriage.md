# Changes in teachings on interracial marriage

## General response

Several past Church leaders taught against interracial marriage in
terms tied directly to the priesthood/temple restriction's now-disavowed
theological rationale (see `race-and-the-priesthood-temple-
restriction.md` and `past-prophetic-explanations-for-the-racial-
restriction.md`) — current Church materials and leaders do not repeat
these teachings and the Gospel Topics Essay's disavowal extends
logically to them, though the Church has not issued a specific,
freestanding statement retracting the interracial-marriage teachings by
name in the way it disavowed the priesthood-restriction theories
directly. Note that distinction precisely rather than assuming an
equally explicit retraction exists.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Both traditions also have documented histories of leaders teaching
against interracial marriage on scriptural or social grounds within
living memory (many U.S. denominations, North and South, before and
through the civil rights era) — situate the LDS case within that broader
pattern of American Christian history rather than treating it as
uniquely LDS.

## Exmo/atheist response

Give the specific documented history directly: Matthew Harris's *Second-
Class Saints* documents specific past leaders' statements opposing
interracial marriage without clear later correction of those specific
statements by name — [p.
330](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/harris-second-class-saints-black-mormons-and-5528/330)
notes one Church president's later remarks on the subject "giving no
indication if his views had changed or modified" from earlier, harsher
statements. State plainly: this is one of the areas where the Church's
public correction has been less explicit and less specific than the
priesthood-restriction disavowal itself, and that gap is a fair thing to
point out rather than assume already closed.
