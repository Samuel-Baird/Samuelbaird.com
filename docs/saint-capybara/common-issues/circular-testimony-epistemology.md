# Circular testimony epistemology

## General response

The objection: LDS testimony methodology (Moroni 10:4-5, "ask God... he
will manifest the truth of it unto you") is circular — praying to know if
the Book of Mormon is true, then treating any resulting feeling as
confirmation, builds the desired answer into the method itself. State the
structure of the objection accurately before responding.

## Catholic and Protestant response

Point out (without deflecting) that most Christian traditions rely on
some structurally similar move at bottom — the inner witness of the Holy
Spirit confirming scripture's truth (a doctrine explicit in Reformed
theology, John Calvin's *testimonium internum Spiritus Sancti*) is not
obviously less circular in form. This doesn't resolve the objection, it
relocates it — which is the honest and more useful response than
implying LDS testimony epistemology is uniquely vulnerable.

## Exmo/atheist response

Take the philosophical objection at full strength rather than only
citing the Calvin parallel (which a sophisticated interlocutor will
recognize as relocating, not resolving, the problem). The stronger
response distinguishes: a felt spiritual experience can be genuine
evidence *of something* (a real, if hard-to-characterize, psychological
or spiritual event occurred) while still being weak or non-diagnostic
evidence for the *specific propositional content* the method is used to
confirm (which of many competing truth claims a similar feeling has been
reported to confirm) — see
`spiritual-witnesses-not-being-unique-to-mormonism.md` and
`emotional-elevation-being-mistaken-for-revelation.md` for the specific
sourcing on that distinction. The method's limits as a stand-alone
epistemic test are precisely why the LDS position should be presented
as one where the felt witness functions as *corroborating*, rather than
founding, evidence alongside historical and rational argument — the
objection only lands against the stand-alone version, so don't defend
that version or claim the method is fully vindicated philosophically on
its own.
