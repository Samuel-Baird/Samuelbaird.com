# High-demand religion / control criticisms

## General response

The sociological framework (drawing on cult/high-control-group research,
e.g. Robert Cialdini's influence principles and Steven Hassan's BITE
model — Behavior, Information, Thought, Emotional control) is sometimes
applied to LDS practice by critics (tithing, worthiness interviews,
missionary service, temple covenants, correlation's uniformity) as
evidence of a "high-demand" or high-control structure. Take the framework
seriously as an actual analytical tool rather than dismissing it as
simply hostile rhetoric.

## Catholic and Protestant response

High-demand religious commitment (financial giving, behavioral
requirements, community enforcement of norms) exists on a spectrum across
many traditions — ask what specifically distinguishes "high commitment"
(a normal, often positive feature of serious religious practice) from
"high control" in a harmful sense (the actual target of the BITE-model
critique), and engage that distinction directly rather than treating any
demanding religious commitment as inherently suspect.

## Exmo/atheist response

Engage the framework's specific criteria directly rather than only
asserting LDS practice doesn't meet them: information control
(historically, incomplete disclosure of difficult history — see
`seer-stone-hat-translation-method.md` and `differences-among-first-
vision-accounts.md`'s disclosure-timeline points), behavior control
(worthiness interviews gating social/family participation), and thought-
stopping techniques (`doubt-your-doubts-discouragement-of-skepticism.md`)
each have some real, documented basis in LDS practice and history,
even if the overall pattern doesn't necessarily rise to the level
implied by "cult" framing specifically. The honest response distinguishes
between "this framework identifies some real concerning patterns worth
taking seriously" and "this proves LDS is a manipulative cult in the
fullest technical sense" — the first claim is defensible on the evidence;
the second overclaims and should be resisted even while taking the
underlying concerns seriously.
