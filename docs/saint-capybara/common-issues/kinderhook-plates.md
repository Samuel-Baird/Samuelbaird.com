# Kinderhook Plates

## General response

In 1843, a group of men (later confirmed to have fabricated the plates
as a hoax to trap Joseph Smith) presented him with forged brass plates.
Contemporary accounts (William Clayton's journal) record that Joseph
"translated a portion" of the characters. The plates were conclusively
proven a 19th-century forgery — Stanley Kimball's 1981 Ensign article,
["Kinderhook Plates Brought to Joseph Smith Appear to be a
Nineteenth-Century
Hoax"](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/kinderhook-plates-1c44/19), and
later metallurgical testing on the one surviving plate, confirmed this
directly — published by the Church's own magazine, not suppressed.

## Catholic and Protestant response

The theological stakes here concern prophetic discernment, not doctrine
— if Joseph Smith engaged with a forgery as though it might be genuine
ancient material, what does that say about his prophetic gift generally?
Engage the question head-on rather than deflecting to "it doesn't
matter."

## Exmo/atheist response

Give the most direct, currently-best-documented account:

- The Church's own Church History Topics essay states plainly that
  "Joseph evidently did not attempt" a real revelatory translation of
  the Kinderhook Plates, based on the surviving evidence — cited in
  Mormonr's ["Prophetic
  Fallibility"](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/prophetic-fallibility-61d8/34)
  essay.
- Recent LDS-affiliated scholarship (*Producing Ancient Scripture:
  Joseph Smith's Translation Projects*, an academic volume) argues the
  "translated a portion" comment likely reflects Joseph attempting a
  characters-analysis exercise similar to what he'd done with the Book of
  Abraham papyri, rather than declaring a formal, sustained revelatory
  translation project — this reframes the incident as reflecting Joseph's
  general (fallible) approach to unfamiliar ancient-looking characters,
  not evidence he claimed sustained prophetic authority over a hoax.
- D. Michael Quinn notes candidly that this "leaves unanswered what
  Joseph Smith meant when he said he
  'translated'" the plates — [source](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/quinn-the-mormon-hierarchy-origins-of-power-d9a7/672)
  — a fair statement that the incident isn't fully resolved even by
  faithful historians.

State plainly: this is one of the more genuinely difficult episodes for
claims of consistent prophetic discernment, and the best current LDS
response concedes real uncertainty about what happened rather than
explaining it away completely.
