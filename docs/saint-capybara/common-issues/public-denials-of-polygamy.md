# Public denials of polygamy

## General response

Joseph Smith and other Church leaders issued explicit public denials that
plural marriage was being practiced, including statements in Church
publications (e.g. the Times and Seasons), while it was privately
practiced — a documented historical fact, not a disputed allegation.
FAIR's own history notes the leadership "realized that the introduction
of plural marriage would inevitably invite severe
criticism" ([source](https://corpus.noahairmet.com/b/the-church-of-jesus-christ-and-plural-marriage-today-7ca9/22))
as the stated reason for concealment and denial.

## Catholic and Protestant response

The direct theological question: can a prophet issue knowingly false
public statements for what he considers good institutional reasons
without that undermining trust in his other prophetic claims? This
deserves a real answer, not a dismissal — the LDS response
distinguishes between prudential/protective deception in a specific
fraught circumstance and doctrinal/revelatory integrity, and the
objection only works if that distinction is collapsed: the tension the
critic points to is about how a practice was handled publicly under
threat, which is a different question from whether the revelatory
claims themselves are true, and the second does not follow from the
first.

## Exmo/atheist response

State the fact once, precisely — public denial of a practice one is
privately engaged in is a harder case than mere secrecy
(`secrecy-surrounding-plural-marriage.md`), since it involves
affirmative false statements, not merely withholding information — and
name that distinction explicitly rather than treating "secrecy" and
"denial" as interchangeable, because precision here is what lets the
rebuttal land: the false public statements are a question about how the
practice was handled publicly under the stated threat of severe
criticism (see the General response), and that question is separate
from whatever judgment is reached about the underlying practice or
revelation claim itself, so the critic's move from "they denied it" to
"therefore the revelation was false" does not follow.
