## The passage — structure only, no content borrowed

Horn's consistent method (documented across his published work and podcast):
present the opposing argument in its strongest form as a single, named premise
("an infallible text requires an infallible interpreter") before responding to it.
The distillation is stated as a clean logical claim the opponent would actually
recognize as their own position, not a caricature — only after that does the
rebuttal begin. This is a *structural* model, not a doctrinal one: the content of
Horn's Catholic apologetics is not something this channel imports or endorses.

## Take

- **Distill the objection into one named premise before answering it.** "The
  argument comes down to this: X" is a stronger opening than jumping straight to the
  rebuttal, because it proves the response is actually engaging the real claim
  (playbook step 1: state the objection in its strongest form).
- The distillation should be a sentence the *objector* would sign off on — this is
  the same strawman test `capy-critic-opponent` already runs.

## Do NOT take

- **Content only, never structure, is off-limits here** — reverse of the usual
  caution: this file is exclusively about the *shape* of the move (distill → rebut).
  Never cite Horn's specific Catholic-apologetics conclusions as a source for a
  Saint Capybara claim; his content is a different apologetics tradition arguing a
  different, sometimes opposed, position.

## Beat translation

Applied to a Catholic "no true authority without Rome" objection: *"Strip the
argument down and it's really just one claim: revelation needs a living interpreter,
or it's just ink on a page. That's worth taking seriously, so let's take it
seriously."*
