## The passage

A critic (Salmon) accused Nibley's "followers" of never having articulated a proper
methodology for comparing the Book of Mormon to ancient parallels. Hamblin's
response: he had personally published on exactly that methodology a decade earlier —
a four-phase model (analyze the text as ancient; analyze it as 19th-century; compare
explanatory power; judge the most plausible model) — and Nibley himself had stated
his own method's limits in print ("'Proving' the Book of Mormon is another matter").
Hamblin names the actual failure precisely: the critic simply asserts this
literature doesn't exist rather than engaging it, faulting Nibley for never reaching
a stage of argument (proof) Nibley had explicitly never claimed to attempt.

## Take

- **"You didn't engage the existing literature" is a stronger move than "you're
  wrong"** — it shows the critic's confidence outran their homework, using their own
  silence about existing scholarship as the evidence.
- **Distinguish what a source actually claims from what the critic accuses it of
  claiming** — Hamblin catches Salmon faulting Nibley for failing at a stage of
  argument Nibley was never attempting in the first place. This is playbook step 1
  (steelman/strawman test) applied in the other direction, catching the critic's
  strawman of the LDS position.

## Do NOT take

- Written for a specialist academic audience (FARMS Review) with dense footnoting
  and insider references (Jonathan Z. Smith's homology/analogy distinction) — the
  Short needs the one-sentence version of the move, not the scholarly apparatus.

## Beat translation

*"He says our side never explained its own method. We did — years before he wrote
this. He just didn't read it before deciding we hadn't."*
