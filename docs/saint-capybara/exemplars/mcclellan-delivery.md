## The passage — delivery note, not a quoted text

McClellan's short-form videos (widely reported as building a large following on a
"data over dogma" premise) are consistently described as dry, deadpan, and
adjective-free: the verdict on a claim comes first, the reasoning follows in plain
declarative sentences, and the delivery treats a strong claim exactly like a mild
one — no vocal or verbal signaling that a point is landing. No specific line is
quoted here; this file is a **delivery model**, not a content or argument source, and
should never be cited as a source for a claim.

## Take

- **Verdict-first structure**: say what the evidence shows before explaining why —
  the reverse of building up to a conclusion.
- **Flat delivery is what makes a dry line read as confident rather than performed.**
  A jab loses its edge the moment the narration tries to sound clever about it; say
  it plainly and move on.

- McClellan is a non-LDS academic commentator on separate topics (biblical studies,
  not LDS apologetics); his specific conclusions are never a source for this
