## The passage

Hales states the damaging fact without delay or softening — Helen Mar Kimball was
sealed to Joseph Smith at fourteen — and in the very next move reframes what kind of
event that was: her own later writing describes it as a sacrifice and a covenant, not
a physical marriage, and names her own father as the one who proposed it. The
turnabout: critics who quote Helen's poetry as evidence of trauma are, per Hales,
reading it against her own stated purpose — "as they use her words to support their
agendas, they may do violence to hers." The claim and the reframe arrive as one move,
not a concession followed by a separate rebuttal.

## Take

- **State the age and the sealing in the same sentence as the reframing fact** (her
  own account, her father's role) — never a bare "yes, she was fourteen" standing
  alone before the context arrives.
- **Turnabout on method, not on motive**: the critics aren't accused of lying, they're
  accused of reading a primary source against its own stated purpose — a factual,
  checkable claim about how a source is being used.

## Do NOT take

- The full presentation runs long-form and revisits primary documents at length
  (letters, Woman's Exponent articles) — compress to the one reframing fact a Short
  can actually carry; this is exactly the material `joseph-smith-american-muhammad`
  already used well (see that project's `n01`-`n03b` beats).
- **This is a sensitive-flagged topic** (`review-checklist.md` item 8) — the
  turnabout here stays fully clinical (about a reading method), never a jab at a
  living critic; do not sharpen this beyond what Hales himself does.
